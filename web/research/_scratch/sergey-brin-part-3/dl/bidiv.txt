 Court filings reveal details of Google cofounder Sergey Brin's divorce from his wife, attorney Nicole Shanahan

 Google cofounder Sergey Brin and his wife, the attorney and entrepreneur Nicole Shanahan , are getting divorced.
 On January 4 this year, Brin, a multi-billionaire entrepreneur and investor, quietly filed for divorce from his partner, citing "irreconcilable differences," according to court filings obtained by Insider.
 Brin, 48, and Shanahan, who is in her late thirties, have been linked since 2015 and quietly married on November 7, 2018. Shanahan gave birth to their daughter the same year. They separated just over three years later, according to filings with the Santa Clara Superior Court — on December 15, 2021.
 The divorce is a significant development in the life of one of the world's richest men, who retains a controlling stake in Google despite stepping back from the search engine giant's day-to-day operations in 2019. The court filings do not specify what assets Brin and Shanahan jointly own or how they will be divided — instead only confirming that some do exist, the details of which "are to be determined."
 "All issues as to the property rights or obligations of the parties will be determined by confidential binding arbitration in accordance with a written agreement between the parties," one of the documents reads.
 A Google spokesperson, Brin's family office, and Shanahan did not respond to requests for comment. 
 Brin started Google with Larry Page in 1998, and has grown immensely wealthy off its success. He has an estimated net worth of $88 billion, according to Forbes , making him the world's seventh-richest person.
 Shanahan, a California-based attorney, founded the Bia-Echo Foundation, a private fund focused on reproductive equality, criminal justice reform, and the preservation of the planet. In 2018, she and Brin cofounded a center at the medical research group Buck Institute focused on reproductive longevity, after her own struggles with infertility, she told Marie Claire in June 2020.
 She is Brin's second wife; he married Anne Wojcicki, cofounder and CEO of genetics firm 23andMe, in 2007. The two divorced in 2015, and have two kids together.

 The court filings emphasize Brin and Shanahan's desire for secrecy around the proceedings, and they have attempted to have much of the case placed under seal. "Petitioner is a co-founder of Google and one of the wealthiest and most famous technology entrepreneurs in the world. Because of the high-profile nature of their relationship, there is likely to be significant public interest in their dissolution and child custody issues," a filing from Brin's lawyers reads.
 "Of great concern is that such publicity puts their minor child at risk of danger, harassment, and even kidnapping, if the specifics of their day-to-day whereabouts are exposed to the public," the filing warned.
 The celebrity real estate blog Dirt reported earlier that the couple were separating. The court documents obtained by Insider and their contents have not previously been reported. 
 Brin is being represented in the divorce proceedings by the Bay Area law firm Hanson, Crawford, Crum Family Law Group, LLP, while Shanahan is has hired Los Angeles-based firms Spector Law Firm, APLC and Meyer, Olson, Lowy & Meyers, LLP.
 Brin has asked that he and Shanahan have joint custody of their child. He is not seeking spousal support from Shanahan, and is asking that she is not awarded it either.
 The couple have opted to pay for a privately-paid "temporary judge" to oversee their case — a more costly arrangement that can help expedite legal proceedings. The judge's fees are $950 per hour, plus an extra $300 per hour for their assistant.
