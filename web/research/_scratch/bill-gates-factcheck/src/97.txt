 Icos sale to Lilly approved by shareholders 
 Originally published January 25, 2007 at 12:00 am 
 Updated January 25, 2007 at 5:31 pm 
 Icos shareholders this morning approved a $34-per-share buyout offer from Eli Lilly, sealing the acquisition of the Seattle area's largest independent...
 Share story
 By 
 Seattle Times staff 
 Icos shareholders this morning approved a $34-per-share buyout offer from Eli Lilly, sealing the acquisition of the Seattle area's largest independent biotechnology company and the quick end of employment for most of its 700 workers. 
Stockholders endorsed the controversial sale in a lopsided vote, with 77 percent of the shares voted approving. Lilly raised its bid by 6 percent last month after widespread criticism of its initial $32 offer.
The Bothell-based company developed the world's No. 2 impotence drug, Cialis, and markets it in a 50-50 joint venture with Lilly.
Last week the joint venture said worldwide Cialis sales rose 30 percent in 2006 to $971 million. The venture's profit rose eightfold to $320.3 million. Sales for 2007 are forecast to be at least $1.1 billion.

"They beat our numbers in the U.S., they beat them in Europe, in Canada, Mexico and the rest of the world," said Chris Raymond, an analyst with Robert W. Baird in Chicago, after the Cialis results were announced. "It's clear why people think Lilly is getting a good deal," he said.
A leading proxy advisory firm, Institutional Shareholder Services, urged shareholders earlier this month to reject the improved offer, calling it "insufficient to match the increase in value" revealed in late December when Icos laid out its latest forecast for Cialis. But another proxy advisor, Glass Lewis, switched to favoring the deal after Lilly upped its offer by $2.
Hedge fund HealthCor Management, which held a 5.2 percent stake as of Jan. 8, had been vocal in opposing both the original bid and the revised $34 price. It wrote to the Icos board on Jan. 8 that the improved financial outlook revealed by Icos in mid-December meant "that Icos is selling for an even lower relative valuation than was initially proposed."
 Bloomberg News contributed to this report.
