 Gates-Backed Vaccine Helps Eliminate Polio in India for a Year
 SHARE THIS STORY:
 January 13, 2012 | Read Time: 1 minute
 As of Friday, India has gone one full year without a reported polio infection, a milestone hailed by backers of efforts to eradicate the disease worldwide, according to Bloomberg and Reuters .
 The anniversary marked the longest period without polio transmission in India, which had the world’s highest number of cases in 2009 and is one of four countries where the disease remains active. India will be considered officially polio-free if it goes two more years without a new case.
 The Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation has made wiping out polio its top global-health priority , investing $150-million a year in the eradication effort let by Rotary International, the Centers for Disease Control, the United Nations Children’s Fund, and the World Health Organization.
 Mr. Gates said India’s success shows polio can be tamed with “political will, quality immunization campaigns and an entire nation’s determination” and called for greater global support of the campaign. Activists say a gap of hundreds of millions of dollars increases the potential for the disease to cross back into India from Pakistan, which has seen a rise in new cases.
