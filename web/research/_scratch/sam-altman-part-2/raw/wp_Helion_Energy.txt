'''Helion Energy, Inc.''' is an American fusion research company located in [[Everett, Washington]].<ref name="Commercial">{{Cite web |last=Bergan |first=Brad |date=28 July 2021 |title=Helion Energy Says It Will Offer the World's First Commercial Fusion Power |url=https://interestingengineering.com/helion-energy-commercial-fusion-power |access-date=13 August 2021 |website=Interesting Engineering |archive-date=12 August 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210812183309/https://interestingengineering.com/helion-energy-commercial-fusion-power |url-status=live }}</ref> It is developing a [[magneto-inertial fusion]] technology to produce [[fusion power]] by combining [[deuterium]] with [[helium-3]] via [[aneutronic fusion]].<ref name="Helion">{{Cite web |title=Helion |url=https://www.helionenergy.com/ |access-date=2021-06-24 |website=Helion |language=en-US |archive-date=23 June 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210623060722/https://www.helionenergy.com/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last=Virgin |first=Bill |date=2018-04-16 |title=Redmond's Helion Energy Looks to Nuclear Fusion As the Next Big Thing in Power Generation |language=en |work=Seattle Business Magazine |url=https://seattlebusinessmag.com/technology/redmond%E2%80%99s-helion-energy-looks-nuclear-fusion-next-big-thing-power-generation |access-date=2021-06-24 |archive-date=16 January 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210116001456/https://seattlebusinessmag.com/technology/redmond%E2%80%99s-helion-energy-looks-nuclear-fusion-next-big-thing-power-generation }}</ref>

== Technology ==
Helion's approach uses a linear fusion system with pulsed magnetic compression and differs from the traditional design for fusion reactions, which relies on [[Tokamak|tokamaks]].<ref name=":5">{{Cite web |last=sarahrudge |date=2025-02-04 |title=Helion Energy is Building the World's First Fusion Power Plant |url=https://energy-oil-gas.com/news/helion-energy-is-building-the-worlds-first-fusion-power-plant/ |access-date=2025-07-16 |website=Energy, Oil & Gas magazine |language=en-GB}}</ref>

The system is intended to operate at 1&nbsp;Hz, injecting plasma, compressing it to fusion conditions, expanding it, and recovering the energy to produce electricity.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Svoboda |first=Elizabeth |date=2011-06-21 |title=Is Fusion Power Finally For Real? |url=https://www.popularmechanics.com/science/energy/next-generation/is-fusion-power-finally-for-real |access-date=2021-06-24 |website=[[Popular Mechanics]] |language=en-US |archive-date=31 December 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131231043118/http://www.popularmechanics.com/science/energy/next-generation/is-fusion-power-finally-for-real |url-status=live }}</ref> The pulsed-fusion system used is said to be theoretically capable of running 24/7 for electricity production. Due to its compact size, the systems would be able to replace current fossil fuel infrastructure without major needs for investment.<ref name=":2">{{Cite web |last=Emilio |first=Maurizio Di Paolo |date=2021-07-21 |title=EETimes - Helion Energy Achieves Key Fusion Milestone |url=https://www.eetimes.com/helion-energy-achieves-key-fusion-milestone/ |access-date=2022-04-30 |website=[[EE Times]] |archive-date=29 October 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221029184622/https://www.eetimes.com/helion-energy-achieves-key-fusion-milestone/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

=== Fuel ===
Helion uses a combination of [[deuterium]] and [[Helium-3]] (<sup>3</sup>He) as fuel. Deuterium and <sup>3</sup>He allows mostly [[aneutronic fusion]], releasing only 5% of its energy in the form of fast [[neutron]]s. Commercial <sup>3</sup>He is rare and expensive. Instead Helion plans to produce <sup>3</sup>He by [[Deuterium fusion|deuteron–deuteron (D–D)]] side reactions to the D–<sup>3</sup>He reactions. D–D fusion has an equal chance of producing a <sup>3</sup>He atom and of producing a [[tritium]] (<sup>3</sup>H) atom plus a proton. Tritium beta decays into more <sup>3</sup>He with a [[half-life]] of 12.32 years. Helion plans to capture the <sup>3</sup>He produced this way and reuse it as fuel. Helion has a patent on this process.<ref name="Google Patents 2015">{{Cite patent|title=Advanced D–<sup>3</sup>He fuel cycle for a pulsed fusion reactor|country=EP|number=3103119|pubdate=2021-03-24|assign=Helion Energy Inc.|inventor1-last=Slough|inventor1-first=John Thomas|inventor2-last=Kirtley|inventor2-first=David Edwin|inventor3-last=Pihl|inventor3-first=Christopher James}}</ref>

=== Development history ===
The company's fusion generator is based on the inductive plasmoid accelerator (IPA) experiments performed from 2005 through 2012.<ref name="Slough_JFE">{{Cite journal |last1=Votroubek |first1=G. |last2=Slough |first2=J. |last3=Andreason |first3=S. |last4=Pihl |first4=C. |date=June 2008 |title=Formation of a Stable Field Reversed Configuration through Merging |url=https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s10894-007-9103-4 |journal=Journal of Fusion Energy |language=en |volume=27 |issue=1–2 |pages=123–127 |doi=10.1007/s10894-007-9103-4 |bibcode=2008JFuE...27..123V |issn=0164-0313 |s2cid=122861398|url-access=subscription }}</ref><ref name="Slough_IAEA"/> These experiments used deuterium–deuterium fusion, which produced a 2.45-MeV neutron in half of the reactions. The IPA experiments claimed 300&nbsp;km/s velocities, deuterium neutron production, and 2-keV deuterium ion temperatures.<ref name="Slough_IAEA">{{Cite journal |last1=Slough |first1=John |last2=Votroubek |first2=George |last3=Pihl |first3=Chris |date=2011-05-01 |title=Creation of a high-temperature plasma through merging and compression of supersonic field reversed configuration plasmoids |url=https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/0029-5515/51/5/053008 |journal=[[Nuclear Fusion (journal)|Nuclear Fusion]] |volume=51 |issue=5 |article-number=053008 |bibcode=2011NucFu..51e3008S |doi=10.1088/0029-5515/51/5/053008 |issn=0029-5515 |s2cid=120579314 |url-access=subscription}}</ref> Helion and MSNW LLC published articles describing a [[Deuterium–tritium fusion|deuterium–tritium]] (D–T) implementation that is the easiest to achieve but generates 14 MeV neutrons. The Helion team published peer-reviewed research demonstrating D–D neutron production in 2011.<ref name="Slough_IAEA"/>

==== 6th prototype, 'Trenta' ====
In 2021, the firm announced that after a 16-month test cycle with more than 10,000 pulses, its sixth prototype, Trenta, had reached 100 million degrees C, the minimum temperature where a commercial generator could run.<ref name=":2" /> This made Helion the first private fusion company to reach these temperatures.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Tracey |date=2024-08-02 |title=Helion secures licence for Polaris fusion |url=https://www.neimagazine.com/news/helion-secures-licence-to-advance-polaris-fusion-facility/ |access-date=2025-08-01 |website=Nuclear Engineering International |language=en-US}}</ref> Magnetic compression fields exceeded 8&nbsp;T, ion temperatures surpassed 8&nbsp;keV, and electron temperatures exceeded 1&nbsp;keV.<ref>{{Cite news |last=<!-- Unstated: staff writer. --> |date=June 23, 2021 |title=Helion passes 100 million degrees Celsius |language=en-US |work=World Nuclear News |publisher=World Nuclear Association |url=https://world-nuclear-news.org/Articles/Helion-announces-fusion-milestones |access-date=2021-06-25 |archive-date=25 June 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210625090339/https://world-nuclear-news.org/Articles/Helion-announces-fusion-milestones |url-status=live }}</ref> The company further reported ion densities up to {{nowrap|3 × 10<sup>22</sup> ions/m<sup>3</sup>}} and confinement times of up to 0.5&nbsp;ms.<ref name="auto">{{Citation |title=Trenta 2020 Program Results | date=December 17, 2021 |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wHirlGXlJ38 |language=en |access-date=2022-10-30 |archive-date=30 October 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221030193121/https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wHirlGXlJ38 |url-status=live }}</ref>

==== 7th prototype, 'Polaris' ====

Helion's seventh-generation prototype, Polaris, started in 2011 and completed in 2024.<ref name=":4"/> The device is expected to increase the pulse rate from one pulse every 10 minutes to one pulse per second for short periods.<ref>{{Cite web |last=NRC |date=January 26, 2021 |title=Developing a Regulatory Framework for Fusion Energy Systems |url=https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML2102/ML21026A315.pdf |website=Nuclear Regulatory Commission |access-date=7 September 2021 |archive-date=23 June 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210623175039/https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML2102/ML21026A315.pdf |url-status=live}}</ref> This prototype is expected to be able to heat fusion plasma up to temperatures greater than 100 million degrees C.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2022-04-19 |title=Why did OpenAI invest in a nuclear fusion startup? |url=https://analyticsindiamag.com/why-would-openai-invest-in-a-nuclear-fusion-startup/ |access-date=2022-04-30 |website=Analytics India Magazine |language=en-US |archive-date=28 April 2022 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220428160430/https://analyticsindiamag.com/why-would-openai-invest-in-a-nuclear-fusion-startup/ |url-status=live}}</ref> Polaris is planned to be 25% larger than Trenta to ensure that ions do not damage the vessel walls.<ref name=":4">{{Cite web |date=December 2022 |title=A New Way to Achieve Nuclear Fusion: Helion |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_bDXXWQxK38 |access-date=2023-01-13 |website=Youtube.com |language=en |archive-date=13 January 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230113205507/https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_bDXXWQxK38 |url-status=live}}</ref> In August 2024, Helion received a Large Broad Scope license from [[Washington State Department of Health]]. This allowed Helion to possess and use necessary quantities of byproduct material to support the operation of Polaris.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Tracey |date=2024-08-02 |title=Helion secures licence for Polaris fusion |url=https://www.neimagazine.com/news/helion-secures-licence-to-advance-polaris-fusion-facility/ |access-date=2025-07-09 |website=Nuclear Engineering International |language=en-US}}</ref>

Helion completed construction of its seventh-generation prototype, Polaris, in late 2024 and began initial operations. While the company's original 2021 target was to demonstrate net electricity production in 2024, this milestone was not met. As of mid-2025, Helion reported that Polaris was operational and successfully forming the largest ''field reversed configuration'' (FRC) plasmas the company had created to date, with the goal of demonstrating net electricity production by the end of the year.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Behr |first1=Peter |last2=Marshall |first2=Christa |date=July 31, 2025 |title=Startup begins work on US fusion power plant. == Criticism ==
Retired [[Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory]] researcher Daniel Jassby mentioned Helion Energy in a letter included in the [[American Physical Society]] newsletter ''Physics & Society'' (April 2019) as being among fusion start-ups allegedly practicing "voodoo fusion" rather than legitimate science. He noted that the company is one of several that has continually claimed "power in 5 to 10 years, but almost all have apparently never produced a single D–D fusion reaction".<ref name="voodoo">{{Cite web |last=Jassby |first=Daniel L. |date=April 2019 |title=Voodoo Fusion Energy |url=https://engage.aps.org/fps/resources/newsletters/april-2019 |access-date=2020-09-09 |website=[[American Physical Society]] |language=en |archive-date=26 May 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210526153154/https://engage.aps.org/fps/resources/newsletters/april-2019 |url-status=live }}</ref>

The same 2018 [[MITRE]]/JASON report commissioned by [[ARPA-E]] evaluated Helion’s approach and identified the key technical challenge as “whether they can simultaneously achieve sufficiently high compression while maintaining plasma stability”.<ref name=":1" /><!-- Table 6 on p. 36 and Figure 11 on p. 34 -->
