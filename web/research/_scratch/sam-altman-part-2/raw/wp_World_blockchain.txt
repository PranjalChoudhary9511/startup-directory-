== History ==
===Founding and beta (2019–2023)===
World, originally called Worldcoin, is a project started by [[OpenAI]] chief executive [[Sam Altman]], Max Novendstern, and Alex Blania<ref name="2:6"/> and based in [[San Francisco]] and [[Munich]].<ref name="2:7">{{Cite web|url=https://www.statesman.com/story/business/technology/2025/05/15/sam-altman-openai-chatgpt-alex-blania-tools-for-humanity-world-orb-biometric-crypto/83602870007/|title=Get a peek into Sam Altman’s high-tech eye-scanning orbs coming to Austin|first=Karoline|last=Leonard|date=May 15, 2025|website=Austin American-Statesman}}</ref> Developed by the private company Tools for Humanity (TFH) in 2019, World outlined a three-part mission to create a global ID, a global currency, and an app enabling payment with World's own digital token as well as other digital assets and fiat currencies.<ref name="2:6">{{Cite web |last=Loizos |first=Connie |date=2023-03-08 |title=Worldcoin, co-founded by Sam Altman, is betting the next big thing in AI is proving you are human |url=https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/07/worldcoin-cofounded-by-sam-altman-is-betting-the-next-big-thing-in-ai-is-proving-you-are-human/ |access-date=2023-04-14 |website=TechCrunch |language=en-US |archive-date=November 18, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231118230623/https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/07/worldcoin-cofounded-by-sam-altman-is-betting-the-next-big-thing-in-ai-is-proving-you-are-human/ |url-status=live }}</ref> It began developing World ID,<ref name="3:14">{{Cite web|url=https://observer.com/2024/11/sam-altman-worldcoin-rebrand-world-ai/|title=Sam Altman Has a Side Project Called ‘World’ Aimed at Tackling a World Ruled by A.I.|first=Alexandra|last=Tremayne-Pengelly|date=November 11, 2024}}</ref> an open-source protocol<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://world.org/blog/world/world-id-faqs|title=World ID FAQs|website=world.org}}</ref> it describes as a digital passport for confirming someone is a unique human.<ref name="3:14"/> The project also began developing the cryptocurrency token WLD, or Worldcoin. It described WLD as part of a larger effort to drive a more unified and equitable global economy, with the token to be a Layer 2 [[Ethereum]]-based cryptocurrency that leverages the security of the Ethereum [[blockchain]] while having its own economy.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Sam Altman wants to give you crypto to scan your eyeballs. Can he get 8 billion people stare into a futuristic orb to prove they're not A.I. bots? |url=https://fortune.com/crypto/2023/03/21/sam-altman-orb-scan-eyeballs-ai-bot-worldcoin/ |access-date=2023-06-25 |website=Fortune Crypto |language=en |archive-date=March 21, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230321121213/https://fortune.com/crypto/2023/03/21/sam-altman-orb-scan-eyeballs-ai-bot-worldcoin/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Matney |first=Lucas |date=2021-10-21 |title=Sam Altman's Worldcoin wants to scan eyeballs in exchange for crypto |url=https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/21/sam-altmans-worldcoin-wants-to-scan-every-humans-eyeball-and-give-them-crypto-in-exchange/ |access-date=2023-06-25 |website=TechCrunch |language=en-US |archive-date=October 21, 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211021153327/https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/21/sam-altmans-worldcoin-wants-to-scan-every-humans-eyeball-and-give-them-crypto-in-exchange/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{Cite web | url=http://coinspotreview.com.au/ | title=Secure Bitcoin Exchange | access-date=July 23, 2023 | archive-date=January 29, 2021 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210129110908/http://coinspotreview.com.au/ | url-status=live }}</ref> In October 2021, the project raised an initial $25 million by investors such as [[Andreessen Horowitz]]. While in beta mode,<ref name=":10"/> by March 2022, World had verified 450,000 individuals in 24 countries using its Orb technology, using the data for training its algorithms. == Design ==
[[File:SlavaBlazerPhotography-31.jpg|thumb|Orb-shaped iris scanners on display]]
World hopes to provide a reliable way to authenticate humans online via World ID accounts,<ref name="0:13">{{Cite web |last=Pandolfo |first=Chris |date=May 16, 2023 |title=OpenAI CEO Sam Altman raises $100M for Worldcoin crypto project, which uses 'Orb' to scan your eye: report |url=https://www.foxbusiness.com/markets/openai-ceo-sam-altman-raises-100m-worldcoin-crypto-project-uses-orb-scan-eye |website=FOXBusiness |access-date=May 31, 2023 |archive-date=May 16, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230516201226/https://www.foxbusiness.com/markets/openai-ceo-sam-altman-raises-100m-worldcoin-crypto-project-uses-orb-scan-eye |url-status=live }}</ref> to counter bots and fake virtual identities facilitated by [[artificial intelligence]].<ref name="bbc-kenya"/> In 2025, World's website featured several products: its Worldcoin digital token, its World Chain blockchain, its World App portal, and its anonymous "proof of human" World ID.<ref name="0:5">{{Cite web|url=https://world.org/|title=World - The real human network.|website=world.org}}</ref>

To create a user's World ID, a device called the Orb takes images of a user's face and eyes<ref name="0:12"/> to confirm they are unique and human.<ref name="0:14">{{Cite web|url=https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/24/sam-altmans-world-now-wants-to-link-ai-agents-to-your-digital-identity/|title=Sam Altman's World now wants to link AI agents to your digital identity|first=Maxwell|last=Zeff|date=January 24, 2025}}</ref><ref name="0:13"/> According to Tools for Humanity, iris codes are used because irises have more randomness and complexity than fingerprints or faces, and are also less susceptible to change.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.businessinsider.com/worldcoin-sam-altman-iris-scanning-face-auth-tools-humanity-ubi-2024-8|title=Sam Altman's Worldcoin could support universal basic income by turning your iris into a code. In October 2025, the Philippines' [[National Privacy Commission]] (NPC) issued a cease and desist order against Tools for Humanity for multiple violations of the [[Data Privacy Act of 2012]] and related regulations. The order directed TFH to immediately stop all collection and processing of personal data in the Philippines, including the use of iris scans. The NPC found that TFH obtained invalid consent by offering monetary incentives for biometric registration, failed to provide sufficient transparency about how user data would be collected and processed, and gathered biometric information beyond what was necessary for to verify a user's "proof of humanity". It ruled that these posed risks of identity theft, fraud, and reputational harm, and noted similar suspensions of the World app in other countries. The NPC also rejected TFH's claims that it had implemented safeguards such as encryption and temporary data capture, stating that these measures failed to comply with Philippine data protection standards.<ref name="NPCCeaseAndDesist2025">{{Cite web |date=2025-10-08 |title=NPC issues Cease and Desist Order against Tools For Humanity |url=https://privacy.gov.ph/npc-issues-cease-and-desist-order-against-tools-for-humanity/ |access-date=2025-10-12 |website=[[National Privacy Commission]]}}</ref> World Philippines disputed the NPC's findings, calling it a "setback for responsible digital innovation". The company claimed that it had completed a year-long compliance process, including registration with the NPC and participation in the [[Department of Information and Communications Technology (Philippines)|Department of Information and Communications Technology]]'s Sandbox Program. It maintained that its system verifies "proof of humanity" without storing personal information and that biometric data are processed locally and deleted within seconds. World said it would file a motion for reconsideration while continuing to cooperate with regulators.<ref name="PhilStarWorldResponse2025">{{Cite news |last=Philippine Star |date=2025-10-11 |title=World Philippines disputes NPC decision, cites full compliance with privacy law |url=https://www.philstar.com/business/2025/10/11/2407854/world-philippines-disputes-npc-decision-cites-full-compliance-privacy-law |access-date=2025-10-12 |work=The Philippine Star}}</ref>
