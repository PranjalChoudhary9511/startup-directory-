'''Oklo Inc.''' is a publicly traded American [[nuclear power]] company based in Santa Clara, California. The company develops a [[small modular reactor|small modular nuclear reactor]], the [[Aurora nuclear reactor|Aurora Powerhouse]], and intends to sell both electricity and [[radioisotope]]s produced by their Aurora reactors.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Confino |first=Paolo |date=July 11, 2023 |title=Sam Altman just took a nuclear energy startup public for $500 million. Its CEO wants to provide 'energy at planetary scales for a billion-plus years' |url=https://fortune.com/2023/07/11/sam-altman-oklo-ipo-spac-openai-nuclear-microreactors-green-energy/ |work=Fortune |url-access=subscription}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Patel |first=Sonal |date=May 18, 2023 |title=Oklo's Next Two Nuclear Power Plants Planned for Southern Ohio |url=https://www.powermag.com/oklos-next-two-nuclear-power-plants-planned-for-southern-ohio/ |work=POWER}}</ref> The company's name is derived from [[Oklo]], a region in the country of [[Gabon]], Africa, where self-sustaining [[Natural nuclear fission reactor|nuclear fission reactions]] occurred approximately 1.7 billion years ago.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Judge |first=Peter |title=Sam Altman-backed nuclear reactor firm Oklo to go public via SPAC |url=https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/sam-altman-backed-nuclear-breeder-reactor-firm-oklo-to-go-public-via-spac/ |website=Data Centre Dynamics |date=19 July 2023 }}</ref>

Founded in 2013 by Jacob and Caroline DeWitte, Oklo Inc. focuses on fast-fission power plants. [[OpenAI]] co-founder [[Sam Altman]] served as Chairman of the Board of Directors, but stepped down in April 2025<ref>{{Cite web |last=Hamilton |first=Katherine |title=OpenAI CEO Sam Altman to Resign as Oklo Chairman |url=https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/openai-ceo-sam-altman-to-resign-as-oklo-chairman-10a53edc |access-date=2025-04-28 |website=WSJ |date=22 April 2025 |language=en-US |url-access=subscription}}</ref> to "avoid a conflict of interest ahead of talks between his company and the nuclear start-up on an energy supply agreement."<ref>{{Cite news |last1=Muir |first1=Martha |last2=Hammond |first2=George |date=2025-04-22 |title=Sam Altman steps down as chair of nuclear power supplier Oklo to avoid conflict of interest |url=https://www.ft.com/content/a511bae0-d19f-4ebd-9520-69d3f89d8556 |access-date=2025-04-28 |work=Financial Times |url-access=subscription}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Stevens |first=Pippa |date=2025-04-22 |title=Sam Altman steps down as Oklo board chair, freeing nuclear startup to work with more AI companies |url=https://www.cnbc.com/2025/04/22/sam-altman-steps-down-as-oklo-chair-freeing-nuclear-company-up-to-work-with-more-ai-companies.html |access-date=2025-04-28 |website=CNBC |language=en}}</ref>

== Aurora Powerhouse ==
{{Main|Aurora nuclear reactor}}

{{Promotional|section|date=June 2026}}
The Aurora Powerhouse is a 75&nbsp;MW<sub>e</sub> [[small modular reactor]] designed by Oklo. It is a compact [[sodium-cooled fast reactor]], modeled after [[Experimental Breeder Reactor II]] (EBR-II), and uses metallic [[HALEU]] fuel. The Aurora Powerhouse is intended for off-grid applications, including [[data center]]s, [[remote communitie]]s, industrial sites, and [[military base]]s.<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Patel |first=Sonal C. |date=2025-05-15 |title=Oklo Completes Key Siting Milestone for First Commercial Nuclear Fast Reactor at INL |url=https://www.powermag.com/oklo-completes-key-siting-milestone-for-first-commercial-nuclear-fast-reactor-at-inl/ |magazine=POWER |access-date=2026-04-30}}</ref> Oklo's business model is aimed at developing, building, and operating nuclear power plants to sell power to customers, with the Aurora Powerhouse being its flagship product.<ref>{{Cite web |author=Oklo Inc. |date=2024-02-02 |title=Oklo Investor Presentation |url=https://oklo.com/files/doc_presentation/2024/FEB/Oklo-Investor-Day-Deck-2024-Final-Draft-Print-Version.pdf |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240625212839/https://oklo.com/files/doc_presentation/2024/FEB/Oklo-Investor-Day-Deck-2024-Final-Draft-Print-Version.pdf |archive-date=2024-06-25}}</ref>

Around 20 fast reactors have been tested since the 1950s.<ref>{{Cite web |author= |date=2026-03-31 |title=Fast Neutron Reactors |url=https://world-nuclear.org/information-library/current-and-future-generation/fast-neutron-reactors |website=World Nuclear Association |access-date=2026-04-30}}</ref> As a liquid metal-cooled fast reactor, the Aurora Powerhouse will offer several advantages over [[light-water reactor]]s regarding its operation and safety. The Aurora reactor features strongly negative reactivity feedback coefficients that arise from the system's physics. These inherent feedback mechanisms will reduce reactor power in response to temperature excursions without requiring any operator intervention or active safety systems. This was demonstrated in the Shutdown Heat Removal Test series at the EBR-II,<ref>{{Cite report |url=https://www.osti.gov/biblio/5913652 |title=EBR-II inherent shutdown and heat removal tests: a survey of test results |last1=Planchon |first1=H. P. |last2=Singer |first2=R. M. |date=1985-01-01 |publisher=Argonne National Lab., IL (USA) |issue=CONF-850410-6 |language=English |last3=Mohr |first3=D. |last4=Feldman |first4=E. E. |last5=Chang |first5=L. K. |last6=Betten |first6=P. R. |osti=5913652 }}</ref> a sodium fast reactor operated between 1964 and 1994 that inspired much of the design of the Aurora powerhouse.

The Aurora Powerhouse was initially designed as a 1.5&nbsp;MW<sub>e</sub> [[heat pipe]]-cooled microreactor. Oklo's application for a combined construction and operating license for this version of the Aurora powerhouse was initially denied by the [[Nuclear Regulatory Commission]] (NRC) on January 6, 2022.<ref>{{Cite web| title=NRC Denies Oklo Combined License Application for Lack of Information; Company May Reapply in the Future | url=https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/news/2022/22-002.pdf | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220106180922/https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/news/2022/22-002.pdf | archive-date=2022-01-06}}</ref> The NRC cited a lack of information provided by Oklo during the application process and that Oklo could re-submit in the future. Oklo has remained engaged with the NRC since that decision through pre-application activities and readiness work intended to support a future combined license application. In 2025, Oklo reported completing the NRC's pre-application readiness assessment for Phase 1 of its Aurora combined license process. Oklo began pre-construction of its first Aurora powerhouse at [[Idaho National Laboratory]] in 2025 and aims to begin commercial operations of the plant by 2028.<ref>{{cite news |title=DOE signs off on Oklo fuel fabrication facility design |url=https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/doe-signs-off-on-oklo-fuel-fabrication-facility-design |access-date=13 November 2024 |work=World Nuclear News |date=16 October 2024 |language=en}}</ref> Oklo has an around 15 GW order book through an agreements with Meta and a Master Power Agreement with Switch and has signed [[letters of intent]] with [[Diamondback Energy]] and Wyoming Hyperscale to provide electricity for Diamondback's [[Permian Basin (North America)|Permian Basin]] operations and Wyoming Hyperscale's data center campus over 20-year periods.<ref>{{Cite web | last1=Wade | first1=Will | last2=Ferman | first2=Mitchell | title=Shale Drillers Weigh Using Small Nuclear Reactors for Permian Oil Patch (FANG) - Bloomberg | work=Bloomberg | date=April 2024 | url=https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-04-01/us-shale-drillers-seek-to-power-oil-patch-with-small-nukes | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240402011726/https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-04-01/us-shale-drillers-seek-to-power-oil-patch-with-small-nukes | access-date=2025-05-16 | archive-date=2024-04-02}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |title=Altman-Backed Oklo Sees Rising Nuke Demand From Data Centers |url=https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-05-24/altman-backed-oklo-sees-rising-nuke-demand-from-data-centers |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20240525110052/https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-05-24/altman-backed-oklo-sees-rising-nuke-demand-from-data-centers |archive-date=2024-05-25 |access-date=2025-05-16 |work=Bloomberg.com |language=en |url-status=live }}</ref> 

In May 2025, Oklo was among several advanced nuclear developers picked for DOE's Reactor Pilot Program as part of a federal initiative to power AI data centers with nuclear energy through fast-track deployment at [[United States Department of Energy|U.S. Department of Energy]] sites, including Idaho National Laboratory.<ref>{{Cite news |date=2025-05-13 |title=US nuclear builders tight for time in race to power AI |url=https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/us-nuclear-builders-tight-time-race-power-ai-2025-05-13/ |access-date=2025-05-13 |work=Reuters}}</ref> A number of SMR construction site projects have received NRC approval, including several for Oklo Inc.<ref>{{cite web |last1=Davis |first1=Anthony |title=Oklo Breaks Ground on Aurora-INL Advanced Nuclear Powerhouse |url=https://highways.today/2025/11/12/oklo-nuclear-powerhouse/ |website=highways.today |publisher=Highways Today |access-date=15 November 2025}}</ref> Oklo is currently working under Department of Energy oversight in INL as part of the Reactor Pilot Program and will demonstrate viability under DOE before working with the NRC to take the Aurora-INL to commercial operations, expected in 2028. <ref>{{cite web |title=Trump Signs Executive Orders on Nuclear Energy to Speed Up Deployment of Nuclear Power Plants |url=https://www.instituteforenergyresearch.org/nuclear/trump-signs-executive-orders-on-nuclear-energy-to-speed-up-deployment-of-nuclear-power-plants/ |website=instituteforenergyresearch.org |publisher=IER |access-date=11 November 2025}}</ref>

Since 2023, design documents, including the Principal Design Criteria for the Aurora Powerhouse (ML25220A124), have been accepted for review and remain under active evaluation.<ref>{{cite web |title=Principal Design Criteria for the Aurora Powerhouse |url=https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML2522/ML25220A128.pdf |website=nrc.gov |publisher=NRC |access-date=11 November 2025}}</ref> Oklo has signed a DOE Other Transaction Agreement and the DOE Idaho Operations Office has approved the Nuclear Safety Design Agreement (NSDA) for Oklo's fast-fission power plant at INL. In 2026, Oklo has stated it expects Aurora-INL to start operations in 2028.<ref name="Power2026">
{{cite magazine |last=Patel |first=Sonal C. |date=2026-06-18 |title=In a First for Advanced Nuclear: Siemens Energy Turbine Package Advances for Oklo's Aurora-INL |magazine=POWER |url=https://www.powermag.com/in-a-first-for-advanced-nuclear-siemens-energy-turbine-package-advances-for-oklos-aurora-inl/ |access-date=2026-08-08}}</ref>

== Fuel recycling ==
Oklo intends to recycle the [[spent nuclear fuel]] produced by its Aurora reactors.<ref>{{cite web |author= |date=2024-10-24 |title=DOE approves conceptual safety design report for Oklo fuel fabrication facility |website=Nuclear Newswire |url=https://www.ans.org/news/article-6479/doe-approves-conceptual-safety-design-report-for-oklo-fuel-fabrication-facility/ |access-date=2026-04-30}}</ref> In 2025, it announced that it would build a first-of-its-kind fuel recycling facility in [[Oak Ridge, Tennessee]].<ref>{{cite magazine |last=Patel |first=Sonal C. |date=2025-09-23 |title=Oklo Breaks Ground on INL Nuclear Fast Reactor Project, Launches Private Fuel Recycling Facility |url=https://www.powermag.com/oklo-breaks-ground-on-inl-nuclear-fast-reactor-project-launches-private-fuel-recycling-facility/ |magazine=POWER |access-date=2026-04-30}}</ref> On 26 May, 2026, Oklo was selected by the [[United States Department of Energy|Department of Energy]] (DoE) for advanced talks to join the Surplus Plutonium Utilization Program.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Martin |first=Patrick |date=2026-05-26 |title=Nuclear Energy Stock Breaks Out After DoE Nod - Schaeffer's Investment Research |url=https://www.schaeffersresearch.com/content/news/2026/05/26/nuclear-energy-stock-breaks-out-after-doe-nod |access-date=2026-05-27 |website=Schaeffers Investment Research |language=en}}</ref> The program converts designated surplus [[plutonium]] material into fuel for advanced reactors, and Oklo is pairing it with a partnership with [[European Union|European]] developer [[newcleo]] that could bring up to $2 billion in project capital, subject to definitive agreements.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Duprey |first=Rich |date=2026-05-26 |title=Oklo Moves One Step Closer to Commercialization |url=https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/05/26/oklo-moves-one-step-closer-to-commercialization/ |access-date=2026-05-27 |website=24/7 Wall St. |language=en-US}}</ref>

== Atomic Alchemy ==
Oklo also intends to produce [[radioisotope]]s through its [[nuclear reprocessing|nuclear fuel recycling process]]. These radioisotopes have a wide range of applications, including medical [[diagnostic imaging]] and [[nuclear medicine|cancer treatment]]; industrial uses like [[non-destructive testing]] and [[process control]]; and energy applications including [[radioisotope thermoelectric generators]], [[nuclear battery|nuclear batteries]] and [[nuclear fusion|fusion research]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=How Radioactive Isotopes are Used in Medicine {{!}} Britannica |url=https://www.britannica.com/story/how-radioactive-isotopes-are-used-in-medicine |access-date=2025-05-16 |website=www.britannica.com}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |title=Nondestructive Evaluation Techniques : Radiography |url=https://www.nde-ed.org/NDETechniques/Radiography/radiationsources.xhtml |access-date=2025-05-16 |website=www.nde-ed.org}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web | title=5 Radioactive Products We Use Every Day {{!}} Department of Energy | url=https://www.energy.gov/ne/articles/5-radioactive-products-we-use-every-day | access-date=2025-05-16 | website=www.energy.gov}}</ref> In May 2024, the company announced a partnership with Idaho-based radioisotopes company Atomic Alchemy.<ref>{{Cite web |author= |date=2024-05-16 |title=Oklo to collaborate with Atomic Alchemy on isotope production |website=Nuclear Newswire |url=https://www.ans.org/news/article-6041/oklo-to-collaborate-with-atomic-alchemy-on-isotope-production/ |access-date=2025-05-16}}</ref><ref name="POWERAtomicAlchemy">{{cite magazine |last=Patel |first=Sonal C. |date=2024-11-14 |title=Oklo's $25M Move into Radioisotope Production to Expand Advanced Nuclear Revenue Stream |url=https://www.powermag.com/oklos-25m-move-into-radioisotope-production-to-expand-advanced-nuclear-revenue-stream/ |magazine=POWER |access-date=2026-04-30}}</ref> Later in 2024, Oklo announced it would acquire Atomic Alchemy, and completed the purchase in March 2025.<ref>{{cite web |author= |date=2025-03-05 |title=Oklo Closes Acquisition of Radioisotope Producer Atomic Alchemy |website=Oklo Inc. |url=https://oklo.com/newsroom/news-details/2025/Oklo-Closes-Acquisition-of-Radioisotope-Producer-Atomic-Alchemy/default.aspx |access-date=2026-04-30}}</ref>

On September 12, 2025, Atomic Alchemy submitted an application to construct a radioisotope production facility to the NRC. The facility, Meitner-1, would house four isotope production reactors and be located at Idaho National Laboratory.<ref>{{cite web |author=Atomic Alchemy |date=2025-09-12 |title=Part I – General Information |url=https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML2525/ML25255A202.pdf |website=Nuclear Regulatory Commission |access-date=2026-04-30}}</ref> However, in December 2025, the company withdrew its construction permit application, following Atomic Alchemy's selection by the Department of Energy's Reactor Pilot Program.<ref>{{cite web |author= |date=2026-01-09 |title=DOE backs Oklo isotope facility |url=https://www.neimagazine.com/news/doe-backs-oklo-isotope-facility/ |website=Nuclear Engineering International |access-date=2026-04-30}}</ref>

As part of the program, Oklo is constructing a [[Swimming pool reactor|pool-type]] isotope production reactor, the Groves Isotope Test Reactor, near [[Lockhart, Texas]]. Groves is a pilot-scale project built as a test platform for future production-scale reactors.<ref>{{cite web |author= |date=2026-03-19 |title=Oklo provides updates on DOE, NRC approvals |website=Nuclear Newswire |url=https://www.ans.org/news/2026-03-19/article-7855/oklo-provides-updates-on-doe-nrc-approvals/ |access-date=2026-06-16}}</ref> The Groves reactor achieved criticality on August 5th, 2026, having been constructed within less than a year.<ref>{{cite web |last=Maciborski |first=Walt |date=2026-06-12 |title=The New Atomic Age: America's Nuclear Reboot Begins in Texas |website=CBS Austin |url=https://cbsaustin.com/news/local/the-new-atomic-age-americas-nuclear-reboot-begins-in-texas |access-date=2026-06-16}}</ref><ref>{{cite magazine |last=Patel |first=Sonal C. |date=2026-08-06 |title=Oklo's Groves Becomes Fifth DOE Pilot Reactor to Reach Criticality, First on Private Land |magazine=POWER |url=https://www.powermag.com/oklos-groves-becomes-fifth-doe-pilot-reactor-to-reach-criticality-first-on-private-land/ |access-date=2026-08-23}}</ref>

== Funding and investment ==
[[File:CES 2026 - Jacob DeWitte, JoeBen Bevirt, JP Nauseef 01.jpg|thumb|[[Jacob DeWitte]], [[JoeBen Bevirt]], J.P. Nauseef at [[Consumer Electronics Show|CES]] 2026]]

The company has received [[venture capital]] from various investors, including Hydrazine Capital, founded by Sam Altman with [[Peter Thiel]] as its sole limited partner; Facebook co-founder [[Dustin Moskovitz]]; [[Ron Conway]] of [[SV Angel]]; Kevin Efrusy of [[Accel Partners]]; and [[Tim Draper]] of Draper Associates.<ref>{{Cite journal|last=LASSITER|first=JOSEPH B.|year=2017|title=UPower Technologies Inc. Case # 9-816-054|journal=Harvard Business School}}</ref> In July 2023, it was announced that the company planned to go public via a [[Special-purpose acquisition company|special purpose acquisition company]] (SPAC) at a value of $850 million.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Nuclear power Spac: Sam Altman, AI king, seeks to split atoms |url=https://www.ft.com/content/dfa90dec-3c94-49fd-abf4-539e8e21e2d7 |access-date=2024-05-11 |website=www.ft.com}}</ref> On May 10, 2024, Oklo merged with AltC Acquisition Corp, a SPAC founded and led by Sam Altman, receiving $306 millions in gross proceeds.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |last1=Sigalos |first1=Hayden |last2=Field |first2=MacKenzie |date=2024-05-10 |title=Sam Altman's nuclear energy company Oklo plunges 54% in NYSE debut |url=https://www.cnbc.com/2024/05/10/sam-altman-takes-nuclear-startup-oklo-public-to-power-ai-ambitions.html |access-date=2024-05-10 |website=CNBC |language=en}}</ref>
