'''Loopt, Inc.''' was an American company based in [[Mountain View, California]], which provided a service for smartphone users to [[location-based service|share their location]] selectively with other people. The company entered partnerships with major U.S. mobile phone carriers including [[Verizon (wireless service)|Verizon Wireless]] and [[Sprint-Nextel]] to include Loopt on their handsets<ref>{{Cite web |last=Hendrickson |first=Mark |date=2008-03-28 |title=Loopt Embraced by Verizon; Starts to Spread Its Mobile Wings |url=https://techcrunch.com/2008/03/28/loopt-embraced-by-verizon-starts-to-spread-its-wings/ |access-date=2026-02-23 |website=TechCrunch |language=en-US}}</ref> and the service reached 4 million registered users in July 2010.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Siegler |first=M. G. |date=2010-07-09 |title=Loopt Hits 4 Million Users, Propelled By iOS 4 And Android |url=https://techcrunch.com/2010/07/09/loopt-4-million/ |access-date=2026-02-23 |website=TechCrunch |language=en-US}}</ref> In addition to its core features, users also had the ability to integrate Loopt with other social networks, including [[Facebook]] and [[Twitter]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Siegler |first=M. G. |date=2010-12-06 |title=Loopt 4.0: Redesigned, Facebook-Heavy, And Location-Based Texting |url=https://techcrunch.com/2010/12/06/loopt-4/ |access-date=2026-02-23 |website=TechCrunch |language=en-US}}</ref>

The company was founded in 2005 and received initial funding from [[Y Combinator (company)|Y Combinator]],<ref name="Loopt: How it all began">{{cite web|url=http://www.loopt.com/about/history|title=Loopt: How it all began...|url-status=dead|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20090119204733/http://www.loopt.com/about/history|archivedate=2009-01-19}}</ref> and completed Series A and B financing led by [[Sequoia Capital]]<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.sequoiacap.com/company/loopt/|title=Sequoia Capital provides funding to Loopt|access-date=2008-01-27|archive-date=2007-12-10|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20071210074305/http://www.sequoiacap.com/company/loopt/|url-status=dead}}</ref> and [[New Enterprise Associates]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.nea.com/PortfolioCompanies/IT/ConsumerTechnology/|title=New Enterprise Associates provides funding to Loopt|url-status=dead|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20080914083053/http://www.nea.com/PortfolioCompanies/IT/ConsumerTechnology/|archivedate=2008-09-14}}</ref> The company's board members included [[TiVo]]-founder Mike Ramsay and Greg McAdoo of Sequoia Capital.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.loopt.com/about/board|title=Loopt Board of Directors|url-status=dead|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20081220134036/http://www.loopt.com/about/board|archivedate=2008-12-20}}</ref> In March 2012 Loopt agreed to be acquired<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |date=2012-03-21 |title=Green Dot to Acquire Loopt |url=https://www.gpsworld.com/wirelessnewsgreen-dot-acquire-loopt-12781/ |access-date=2025-07-09 |website=GPS World |language=en-US}}</ref> by [[Green Dot Corporation]] for $43.4 million in cash, with $9.8 million of that to be set aside for employee retention.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Loopt’s Sam Altman on Why He Sold to Green Dot for $43.4M |url=https://allthingsd.com/20120309/green-dot-buys-location-app-loopt-for-43-4m/ |access-date=2025-07-09 |website=AllThingsD |language=en-US}}</ref>

==History==
Initially called Radiate,<ref name=vox>{{Cite web |last=Gannes |first=Liz |date=2014-03-18 |title=Y Combinator's New Head Startup Whisperer Sam Altman Is Quite a Talker |url=https://www.vox.com/2014/3/18/11624658/y-combinators-new-head-startup-whisperer-sam-altman-is-quite-a-talker |website=Vox}}</ref> Loopt began with funding from [[Y Combinator (company)|Y Combinator]]. That summer, Stanford [[sophomore]]s [[Sam Altman]] and Nick Sivo worked to build the first prototype of Loopt. They were later joined by Alok Deshpande as well as two of Sam's childhood friends, Rick & Tom Pernikoff.<ref name="Loopt: How it all began"/>

Loopt received [[United States dollar|US$]]5 million in Series A funding from [[Sequoia Capital]] and [[New Enterprise Associates]] and struck a deal to launch the service on [[Boost Mobile (Australia)|Boost Mobile]] devices in September 2006. Boost Mobile featured Loopt in a series of commercials that are most known for the "Where you at?" tag line.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ew94okDkCwU |archive-url=https://ghostarchive.org/varchive/youtube/20211213/ew94okDkCwU |archive-date=2021-12-13 |url-status=live|title=Boost Loopt Commercial|website=[[YouTube]] |date=15 December 2006 }}{{cbignore}}</ref>

In August 2007, Loopt expanded the service to select [[Sprint Nextel|Sprint]] phones, and in June 2008, to [[Verizon Wireless|Verizon]]. Loopt announced support for most GPS-enabled Blackberries on June 13, 2008.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.looptblog.com/2008/06/loopt-is-now-su.html|title=Loopt now supports Blackberry|url-status=dead|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20090302125105/http://www.looptblog.com/2008/06/loopt-is-now-su.html|archivedate=2009-03-02}}</ref> Loopt received US$8.25 million in Series B funding in July 2007.

In February 2008, Loopt and [[CBS]] partnered to deliver [[location-based advertising]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://mashable.com/2008/02/06/loopt-cbs-mobile/|title=Loopt and CBS Mobile Partner for GPS Location-Based Ads|website=[[Mashable]] |date=6 February 2008 }}</ref> Seven months later, Loopt released an opt-in feature in Loopt's iPhone application, called Loopt Mix, which uses location-based services to enable iPhone users to find and meet new people nearby.

At Apple's [[Worldwide Developers Conference]] in June 2008, Altman presented the Loopt application for the [[iPhone]]. Loopt for the iPhone became available to US customers of the [[Apple Inc.|Apple]] [[iTunes Store|iTunes]] [[App Store (Apple)|App Store]] on July 11, 2008.

In the summer of 2008, Loopt sponsored [[Black20]]'s ''The Middle Show'' with host Dave Price.

In October 2008, Loopt was sued by Earthcomber for patent infringement.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Hendrickson |first=Mark |date=2008-10-03 |title=Earthcomber Cries Patent Infringement Against Loopt |url=https://techcrunch.com/2008/10/03/earthcomber-cries-patent-infringement-against-loopt/ |access-date=2025-07-09 |website=TechCrunch |language=en-US}}</ref> The case was dropped by Earthcomber in March 2009.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://techcrunch.com/2009/03/23/earthcomber-drops-patent-lawsuit-against-techcrunch-loopt/|title=Earthcomber drops patent lawsuit against TechCrunch, Loopt|date=23 March 2009 }}</ref>

In October 2009, Loopt acquired Y Combinator-backed startup GraffitiGeo for an undisclosed sum.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://techcrunch.com/2009/10/21/more-details-on-loopts-acquisition-of-graffitigeo-and-its-plans-for-the-future/|title=More Details On Loopt's Acquisition of GraffitiGeo.|date=22 October 2009 }}</ref>

In March 2010, Loopt launched an upgraded version of its iPhone app,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.looptblog.com/2010/03/looking-for-something-fun-to-do-try-the-new-loopt-pulse.html|title=Looking for something fun to do? Try the new Loopt Pulse.|url-status=dead|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20100315000456/http://www.looptblog.com/2010/03/looking-for-something-fun-to-do-try-the-new-loopt-pulse.html|archivedate=2010-03-15}}</ref> incorporating place and event information to its Pulse database, bringing in content from ZVents, [[Metromix]], and SonicLiving. These are added to existing content partnerships with [[Citysearch]], [[Zagat]], and [[Microsoft Bing|Bing]].

In March 2010, Loopt launched a product called Loopt Pulse, exclusively designed for the iPad.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.looptblog.com/2010/04/the-vision-behind-looptpulse-for-the-ipad.html|title=The Vision Behind Loopt Pulse.|url-status=dead|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20100411233017/http://www.looptblog.com/2010/04/the-vision-behind-looptpulse-for-the-ipad.html|archivedate=2010-04-11}}</ref>

In April 2010, Loopt launched an upgraded version of its BlackBerry app.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.looptblog.com/2010/04/plan-your-next-move-with-the-new-pulse-on-loopt-for-blackberry.html|title=Plan your next move on the new Pulse on Loopt for BlackBerry.|url-status=dead|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20100418153915/http://www.looptblog.com/2010/04/plan-your-next-move-with-the-new-pulse-on-loopt-for-blackberry.html|archivedate=2010-04-18}}</ref> The upgraded version includes the same places and events upgrade formerly launched in March 2010 for iPhone users.

August 2010 saw [[Steve Boom]] taking over as president of the company.

In December 2010, Loopt launched Loopt version 4.0, which featured a completely updated design.

In March 2012, after raising more than $30 million in venture capital, Loopt announced it had agreed to be acquired by Green Dot Corporation for US$43.4 million in a deal that was most likely orchestrated as a marriage of convenience by joint investor Sequoia Capital, with its products to be shut down at an unspecified date.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Van Grove |first=Jennifer |date=10 March 2012 |title=Loopt was a lemon, dropping to just 500 daily active users prior to sale |url=https://www.reuters.com/article/business/loopt-was-a-lemon-dropping-to-just-500-daily-active-users-prior-to-sale-update-idUS2505978580/ |website=Reuters}}</ref><ref name=":0" />

==SMS invitation issues==
Users of Loopt had to register their [[mobile phone]] number, full name, and [[Birthday|date of birth]]. Loopt's [[privacy]] notice stated that users could control who receives geo-location information via privacy settings.

When Loopt released its native iPhone application on July 10, 2008, the software quickly gained notoriety for sending Short Message Service ([[SMS]]) invites to users' [[address book]]s, seemingly without the user's knowledge; additionally, the SMS service failed to respond to the industry-required STOP message.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.informationweek.com/blog/main/archives/2008/07/loopt_social_ne.html|title=getsatisfaction.com: How can I never receive another Loopt SMS invitation ever?|url-status=dead|archiveurl=https://web.archive.org/web/20111229173601/http://www.informationweek.com/blog/main/archives/2008/07/loopt_social_ne.html|archivedate=2011-12-29}}</ref>

On July 14, 2008, Loopt posted to its blog that the mass invites could be attributed to a confusing user interface, and they were working on an improved invitation flow.<ref>{{cite web |title=Loopt In: iPhone Invite Confusion |url=http://loopt.typepad.com/loopt/2008/07/iphone-invite-c.html}}</ref> Respect for STOP was announced July 15, 2008,<ref>{{cite web |title=Loopt In: Improved iPhone Invites |url=http://loopt.typepad.com/loopt/2008/07/sorry-everyone.html}}</ref> and on July 17, 2008, Loopt released an updated version which addressed the issues with the confusing user interface.<ref>{{cite web |title=Loopt In: Get new version of Loopt for iPhone now! |url=http://www.looptblog.com/2008/07/get-new-version.html}}{{dead link|date=September 2017|bot=InternetArchiveBot|fix-attempted=yes}}</ref>
