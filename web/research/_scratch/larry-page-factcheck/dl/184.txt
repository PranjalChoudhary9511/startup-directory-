Today, Sundar Pichai can afford to laugh over it. The CEO of Google was unsure of getting a job when he interviewed with the company. Chennai-born Pichai, who became CEO of Google in August this year, had at least three reasons to doubt his chances. He was interviewed on April 1, 2004—Google has a long tradition of pulling off some elaborate April Fool’s day hoaxes. In his interview, he was asked about Gmail, a service he had not used until then. And Google co-counder Larry Page, who would interview all key hires until then, did not meet Sundar. “I often joke that I got the job with Google because Page did not interview me,” quipped Pichai at a media interaction in Delhi on Wednesday. Pichai is visiting India for the first time since taking over as the CEO of the company that is currently valued at $260 billion. Pichai’s visit comes at a time when his home country is becoming central to Google’s future business and strategy. India generates second highest traffic on Google search after the US. By early 2016, it is likely to overtake America on the number of active Android devices in use. Pichai was the head of Android before he became the CEO of Google. India is also the country with fastest adoption of Chrome web browser, another product overseen by Pichai. Pichai sees India taking a lead in developing products for the world. “What we make in India, eventually reaches a global scale. And given that India is at the cutting edge of mobile revolution, we think what we build in India will work in many, many places,” he said. YouTube Offline and Map Maker are two examples of made in India Google products that have gone global. To power such initiatives, the company is stepping up hiring and training in the country. Google is is setting up a new campus in Hyderbad and will expand its team in Bengaluru and Hyderabad. It also announced a plan to work with 30 Indian universities and the National Skill Development Corporation to train two million Android developers over the next three years. “It’s a very, very ambitious program and I can’t wait to see what they (developers) build,” Said Pichai. Google is also rolling out free wif-fi service on train stations—a promise it made to Prime Minister Narendra Modi when he visited the company’s Mountain View headquarters in September. Starting with Mumbai Central station in January next year, the company will partner with RailTel in offering high speed free wifi service at 100 railway stations in India. The stations will be chosen in a way that every railway zone is covered. The access for first hour will be high speed (HD video quality download in real time), and will slow down afterwards. Google’s other initiative to take internet services to remote areas—Project Loon—has recently been questioned by the government. The project, which is also being tested in Indonesia and Brazil, provides internet to remote areas through high altitude floating balloons that function as cellphone towers. Recently, union minister for communications and information technology Ravi Shankar Prasad had, in a reply to the Rajya Sabha, said that the project’s proposed frequency band would interfere with cellular transmissions in India. Google said it will demonstrate to the government that its frequency will not interfere.
