IP TAKE: The Carl Victor Page Memorial Foundation is a giant hiding in plain sight. Despite its low profile and lack of a website, tax filings consistently place it among the nation’s largest foundations. As Inside Philanthropy’s Michael Kavate has written , it is a “peer of legacy giants.” Access, however, is nearly nonexistent . The foundation does not appear to accept unsolicited applications or LOIs, and nearly all of its giving flows through donor-advised funds, making the final destination of its grants difficult to trace. When direct grants are visible, they are few, large and often general operating support. The clearest public clues point to climate, clean energy, science and environmental work. Lucinda Southworth’s Oceankind , offers a more transparent window into the family’s ocean conservation interests, while Larry Page’s Google.org-linked giving has included environmentally sustainable energy. Still, the foundation itself remains opaque, and its heavy use of DAFs limits transparency and traditional prospecting. For grantseekers, this is not a cold-LOI prospect. The realistic path is visibility through trusted philanthropic intermediaries and peer-vetted networks: Audacious Project circles, major climate and ocean funder tables, climate regrantors, DAF sponsor and advisor networks, and existing grantee ecosystems. Best-fit organizations will be ambitious, scalable, technically sophisticated and already visible to top-tier philanthropic networks. PROFILE: Established in 2006, the Carl Victor Page Memorial Foundation is the philanthropic vehicle of Google cofounder Larry Page and Lucinda Southworth . Named for Page’s father, the Palo Alto-based fund supports work at the intersection of technology, sustainability and human rights, with some overlap across other family-linked corporate and private giving channels. Page grew up in Michigan, the son of two computer science professors at Michigan State University. His father, Carl Page, was an early artificial intelligence researcher, while his mother, Gloria, also taught computer science. Larry Page studied at the University of Michigan before earning a master’s degree in computer science at Stanford, where he met fellow Ph.D. student Sergey Brin. Their paper, “The Anatomy of a Large-Scale Hypertextual Web Search Engine,” was one of the most downloaded academic articles of its time. Between 1996 and 1997, the two created the PageRank algorithm forming the cornerstone of Google search, the world's largest search engine and the algorithm that became the foundation of Google. Southworth brings her own scientific credentials to the couple’s philanthropy. A research geneticist, she studied at the University of Pennsylvania and Oxford before earning a Ph.D. in biomedical informatics at Stanford. She and Page married in 2007 and now direct the foundation together. Observed Grantmaking Areas: Since the Carl Victor Page Memorial Foundation gives primarily to donor-advised funds, much of the fund's giving is broad in scope and unclear to discern. In 2023, this funder gave $218 million to the Schwab Charitable Fund, while it gave over $275 million to the National Philanthropic Trust in 2022. In previous years, it has also given similar amounts to Vanguard Charitable; however, the National Philanthropic Trust has been a repeat beneficiary. Grants for Climate and Environmental Conservation Tax filings suggest that this funder has ramped climate giving since 2023. One pathway in here occurs via major climate intermediaries, Audacious-linked cohorts, and peer/referral networks, not direct cold outreach. This funding area indicates an interest in the intersection between renewable energy and technology, as well as climate change research: The 2024 grantees included Clean Slate Initiative, Think of Us, Upstream USA, World Resources Institute, Global Fishing Watch, Climate Breakthrough, Rockefeller Philanthropy Advisors, UC Berkeley Foundation, Inkomoko, Avina Americas, Every Cure, Food for Education Foundation, European Climate Foundation, Great Barrier Reef Foundation USA, and Windward Fund. Other recent grantees include WRI ($4.75 million for land restoration), Global Fishing Watch ($3 million), Climate Breakthrough ($10 million general operating support), Rockefeller Philanthropy Advisors ($6 million for ecological priorities identified by marine scientists), Avina Americas ($1.5 million for comprehensive land maps), European Climate Foundation ($2.15 million for wind and solar power), Great Barrier Reef Foundation USA ($2 million for coral reef research and technology in Australia), and Windward Fund ($6 million for an interdisciplinary network in energy, agriculture and waste). Page also serves as a board member of the XPrize Foundation . Grants for Health, Medical Research and Diseases Health grants are fewer than those for other causes, but focused on diseases, influenza and cancer research. Of course, there could be other interests here supported via DAFs, but according to 990s over the past few years, giving here is more modest. Health-related grantees appear to be either major research institutions, Audacious-linked groups, or high-scale systems-change nonprofits. Reproductive health, health/genomics/drug repurposing and child welfare appear as funding interests. Page and Southworth joined the growing number of funders donating to combat Ebola, pledging $15 million from his Carl Victor Page Memorial Foundation, and another $10 million from Google.org. The UC Berkeley Foundation received $750,000 in both FY2023 and FY2024 for genomics research. Other health grantees have included the American Cancer Society, Shoo the Flu and the Regents of the University of California Berkeley. Important Grant Details: The latest 2024 tax-year data shows 17 grants , a $2 million median grant , and a range of $400,000 to $235 million . FY2024 shows revenue of $798.6 million, expenses of $322.0 million, net assets of $4.45 billion, and charitable disbursements of $286.1 million. Previous 990s kept a tight lid on what kinds of focus areas receive support from the fund; however, recent 990s from 2023 onwards reveal an increasing pattern of giving. In 2024, most listed recipients were direct nonprofit grantees, even though the largest share of dollars still went to a DAF. That said, the fund's tendency to give its largest grants to DAFs gives the impression that grants cluster in Colorado, Pennsylvania and New York; however, we can't know yet for certain where grants are actually clustering. For DAFs, the foundation made two FY2024 grants to National Philanthropic Trust: $1,262,880 and $235,000,000, both described as “general grant-making support for a donor-advised fund.” Combined, NPT received $236,262,880, or about 82.8% of the $285,312,880 grants paid during the year. This foundation does not accept unsolicited applications or invite contact. Geography : The foundation is based in Palo Alto, but visible 2024 recipients include organizations based in Washington, D.C.; New York; Boston; Berkeley; Orlando; Clinton, Washington; and international-facing entities. The grant pattern is better described as national/global through U.S. and international intermediaries, not local California grantmaking. Types of work funded: General operating support, project support, research, ecological priorities, land restoration, ocean-data transparency, climate policy/infrastructure, coral reef research/technology, genomics research, drug repurposing, reproductive health systems, child welfare systems, entrepreneurship for displacement-affected communities and regranting/DAF grantmaking.
