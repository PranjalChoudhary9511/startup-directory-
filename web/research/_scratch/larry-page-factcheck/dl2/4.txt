Google co-founder Larry Page speaks with people at his lunch table during the Clinton Global Initiative in New York September 27, 2007.&nbsp; REUTERS/Chip East It must be nice to be a billionaire.&nbsp; Alphabet CEO Larry Page, for example, has discreetly&nbsp;poured part of his wealth into two&nbsp;secret startups,&nbsp;Zee.Aero and Kitty Hawk, which are dedicated to building flying cars, reports Bloomberg . But making a classic science-fiction dream reality&nbsp;isn't even the coolest part. Apparently, Page turned the entire second floor of Zee.Aero's headquarters into a "man cave worthy of a billionaire" —&nbsp;complete with a bedroom, climbing wall, and an actual rocket engine, courtesy of Tesla and SpaceX CEO Elon Musk. From Bloomberg :&nbsp; Page initially restricted the Zee.Aero crew to the first floor, retaining the second floor for a man cave worthy of a multibillionaire: bedroom, bathroom, expensive paintings, a treadmill-like climbing wall, and one of SpaceX’s first rocket engines —&nbsp;a gift from his pal Musk. As part of the secrecy, Zee.Aero employees didn’t refer to Page by name; he was known as GUS, the guy upstairs. Zee.Aero employees got a few nice perks too, such as&nbsp;catered lunches, including at one point, $900 of catered barbecue.&nbsp; But Page didn't keep his secret office crash pad for long. Apparently, Zee.Aero expanded so quickly that eventually it needed the second floor for more engineers —&nbsp;it now employs 150 people —&nbsp;so Page had to move out his expensive paintings, workout gear, and collectibles.&nbsp; But it's not like he can't afford to build a new man cave.
