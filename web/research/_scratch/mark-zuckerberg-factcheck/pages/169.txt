Aytac Unal | Anadolu Agency via Getty Images The online stock-photo marketplace Shutterstock announced Tuesday it would acquire Giphy from Meta Platforms for $53 million, a significant loss for Meta, which acquired Giphy in 2020 for $315 million. The acquisition is an all-cash deal, and in an investor presentation , Shutterstock said it would maintain its full-year revenue guidance. The acquisition would add &quot;minimal revenue in 2023,&quot; Shutterstock noted. The deal is expected to close in June. Shutterstock&#x27;s shares rose nearly 2% in morning trading Tuesday. U.K.&#x27;s Competition and Markets Authority had ordered Meta to divest Giphy in 2022, citing potential anti-competitive effects. The CMA disclosed it was probing the deal in June 2020. Giphy, which is a platform for searching for and using animated images in messaging apps, was well-integrated into Meta&#x27;s ecosystem, and had been an acquisition target for the social-media company years before Meta acquired it in 2020. Technology acquisitions have faced heavy scrutiny from the U.K.&#x27;s anti-trust authority in recent months. The CMA blocked Microsoft&#x27;s proposed $69 billion acquisition of Activision in April, citing potentially adverse effects to the cloud gaming industry . Like many technology companies, Meta has faced stiffening regulatory oversight in the U.S. as well. The FTC proposed a &quot;blanket&quot; ban preventing Meta from monetizing young user&#x27;s data and alleged Meta had violated a 2020 privacy order. In a statement, Meta described the FTC effort as a &quot;political stunt.&quot; &quot;We are grateful to the Giphy team during this uncertain time for their business, and wish them every success,&quot; a Meta spokesperson told CNBC at the time of the divestiture order.
