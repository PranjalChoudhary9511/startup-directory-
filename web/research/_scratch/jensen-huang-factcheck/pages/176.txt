 NVIDIA: Revenue up 105.9% YoY; up to $105B OpenAI guarantee is a key off-balance-sheet risk
 NVIDIA reported FY27 Q2 revenue of $96.2B, up 105.9% year on year, with an operating margin of 66.2%, 5.4 points higher than a year earlier; growth shows no sign of deceleration. The principal risk sits off the balance sheet: in August the company guaranteed up to $105B of OpenAI's Ohio data-center lease obligations (8-K, 2026-08-17), and supply commitments rose from $119B to $279B in a single quarter (10-Q, 2026-08-26). Cash conversion is lagging earnings: days sales outstanding increased from 45 to 60, and Q2 operating cash flow of $24.1B was approximately 40% of net income of $59.7B. The shares trade at 24.4x current-year earnings, below the peer median of 30.5x, despite the highest revenue growth in the peer group. This is the main driver of the favorable scorecard outcome.

 01 Market context
 Rising rates are drawing capital away from equities. The 10-year Treasury yield rose 53.5bp in one month to 5.25%, reaching its highest level since 2007 in September, and the Federal Reserve raised rates by 25bp on September 16. The S&P 500 declined 1.2% over the week and 0.5% over the month, while the Russell 2000 fell 5.5% over the month; small caps came under pressure first.
 Flows have concentrated in AI hardware. The technology ETF (XLK) gained 4.9% over the month and 35.6% year to date, well ahead of the S&P 500's 12.1%. NVDA rose 17.5% over three months, outperforming the S&P 500 by 15.2 points, and has outperformed by 13.6 points over one year. The VIX at 16.04 indicates limited risk aversion.
 Transmission to the stock: NVDA's one-year beta is 1.89 (its sensitivity to S&P 500 moves). A further rise in rates would raise the discount rate applied to highly valued growth equities (our inference: should the 10-year yield exceed 5.5%, multiple compression would outweigh earnings upgrades). A second channel runs through customers: OpenAI and other AI customers rely on debt to fund data-center construction, so higher financing costs directly weaken their capacity to pay, and NVIDIA has already guaranteed part of these obligations.

 8-K 
 Guarantee of up to $105B for OpenAI's SB Energy data-center leases
 8-K (items 1.01, 2.03): NVIDIA entered into multiple residual value guarantees with SB Energy covering leases of approximately 4.25GW at the PORTS campus in Ohio, with OpenAI as tenant. If OpenAI becomes insolvent or fails to pay rent, NVIDIA must cover the shortfall between the guaranteed minimum value and the proceeds of a re-lease or sale, subject to a cumulative cap of $105B. The guarantees take effect in nine phases as construction is completed, with the first expected in FY2029. The company states that each generation deployed represents approximately 1.5 million GPUs, or $150B–$200B of revenue (CFO commentary, 2026-08-26). In effect, NVIDIA is extending credit support to a customer from its own balance sheet in exchange for orders.

 03 Rating and scorecard
 AlphaScope rating
 Buy 
 Total return expected to outperform the S&P 500 by 10–20 percentage points over the next 12 months · Weighted scorecard 3.70 
 Publisher's rating: Buy, in line with the rule-based rating (weighted scorecard score of 3.70). Growth and profitability lead the peer group and the valuation sits below the peer median. Cash conversion and off-balance-sheet guarantees are the principal risks to monitor.
 Exceptional growth. FY27 Q2 revenue was $96.2B (+105.9%), and the operating margin rose from 60.8% to 66.2% within a year.
 Valuation below peers. The current fiscal-year P/E is 24.4x, below the peer median of 30.5x, while revenue growth is the highest in the peer group.
 Positive price trend. The shares outperformed the S&P 500 by 15.2 percentage points over three months. However, they trade only 2.7% below the 52-week high and 14.6% above the 200-day moving average, with a one-year beta of 1.89, so short-term volatility could be significant if sentiment turns.
 Principal risks: Days sales outstanding rose from 45 to 60. Q2 operating cash flow of $24.1B was about 40% of net income of $59.7B, and profit included at least $6.4B of unrealized investment gains. The company has guaranteed up to $105B for OpenAI (8-K, 2026-08-17); guarantees and commitments total $530.5B, 3.9 times annual operating cash flow.
 What would change the rating: A downgrade would follow if Q3 results on November 18 show revenue below $105.8B or gross margin below 73.5%, if receivable days lengthen further, or if OpenAI encounters financing or payment problems.

 Earnings quality 2 / 5 
 Three points deducted from 5: (1) Equity investment gains accounted for 13.3% of trailing 12-month pre-tax income ($30.6B / $229.8B), and 22.8% in Q1 alone. The 10-Q states that they were "primarily driven by unrealized gains", and at least $6.4B of Q2's $7.8B represented unrealized gains on holdings still held at quarter-end (10-Q, 2026-08-26): one point deducted. (2) Change in non-GAAP definition: from FY27, adjusted figures no longer exclude stock-based compensation, and prior periods have been restated (8-K, 2026-05-20). The change is more conservative, but old and new figures are not directly comparable, so the rule deducts 1 point. (3) Receivables rose 54.9% and inventory 22.4% quarter on quarter, both faster than revenue growth of 17.9%, reflecting extended customer payment terms (90 days to one year) and the Vera Rubin inventory build described by the company (10-Q, CFO commentary, 2026-08-26): one point deducted. Trailing 12-month FCF / net income is 0.66, above 0.6, so no deduction applies; however, Q2 alone was only 0.36.

 Risk 2 / 5 
 Three points deducted from 5: (1) Customer concentration: the five largest direct customers account for 70% of receivables (22%, 14%, 13%, 11%, 10%), and a single customer represented 16% of Q2 revenue (10-Q).  Key points from the filings
 All base figures reconcile: revenue, operating income, net income, GAAP EPS, operating cash flow, receivables and inventory show zero variance between yfinance and XBRL for the last four quarters. The discrepancies arise in metrics derived by yfinance and in the text of the SEC filings.
 $108.5B of off-balance-sheet guarantees . yfinance reflects no guarantees. An 8-K (2026-08-17, items 1.01, 2.03) discloses residual value guarantees on OpenAI's leases at the SB Energy campus, capped at $105B; together with $3.5B of existing land, power and shell guarantees for AI cloud providers, total exposure is $108.5B (CFO commentary, 2026-08-26). The guarantees phase in from FY2029 and decline as OpenAI pays rent. The arrangement post-dates the close of Q2 and is therefore not reflected in the quarterly financial statements.
 $279B of supply commitments . The commitments table in the 10-Q (2026-08-26) shows supply and capacity commitments rising from $119B in the prior quarter to $279B, primarily for memory; total future commitments are $366B, in addition to $36B of AI cloud agreements and $20B of data-center leases signed on behalf of third parties. Of this, $267B falls due before the end of FY2029 (January 2029).
 The increase in receivables reflects deliberately extended credit terms . yfinance shows receivables up 54.9% quarter on quarter. The filing explains that large multi-quarter contracts with investment-grade customers carry payment terms of "90 days up to one year"; DSO rose from 45 to 60 days, and management states that these arrangements "will continue to affect the timing of our operating cash flows" (10-Q). The five largest customers account for 70% of receivables. Q2 operating cash flow of $24.1B was less than half the prior-quarter level.
 Earnings include unrealized gains . yfinance labels Q2's $7.77B as "Gain On Sale Of Security", whereas the 10-Q states the gains were "primarily driven by unrealized gains": $1.5B on public equities still held at quarter-end and $4.9B of upward revaluations of private holdings, at least $6.4B unrealized in total. Q1's $15.9B was likewise primarily unrealized (CFO commentary, 2026-05-20). Public equity holdings rose from $12.9B to $42.8B, of which $36.9B remains subject to lock-up; a decline in those share prices would reduce reported earnings directly.
 The increase in debt reflects a single bond issuance . Total debt rose from $12.35B to $38.35B (yfinance), consistent with the 2026-06-18 8-K (item 8.01): $25.0B in seven tranches, with coupons of 4.25%–5.625% and maturities of 2028–2056.
 The adjusted definition has changed . From FY27, non-GAAP figures no longer exclude stock-based compensation, and prior periods have been restated (8-K, 2026-05-20): FY26 Q4 adjusted EPS was revised from $1.62 to $1.59. yfinance's earnings_history still uses the previous figure, so its TTM adjusted EPS of $7.01 combines two definitions.
 Items disclosed only in the filings
 Trigger conditions for the OpenAI guarantee : if OpenAI's insolvency results in a lease default, or OpenAI fails to pay rent, NVIDIA must cover the shortfall between the guaranteed minimum value and the proceeds of a re-lease or sale; upon a trigger, NVIDIA may elect to assume the lease, require re-letting or initiate a sale (8-K, 2026-08-17). NVIDIA also holds an option, exercisable at its discretion, to guarantee approximately 3.8GW of additional capacity.
 VIE exposure : $3.3B of equity-method investments in infrastructure financing entities; those classified as variable interest entities (VIEs) carry a maximum loss exposure of $4.7B and are not consolidated (10-Q).
 New derivatives : in Q2 NVIDIA received warrants in public companies with a notional amount of $4.8B and a fair value of $824M (Level 3, i.e., no quoted price and a model-based valuation), as well as a $1.0B equity forward contract (10-Q).
 $47.9B of private equity holdings : $22.3B at the beginning of the year, $31.0B added in the first half, and an upward revaluation of $4.9B this quarter (10-Q). Valuations are based on comparable transactions, with no observable market price.
 Inventory composition : raw materials rose from $3.8B to $11.3B and work in process from $8.8B to $13.4B, while finished goods declined from $8.8B to $6.9B (end-January to end-July, 10-Q). The composition is consistent with an inventory build for Vera Rubin rather than unsold product.
 China H200 : licensed but restricted by Chinese authorities; shipments below 1% of Data Center revenue, and a 25% tariff on units routed through US inspection that cannot be passed on (10-Q).
 Hugging Face acquisition : approximately $11.9B plus up to $1.0B of retention equity, expected to close in the first half of 2027; the 8-K adds a risk factor regarding potential regulation of open-source models, including those originating in China (8-K, 2026-09-03).
 Dividend : the quarterly dividend was raised from $0.01 to $0.25 (board approval on 2026-05-18); Q2 payments totaled $6.0B.
