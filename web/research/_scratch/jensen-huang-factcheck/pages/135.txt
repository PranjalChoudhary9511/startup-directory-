 Nvidia's unprecedented US$105bn backstop tees up SB Energy for IPO
 | 
 IFR 2647 - 22 Aug 2026 - 28 Aug 2026 
 | 
 Gareth Gore 
 , 
 Amy Or 
 Share 
 Nvidia has extended US$105bn of guarantees to SB Energy in an unprecedented deal that will give the relatively new data centre developer the financial backing to build the world’s largest facility – and go public at a potential US$50bn valuation .
 It is the largest example yet of the controversial “circular financing” deals that have underpinned the current AI boom, in which companies like Nvidia provide financing and guarantees to their customers and suppliers. The deal will likely increase concerns about the risks the chipmaker is assuming to keep the hot streak going.
 Nvidia has already announced deals to invest US$30bn in OpenAI and up to US$10bn in Anthropic, the two leading independent AI labs. In recent days, it disclosed a stake worth more than US$20bn in SpaceX and a smaller position in CoreWeave. All are major customers of Nvidia.
 But the SB Energy deal takes that to a new level. In addition to extending US$105bn of so-called residual value guarantees, which would leave Nvidia on the hook for the cost of renting the world’s largest data centre if the tenant – OpenAI – falls into financial difficulty, it will also inject US$1.5bn directly into SB Energy.
 “This partnership underscores Nvidia’s evolving position as a provider of the full AI stack, not just chips – including land, power and shell, which is fast becoming as vital as compute in the age of AI,” said Vivek Arya, a research analyst at Bank of America.
 Aggressive new phase
 Coming just a week after it announced an initiative to unlock US$500bn of financing for customers – with up to a quarter of that underwritten by Nvidia – the SB Energy deal confirms an aggressive new phase for the chipmaker as it takes on huge liabilities to keep the orders coming in.
 “Today’s conditions make this worthwhile: solid GPU rental rates, compute scarcity, and Nvidia’s industry-leading free-cashflow generation,” said Arya. “The risk: if AI demand slows, both Nvidia’s growth rate and balance sheet could come under pressure.”
 The guarantees will underpin the construction of a data centre in Pike County, Ohio, providing 4.25GW of compute running on Nvidia chips. SB Energy will construct and operate the facility, which could later be expanded to 8GW, making it the world’s largest facility, and rent it out to OpenAI under a 20-year lease.
 The Wall Street Journal previously reported that Nvidia was considering a US$250bn backstop to build 10GW on the site. While that appears to have been scaled back, it is possible that Nvidia will step in with tens of billions of further guarantees for SB Energy to support the second phase.
 Such guarantees will leave Nvidia exposed to the success or failure of OpenAI, which is in a battle for supremacy in the AI race against some of the world's most profitable companies. It will burn through more than US$200bn of capital before finally turning a profit in 2030, according to internal projections.
 “We are securing long-lived infrastructure for Nvidia compute so OpenAI can deploy the most productive AI factories that can be upgraded repeatedly with each new generation delivering more intelligence and better economics,” said Jensen Huang, the chipmaker’s founder and CEO.
 Path to IPO
 The guarantees and capital injection are transformational for SB Energy, a relatively new operator in the data centre space that has yet to complete a project. It was set up in 2019 as a solar energy provider but last year pivoted to data centres after signing a deal to provide compute to OpenAI.
 One person with knowledge of the company's plans told IFR that, following the Nvidia deal, SB Energy will now move forward with plans to go public – with current plans to list in the last week of September. It hopes to raise at least US$5bn at a US$50bn valuation, with Nvidia possibly injecting US$1.5bn of that.
 “It's a big contract with OpenAI and backing from Nvidia so the headline is clearly positive,” said the person. “The first round of testing-the-waters meetings were largely introductory and focused on getting to know investors. Now there’s actually some meat on the bone.
 “The devil is in the detail, though, and that's what investors will be scrutinising over the next few weeks.”
 Proceeds from the deal, together with the Nvidia guarantees, would allow SB Energy to move ahead with the initial phase of construction – and give lenders confidence as it goes out to seek additional financing for the Ohio facility. The entire project could cost more than US$400bn based on current industry costs.
 Nvidia’s deal with SB Energy will further entwine the chipmaker with SoftBank Group. Both are major investors in OpenAI, with SoftBank agreeing to invest US$64.6bn in the lab in return for a 13% stake. In February, Nvidia said it would invest US$30bn as part of a commitment from OpenAI to buy more of its chips.
 SB Energy is also a SoftBank company.
 “Infrastructure is vital for the AI economy,” said Rich Hossfeld, the company’s co-CEO, who said the project would create tens of thousands of jobs. “SB Energy is building power-first infrastructure at unprecedented scale while strengthening the communities that make it possible.”
 Shift towards guarantees
 While residual value guarantees are commonly used by other big AI companies – Moody’s last month estimated that the six largest hyperscalers may have US$820bn of such commitments – this appears to be the first time Nvidia has used them.
 Previously, it has chosen to inject equity upfront into its customers to help them acquire its chips, like in January when it invested US$2bn in CoreWeave after the company was hit by concerns over its financial viability. Those concerns pushed up its borrowing costs, threatening its ability to buy chips.
 Bank of America estimates that Nvidia has injected US$70bn of equity into customers and suppliers over the past year. Together with the US$105bn guarantee and the US$125bn of potential liabilities under the US$500bn initiative announced in August, its total potential commitments amount to US$300bn.
 Under the latest agreement, if OpenAI files for insolvency or is unable to make payments to SB Energy, Nvidia will commit to paying any shortfall between the guaranteed minimum value of a lease – totalling US$105bn – and amounts recovered through a replacement lease or sale.
 Goldman Sachs and JP Morgan were financial advisers for SB Energy on the deal with Nvidia. Morgan Stanley was the chipmaker's financial adviser.
 SB Energy confidentially filed on May 20 to go public. JP Morgan , Goldman Sachs , Morgan Stanley , Citigroup and Mizuho Securities were hired as bookrunners to lead the offering, people familiar with the deal told IFR.
