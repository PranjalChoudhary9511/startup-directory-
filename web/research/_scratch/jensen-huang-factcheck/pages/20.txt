 About
 In 1993, I founded NVIDIA with Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem to solve the problem of 3D graphics for the PC. Our pioneering work in accelerated computing led to the redefinition of modern computer graphics and the creation of modern AI. Now, we are fundamentally changing how computing works and what computers can do. The next industrial revolution has begun. NVIDIA has more than 32,000 people. We are strong and growing. We are consistently recognized as one of the “Best Places to Work” by Glassdoor, one of the “100 Best Companies to Work For” by Fortune magazine, a “TIME100 Most Influential Company,” the “Most Innovative Company of 2024” by Fast Company, and many more. We’re hiring, with openings in every corner of our company—and we are looking for talented, driven, and adventurous people who want to tackle challenges no one else can solve. NVIDIA employees build technology that moves humanity forward and support the communities in which they work and live. Recognized as a top company in social responsibility, our employees are passionate donors to hundreds of charities around the globe. Go to careers.nvidia.com to take the first step. I look forward to building the future together.

Today, with over 100 industry partners, we introduced the NVIDIA Open Agent Safety Platform, bringing together OpenShell and Sentry.
Artificial intelligence is extraordinary technology that will advance discovery, productivity, security, health, and prosperity for generations to come. But its full promise can only be realized when people have confidence that AI is being built to be safe and deployed with wisdom and responsibility. 
NVIDIA Open Agent Safety Platform Reference Design combines NVIDIA OpenShell and NVIDIA Sentry. OpenShell is an open-source secure runtime that gives AI agents clear, enforceable boundaries. It traces their actions and enforces policy as they work. NVIDIA Sentry delivers added layer of security with hardware-based enforcement on NVIDIA BlueField, continuously monitoring agent activity through a trusted telemetry and detection pipeline and enabling millisecond-scale containment and quarantine.
This is bigger than a single product. It's the beginning of an open ecosystem to build the trust layer for safe agent systems. Together, we are building the foundation of the AI economy.
Trust and innovation are not in conflict. Safety is how trust is earned. We must build not only the most capable AI, but the most trusted AI, so that this extraordinary technology can realize its enormous promise for the world. https://nvda.ws/4hOkDx7

 Any rapidly growing computing task quickly reaches the stage where it has to be split among numerous processing engines. As soon as that happens, the interconnect among those chips becomes critical: if it’s too slow, or inefficiently utilized, it quickly becomes the bottleneck and processors sit idle waiting for data. Ed Doe and Dan Daly were early to realize that this time was coming for AI inference and agentic  jobs.  
In fact they had two early insights: first, that inference would soon be split among many chips that would have to move sufficient amounts of data that the interconnect would become the bottleneck.  Second, unlike Internet traffic, AI tasks have a certain structure that can be leveraged to dramatically improve performance.  
They knew that doing this right would unlock stranded capacity of compute engines sitting idle waiting for their data fuel, although neither of them - or anyone associated with the company - ever imagined the sheer extent of the opportunity cost we have today of an HBM-laden XPU standing by while the data loads.  The problem they started Delos Data to solve is here today, and it’s an order of magnitude larger than anyone ever thought.
We got involved with Delos because Ed and Dan were the right team working on the right issue.  To create a high performance network you need to understand latency, and to create one for today’s ever-changing AI world you need to understand workloads and programmability. Both came from network switch companies that were acquired by Intel: Dan from Fulcrum, the lowest latency product of its time, and Ed from Broadcom and Barefoot, the time’s most performant and programmable product.  They complement each other in skills, with Dan’s technical brilliance a perfect match for Ed’s savvy and market knowledge.  And they’re terrific people.  
Since starting the company, they have built a phenomenal team of collaborators, and - importantly for the magnitude of the task - have attracted sufficient capital to get the job done.  Their progress has been consistent and impressive.  The problem they are solving is now bigger than any of us ever imagined.  And now they have the financial fuel to build the business the right way.  We at Matrix are thrilled to be a part of it.

 We’re seven weeks into 2026, and $2.3 billion has already poured into AI hardware startups. But this wave is a lot different than what we saw over the past twelve months...
Last year, capital flowed to ecosystem builders: companies focused on making GPU-based infrastructure more efficient. This year, the shift is further down the stack, toward startups reinventing the compute/acceleration architecture layer.
Investors seem keen to back startups built on the premise that GPUs won’t be the same architecture that scales AI efficiently for the future, and two companies just received significant raises for their radically different views.
Cerebras ($1.1B Series H)  Has never wavered that their wafer-scale engine would redefine how frontier models can be trained at scale. It has. That, and their progress in inference with OpenAI is evident.
SambaNova ($350M Series E) By contrast, they’ve built a reconfigurable dataflow architecture designed to optimize how models move and process data, not just how fast cores can compute it.
On the other side of training, inference presents the economic challenge of deploying models at global scale, which is where GPU cost structures really start to strain, especially as token volumes explode.
That’s where companies like Etched ($500M Series B) and Positron AI ($230M Series B) have captured investor attention. Both are building custom ASIC architectures purpose-built for model deployment, and intend to deliver better performance-per-watt and cost-per-token than GPUs.
There are even teams rethinking the fundamental physics of compute itself. Neurophos ($100M Series A) represents one of the more radical bets: optical processing. Using photonics to move and process data promises massive gains in bandwidth and energy efficiency for training.
This funding surge is more than a capital markets story. It’s a signal to an entire workforce of engineers and developers who have spent the past decade architecting GPU clusters and optimizing code. 
A new wave of opportunities was just validated.
With all due respect to Nvidia and their extraordinary technical progress that enabled The Age of AI, organizations that reach critical mass inevitably create a different kind of career question for top engineers: “Once the systems are scaled (and my equity is vested) are there other exciting new challenges?”
One could assume the MANGO companies always get their first pick of the best talent because of their scale and compensation power. But in practice, it’s far more nuanced. The best engineers are mission-driven. They want proximity to unsolved problems and the chance to bring new systems to life. 
Compensation follows impact, not the other way around.
In my view, startups with clear vision have never been in a better place to win talent, and those that will build the strongest teams will articulate their differentiation and mission as passionately as they do their tech.

 The "Age of Inference" is here and it’s fueling the rise of agentic applications
At NVIDIA’s GTC keynote today, Jensen Huang described what he calls the Age of Inference. For the past several years, the AI race has largely focused on training ever larger models with more data and more compute. The center of gravity is now shifting toward inference, the ability for models to generate outputs quickly, efficiently, and continuously in real world applications.
This transition matters because inference is where AI becomes economically useful. Tokens are increasingly the atomic unit of the AI economy. As the cost per token declines and performance improves, as NVIDIA is signaling with Blackwell and Vera Rubin, the economics of deploying AI systems change significantly. Lower inference costs allow AI systems to run more frequently, interact with more data, and support far more complex workflows. Lower cost of AI makes personal superintelligence accessible to everyone. 
This gives rise to agentic applications. Instead of a single model generating a response to a prompt, we are beginning to see systems composed of multiple AI agents coordinating tasks, calling tools, operating software, and interacting with external systems. AI is moving beyond answering questions toward performing productive workflows. 
In many scenarios, AI agents will need to verify identity, exchange information, coordinate decisions, and transact value across networks.  Blockchain infrastructure provides primitives uniquely suited for these interactions.  Decentralized identity, programmable finance, and verifiable economic coordination create a foundation for machine driven AI commerce.
This convergence is giving rise to what many industry leaders describe as agentic commerce. In these systems intelligent agents transact on behalf of individuals, organizations, or even other AI systems.  Procurement agents negotiate with supplier agents, financial agents optimize treasury allocations, and personal AI assistants coordinate services and commerce. Blockchain provides the rails for AI agents to scale, transaction, coordinate, and execute transactions with each other. 
At Decasonic, this is exactly where we are focused as investors. We believe the next generation of enduring technology companies will be built around agent native applications that deliver real outcomes for users and enterprises. The infrastructure layer is advancing rapidly and the cost curves are moving in the right direction. The opportunity now is for founders to build the applications that sit on top of this new inference driven foundation.
