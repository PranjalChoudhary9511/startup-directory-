Forbes' 2026 list of the country's 100 wealthiest, published Wednesday, puts the Adani Group chairman's family fortune at $112.7 billion, ahead of Reliance Industries chairman Mukesh Ambani at $86.3 billion. It is the first time Adani has topped the Forbes India list since 2022. In January 2023, a report by short-seller Hindenburg Research wiped more than $100 billion off his group's market value, and Ambani held the No. 1 spot for the next three annual lists. The swing this year ran in opposite directions. Adani added $20.7 billion, the largest gain of anyone on the list. Ambani lost $18.7 billion, which Forbes attributed to margin pressure at Reliance, including in its retail business, that weighed on the shares. Both results came in a bad year for Indian assets. The benchmark index fell 9.5% and the rupee lost 8.5% against the dollar, yet the combined wealth of the top 100 stayed at about $1 trillion. Adani's recovery has two parts. The business kept growing, across ports, airports, power and cement, and the group has announced a $100 billion push into artificial intelligence infrastructure. The legal overhang also cleared: a $275 million U.S. Treasury sanctions settlement in May, the Justice Department dropping its charges, and this week a settlement with India's market regulator that closed a six-year investigation into the group's shareholding. The family fortune is still below its 2022 peak of $150 billion. Other rich lists agree. Hurun's India ranking, published Sept. 23, also moved Adani above Ambani, with the Adani family up 13% and the Ambani family down 10%. Bloomberg's daily index, which uses a different method and values Adani at about $109 billion, has had him ahead of Ambani for much of 2026; on that index he was briefly Asia's richest person until ByteDance founder Zhang Yiming overtook him in mid-September. Ambani has a catalyst waiting. Jio Platforms, Reliance's mobile operator, has regulatory approval for an initial public offering that could raise up to $3.8 billion, which would be India's largest ever. Reliance has also raised about $2.3 billion in rupee bonds in the past three weeks. Further down the list, Savitri Jindal held third at $39.8 billion, and steel magnate Lakshmi Mittal was the biggest percentage gainer, up 74% to $34.7 billion and eight places to fourth. Four names joined: Krishna Chivukula after listing Indo-MIM in July; brothers Kushal and Chaitanya Desai at No. 69 with $4.25 billion as cable maker Apar Industries nearly doubled; Aditya Khemka at No. 83 with $3.6 billion; and the three Virani brothers behind Balaji Wafers, after selling a stake to General Atlantic. The software founders went backward. Shiv Nadar, Azim Premji and N.R. Narayana Murthy all lost ground. The top spot is not settled. Adani's lead depends on a group still rebuilding trust and now wagering $100 billion on AI; Ambani's comeback depends on a Jio listing that could reprice Reliance in a day.
