Make VnExpress a preferred source to prioritise our updates in your Google search results Mukesh Ambani, the second-richest man in Asia, was among the world’s three largest wealth losers on Thursday, despite his new company Jio’s record-breaking US$3.2 billion initial public offering. The chairman of conglomerate Reliance Industries, the largest company in India by market cap, lost $2.6 billion in wealth, according to real-time data by Forbes. His net worth plunged 3% from Wednesday to $79.9 billion. This made him the world’s third-largest wealth loser on Thursday morning, behind electric car tycoon Elon Musk with a $18.4-billion decline, and social media tycoon Mark Zuckerberg with a $2.6-billion drop. Mukesh Ambani, chairman and managing director of Reliance Industries, and his wife Nita Ambani attend the listing ceremony of Jio Platforms Ltd at the National Stock Exchange of India in Mumbai, India, October 28, 2026. Photo by Reuters On Wednesday his telecom-to-digital firm Reliance Jio Platforms soared 27% in its Mumbai debut, making it India's second-largest company by market capitalization after the largest-ever IPO in India. Jio's valuation of around $137 billion also helped to lift ‌the broader Reliance conglomerate's combined market capitalization to $320 billion to make it India's top firm by that metric, surpassing the salt-to-aviation Tata Group, Reuters reported. The Jio listing, the first for the Reliance conglomerate in two decades, advances Ambani's plan to turn Reliance from an oil-and-chemicals conglomerate into an "everything company" spanning consumer products and technology. The listing ceremony on Wednesday in Mumbai was attended by members of the Ambani family, including Mukesh's eldest son, Akash, who is Jio's managing director. Reliance Industries shares fell 2%, closing at their lowest level in nearly three years. They have plunged 26.5% year to date, weighing on Ambani’s net worth. His family reportedly controls a 50% stake in the company. The stock's performance has come amid volatility in Indian equities, uncertainty over energy markets and concerns over global economic growth, according to The Economic Times.
