MUMBAI: The merger of Reliance Petroleum (RPL) with Reliance Industries (RIL) has, for the second time, closed the lid on RPL. The first time RPL was conceived ��� in 1993 ��� was when the 9 million-tonnes-per-annum Jamnagar refinery plan was announced. RPL, then went public late that year to raise funds with a public issue of Rs 862 crore, making it among the largest for the time. In what was then termed a unique instrument, RPL was offering its potential shareholders a triple-option convertible debenture (TOCD) at Rs 60 each. This was to have a combination of equity shares and debentures. ���The issue was a big success and TOCD was a new concept,��� recalls Kisan R Choksey, chairman, KR Choksey Shares & Securities. The story, he adds, was the establishment of a world-class refinery, which could process any form of crude. While Mr Choksey did not recall how much he invested in that public issue, the shares that came his way are still with him. This was the first step in making Jamnagar the refining hub, and over time, additional capacity was put in place. In 2002, RPL was merged into RIL at a 1:11 ratio (RPL shareholders would get one RIL share for every 11 shares they held). ���It was not a favourable ratio,��� said Mr Choksey. Not all shareholders waited for the 2002 announcement. One of them was BS Prasad, a Chennai-based businessman, who invested Rs 15,000 when RPL went public in 1993. He sold his holding in 1999. ���I wanted to book profits and exited my investment,��� he says. Overall, he made a profit of around 40% on his original investment. Live Events Following the demerger of the Reliance empire in June 2005, the Mukesh Ambani-owned group hit the capital markets in April 2006 with a public offering from RPL. This was to create an export-oriented refinery at Jamnagar. The issue had a size of Rs 2,700 crore and like the one in 1993, the offer price was Rs 60 per unit. The difference was that the issue in 2006 was at a premium of Rs 50 without a debenture component attached to it.
