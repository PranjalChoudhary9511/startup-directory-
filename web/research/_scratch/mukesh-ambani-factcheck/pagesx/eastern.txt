The listing values Jio at around $137 billion (£102 billion), overtaking Bharti Airtel and HDFC Bank. It also lifted the combined value of the Reliance group to $320 billion (£239 billion), making it India’s most valuable business group ahead of the Tata Group. Jio shares closed at 1,417.5 rupees (about £11), near the day’s high, compared with an offer price of 1,119 rupees (about £8.70). They rose as much as 27.3 per cent during the session. “The IPO price offered a valuation entry point; the listing price reflects a much greater degree of optimism,” said Aishvarya Dadheech, founder of Fident Asset Management. An emotional day for the Ambanis The listing, Reliance’s first in two decades, follows a record $3.2 billion (£2.4 billion) IPO, India’s largest. Jio Awaits Clearance as Ambani Responds to Musk&#39;s Claims At the ceremony at the National Stock Exchange, all three of Ambani’s children gave speeches, while Ambani, Asia’s richest person according to Forbes, sat in the audience in tears. His eldest son, Akash, Jio’s managing director, touched his parents’ feet, a traditional gesture of respect, before walking on stage. Ambani has spent years planning his succession to avoid a repeat of the public feud with his younger brother, Anil, that followed the death of their father, Reliance founder Dhirubhai Ambani, in 2002. Dhirubhai took Reliance public in 1977 with an offering aimed at small investors, widely credited with helping build India’s share-owning culture. Reliance shares have returned about 900 per cent since 2006. From telecoms to ‘everything company’ The listing advances Ambani’s plan to turn Reliance from an oil and chemicals conglomerate into an “everything company” spanning consumer products and technology. Jio, backed by Meta and Google, has more than 530 million telecom customers, second only to China Mobile among single-country operators. It plans to use up to $2.89 billion (about £2.2 billion) of the proceeds to repay debt at its telecoms unit. Ambani&#39;s Jio Platforms begins IPO process Brokerages see room for further gains but flagged risks including competition, heavy capital spending and changes to India’s telecom rules. India’s IPO boom India has become the world’s busiest IPO market this year by number of deals, with 246 listings so far. Despite its size, the Jio offering paid its 19 banks only about $20.7 million (£15.4 million) in fees, 0.65 per cent of the offer, roughly half what Hyundai Motor India paid in 2024. The listing comes during the Hindu festive season leading up to Diwali, traditionally seen as an auspicious time for big purchases. In the first major dispute linked to the India-UK trade deal, which came into force in July, Indian authorities had told major liquor companies that bulk Scotch imports did not qualify for the agreed tariff cut from 150 per cent to 75 per cent. At a closed-door meeting on Monday, commerce ministry officials told industry executives that New Delhi would honour the deal in letter and spirit. “We are potentially moving towards a resolution. Bulk Scotch whisky imports are indeed part of concessions under the trade deal,” one government source said. Swinney Leads Scottish Exporters on India Trade Mission A second source said Commerce Secretary Rajesh Agrawal told attendees that steps were being taken “so that the duty concession on bulk Scotch whisky is not impaired”. Tax raids A resolution would be a relief for market leaders Diageo and Pernod Ricard, and for Indian distillers Radico Khaitan and Allied Blenders and Distillers. Indian tax officials searched the offices of these and other companies in recent days and questioned executives. The companies did not respond to Reuters’ requests for comment. Why bulk Scotch matters The dispute centres on customs classifications. Tax inspectors told companies the lower tariff should not apply to Scotch imported in bulk containers, while the industry argues that all Scotch, bottled or bulk, should qualify. India may rethink Scotch whisky tariff cuts over UK steel curbs Bulk imports are central to the business because they are blended with other spirits to make Indian-Made Foreign Liquor (IMFL), which includes many popular whisky brands in India. Scotch exports to India were worth $335 million (about £250 million) in 2024. The Scotch Whisky Association has forecast the trade deal could add $1.34 billion (about £1 billion) over five years. The UK government said it was working with India on the issue, and the Scottish government also raised it with New Delhi.
