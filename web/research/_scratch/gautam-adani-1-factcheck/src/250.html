Adani, Bloomberg discuss Dharavi redevelopment, affordable clean energy at Delhi meeting

The meeting took place during Bloomberg's visit to New Delhi for the New Economy Forum, with discussions also touching on governance, technology and public trust

Akansha Srivastava

New Delhi: Adani Group Chairman Gautam Adani met billionaire businessman and former New York City mayor Michael Bloomberg in New Delhi, where the two discussed India's energy transition, the Dharavi redevelopment project in Mumbai, urban infrastructure and the need to balance economic growth with environmental sustainability.

Bloomberg is in the national capital to attend the Bloomberg New Economy Forum, being held from October 13 to 15, bringing together global business leaders, policymakers and investors to discuss economic and technological challenges.

In a detailed LinkedIn post following the meeting, Adani shared his observations about Bloomberg's approach to development, saying their interaction highlighted the importance of combining large-scale infrastructure investments with transparent governance, reliable institutions and public trust.

Adani said the discussion reflected the differing priorities of emerging and developed economies in addressing climate change and accelerating the transition towards cleaner energy.

While developing countries such as India require reliable electricity, industrial capacity and digital infrastructure to support economic growth, developed markets often place greater emphasis on faster decarbonisation and regulatory alignment, he observed.

However, Adani said there was common ground on ensuring that the shift towards renewable energy remained affordable and accessible.

"A transition that only the wealthy can afford is not a transition. It is a luxury," Adani wrote.

He argued that decarbonisation policies should not make electricity unaffordable for ordinary households, disrupt manufacturing or slow economic development in countries belonging to the Global South.

At the same time, continuing to depend on conventional energy systems without addressing climate risks was not a sustainable option, he said.

According to Adani, the challenge was to align investment, government policies and implementation so that clean energy eventually became a more affordable and reliable alternative.

Dharavi redevelopment discussed

Another significant subject during the meeting was the redevelopment of Dharavi, one of Mumbai's most densely populated settlements, where an Adani-led project seeks to undertake large-scale urban regeneration.

Adani said the project should not be viewed merely as a real estate development but as an exercise in improving living conditions while protecting the livelihoods of existing residents.

Referring to Bloomberg's experience as New York City mayor, Adani highlighted the role of municipal data, administrative accountability and effective implementation in managing densely populated urban communities.

He said Dharavi's redevelopment would need to be assessed against improvements in housing, sanitation, electricity supply, climate resilience and the economic security of residents.

The project involves a complex urban ecosystem comprising small businesses, informal enterprises and families that have lived in the area for generations.

Adani suggested that successful redevelopment would require engineering expertise alongside community trust and effective local governance.

Infrastructure alone cannot deliver development: Adani

Reflecting on the broader discussion, Adani distinguished between physical infrastructure, such as ports, electricity transmission networks, green hydrogen facilities and data centres, and the institutional systems needed to make those investments effective.

He said infrastructure development could not deliver its intended benefits without coordination, access and accountable governance.

A port required efficient logistics, a solar power installation needed a dependable electricity grid, and advanced computing infrastructure would have limited public value if access remained restricted, he explained.

Adani also recalled Bloomberg's interest in emerging technologies, including the potential of ocean energy, saying the discussion ranged from energy systems to consumer technology and future innovation.

Referring to Bloomberg's address at the New Economy Forum, Adani said the American businessman had highlighted India's growing opportunities in artificial intelligence, advanced manufacturing and green infrastructure.

Bloomberg, founder of Bloomberg LP and Bloomberg Philanthropies, has maintained business operations in India for three decades and has supported philanthropic initiatives in the country.

Adani said long-term development required patient capital, efficient project execution, transparent governance and capable local leadership working together.
