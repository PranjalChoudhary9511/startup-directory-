( ABC News ) abc.net.au/news/adani-changes-name-to-barvus-mining-and-resources/12851592 Link copied Share Share article Mining company Adani announced it will change its name to Bravus Mining and Resources and says it is not because the brand has been compromised by the controversy surrounding its proposed Queensland mine. Key points: Adani has changed its name to Bravus Mining and Resources to reflect its changes after 10 years in Australia The company has been at the centre of controversy over its Carmichael Mine in central Queensland An advertising and marketing professor says companies can rebrand either because of a questionable reputation or because they are expanding The company's chief executive, David Boshof, said the shake-up of the business was due to how the business had changed in the 10 years since it started in Australia. "We are motivated by how we feel the organisation is transitioning and for what we represent and, as you know, the Adani mining brand has changed in the 10 years from when we started to where we are today," Mr Boshof said. The company has been at the centre of controversy over its Carmichael Mine in central Queensland, with some contractors previously cutting ties with the project amid growing pressure from environmental groups. Mr Boshof said if the company name-change was motivated by external factors it would have changed it 18 months or two years ago. "We've already delivered on the 1,500 jobs, and $1.5 billion in contracts have been committed to Queensland, so we thought it was a good time to give the organisation its own, unique Australian identity," he said. 'Bravus' was chosen because it was the Latin word for brave, Mr Boshof said. "We believe it's taken a lot of courage and bravery for us to, I guess, stand up for what we believe in and stand up for our community," he said. Mr Boshof said the Indian-owned Adani Group was the the overall holding company of a broad business that also included infrastructure and renewable branches in Australia, and mining was just one part. 'Controversial business' QUT advertising and marketing Professor Gary Mortimer said the rebrand was an interesting move. There were a number of reasons why brands and products changed their names and some of this was because of a questionable or bad reputation, Professor Mortimer said. Hundreds of anti-Adani protesters march over Victoria Bridge towards the ABC at South Bank during peak hour on June 21, 2019. ( ABC News: Kate McKenna ) "When you think of Adani, it is a controversial business," he said. "Yes, it's creating jobs and it's obviously involved in resources and energy, but when you hear the name Adani, certainly over the past 10 years, there is somewhat of a correlation between that and protesting. "The two tend to go together to a reasonable person." KFC was an example of another businesses that had done this, where it moved away from Kentucky Fried Chicken, Professor Mortimer said. "Think about cancel culture — red skins, chicos, coon cheese — all this year just announced they'll remove those brands because of the reputation behind the brands," he said. Professor Mortimer said name changes could also be due to international or domestic growth. For example, the Bank of NSW has been Westpac since 1982 because it wanted to grow outside of that state. The Stop Adani protest group said the rebrand would not change Adani Australia's legacy. In a statement, Mackay Conservation Group coordinator Peter McCallum said people would not be fooled. "They can't scrub away Adani's track record of breaking Queensland laws, endangering the reef, destroying black-throated finch habitat, and intimidating the community," Mr McCallum said. "Dirt like that sticks."
