 SEBI clears Adani Group, top executives in Hindenburg case
 Regulator finds no breach of RPT or PFUTP norms; loans via linked entities repaid with interest before probe By Akshata Gorde Updated - September 18, 2025 at 10:00 PM. &times; Share WhatsApp X Facebook LinkedIn Messenger Reddit Mail https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/news/sebi-clears-adani-group-top-executives-in-hindenburg-case/article70066512.ece Copy 
 FILE PHOTO: The logo of the Adani Group is seen on the facade of its Corporate House on the outskirts of Ahmedabad
 | Photo Credit:
 AMIT DAVE
 The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) has given the Adani Group, its top executives a clean chit on charges levelled by US-based Hindenburg Research, dismissing allegations that the conglomerate routed funds through three entities to conceal related party transactions and manipulate its stocks. The regulator found no violations by Gautam Adani or group companies, including Adani Ports , Adani Enterprises and Adani Power . At the time such transactions with unrelated parties did not qualify as related party dealings, hence no penalty has been imposed, SEBI said in two separate orders. In its order, SEBI said that it found no evidence of fraud, market manipulation, funds diversion or any investor harm. “Having considered the matter holistically, I find that the allegations made against Noticees in the SCN are not established…I, hereby dispose of the instant proceedings against noticees without any direction,” Kamlesh C. Varshney, SEBI whole-time member, said on Thursday. 
 Promoter Gautam Adani, Rajesh Adani and CFO Jugeshinder Singh were also cleared 
 Promoter Gautam Adani, Rajesh Adani and CFO Jugeshinder Singh were also cleared, and the regulator said there was no evidence of deliberate concealment or fraud. Following the order, Gautam Adani, Founder and Chairman of Adani Group, said on X, “After an exhaustive investigation, SEBI has reaffirmed what we have always maintained, that the Hindenburg claims were baseless. Transparency and integrity have always defined the Adani Group.” He added that he felt the pain of the investors who had lost money “because of this fraudulent and motivated report.” 
 Also read: Adani-Hindenburg case: Timeline of events Hindenburg allegations The probe was triggered by Hindenburg Research’s January 2023 report, which alleged that Adani group companies had used Adicorp Enterprises, Milestone Tradelinks and Rehvar Infrastructure as conduits to channel funds to Adani Power and Adani Enterprises. SEBI had issued a show cause notice alleging violations of disclosure rules under the Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements (LODR), the Prohibition of Fraudulent and Unfair Trade Practices (PFUTP) regulations and directors’ duties. The investigation concluded that the loans were repaid with interest, so no funds were siphoned off. “The SCN erroneously applies the concepts introduced by way of amended regulation retrospectively to the investigation period, which is not legally permissible,” the order said. The widened definition covering indirect transactions came into effect only from April 1, 2022. SEBI’s investigations span across financial years 2012–13 to 2020–21 in the Adicorp Enterprises matter and FY18–19 to FY22–23 in the Milestone Tradelinks–Rehvar Infrastructure matter. No fraud SEBI also rejected the charge that Adani engaged in a scheme to conceal related party dealings. “On merit too, it is held that impugned transactions cannot be classified as manipulative or fraudulent transactions or unfair trade practice since: (i) there is no allegation of siphoning off of money or diversion of fund; (ii) all the money has come back with interest before the start of the investigation; and (iii) the impugned transactions have not been held as related party transactions,” the order said. The disposal of the case, clearing all of wrongdoing, closes one of the most high-profile regulatory cases facing the conglomerate, which lost billions in market value after Hindenburg’s allegations in early 2023. The Supreme Court had asked SEBI to investigate the claims and an expert committee reported no regulatory failure, though it called for a need for stronger disclosure norms.
