ET Online Last Updated: Aug 05, 2024, 02:32:00 PM IST Rate Story Follow us Share Font Size Abc Small Abc Medium Abc Large Save Print Comment Synopsis Gautam Adani has started planning for his succession, set to take place in the early 2030s. His sons and nephews, who currently manage various business segments, will take over in a collaborative leadership structure. This succession plan is vital, given Adani Group's significant impact on India's economy. Agencies When Gautam Adani , Asia's richest man who presides over the vast Adani business empire, turned 60 in 2022, his son Karan Adani, the CEO of Adani Ports and SEZ, penned an emotional note for him. "Wish you a very happy birthday dad!! You amaze us every year with your energy and passion. Every experience with you is a new learning for me. You are the foundation that keeps our family together. Love you loads!!" Karan wrote on X. Gautam, a school dropout who built one of India's biggest industrial and business conglomerate from scratch, is indeed the driving force at the Adani Group where he shares work with his two brothers, two sons and two nephews — a usual thing at Indian business houses which often involve extended family. But unlike several other Indian business patriarchs before him, Gautam is aware of the risk the family can pose to the business in future. Now well-known for managing risk at his business, especially after the attack by American short-seller Hindenburg Research last year from which the group has now rebounded, Gautam is planning succession almost a decade in advance , Bloomberg has reported. He plans to retire in early 2030s and pass on the baton to his heirs under a succession plan which is already in the works. Also Read: Adani succession plan: The four heirs to Adani's $213 billion empire The deal in the works In early 2018, Gautam Adani invited his sons and nephews to his home in Ahmedabad, as per a Bloomberg report. Over lunch, the patriarch asked the four younger men (his sons Karan (37) and Jeet (26) and nephews Pranav (45) and Sagar(30)) an unexpected question: Did they want to carve up the Adani Group’s sprawling businesses between themselves and part ways? He gave them three months to decide. That family confab kicked off what’s poised to become one of the world’s largest and most challenging transfers of wealth. When Gautam’s sons and nephews came back to the patriarch, they told him they wanted to run the conglomerate together as a family, even after Gautam’s departure. Live Events You Might Also Like: Adani Enterprises to demerge food FMCG biz, integrate it with Adani Wilmar That answer set in motion a series of moves, many of which have escaped public attention, such as the creation of a four-way leadership structure that splits management of the businesses but requires the heirs to work together, Bloomberg has reported. Their areas of responsibility do not neatly align with specific units or listed entities. Even common services across the group — such as human resources, finance and IT support — have been carved up among the four. When the founder does step back, there will be no first among equals and the joint decision-making will continue, the Adani children told Bloomberg in separate interviews — even in the event of a crisis or a major strategic call. Many think a lack of formal hierarchy can be challenging for joint decision-making. Why Adani succession will be keenly watched It is not just investors who would track the Adani succession. India will be keenly watching it because the country's fortunes are now closely tied to the Adani group, with a total market capitalization of $213 billion across 10 listed units alone. Much like legacy conglomerates such as Tata and Ambani, Adani is present in a very wide variety of segments, from edible oil to airports to ports to airports to finance to cement to energy, including renewables. Adani's bold bet on government policies has meant that it has emerged as a mega infrastructure player and will continue to play a critical role in the expansion of the country's key infrastructure. Adani is India’s biggest importer of coal, its largest owner of solar farms and its second-biggest manufacturer of cement. Adani-owned ports carry almost half the country’s shipping containers, while more than 90 million people use its airports each year. The Adani energy business, which spans from coal to solar power, will see investment of at least $100 billion in capital expenditure over the next decade, which will make him a key player in India's energy transition. You Might Also Like: Billionaire battle heats up as Adani challenges Birla for cement dominance In a decade, Adani will only further cement its position as a national champion, especially with its global play in which it is buying key ports in other countries which give a strategic edge to India when it is trying to emerge as a global power. Adani's ports in foreign countries are seen as a challenge to China's interests. Adani has also entered defence manufacturing as India strives to become self-reliant in manufacturing of defence items. Will it be a smooth succession? Like Reliance Industries chairman Mukesh Ambani , Adani too has groomed his sons and nephews for years who are now grown to be nearly independent. The eldest, Pranav, heads agri and oil & gas segments. Since 1999, he has propelled Adani Wilmar forward. Its Fortune food brand now holds over 20% market share in India's edible oil segment. He also facilitated the group's entry into the agri sector with initiatives like Adani Agri Logistics and Adani Agri Fresh. Pranav made Adani Gas India’s largest private sector listed city gas distribution company and forged collaborations with Indian Oil Corporation and Total, a French energy major. Karan handles Adani Ports and SEZ Limited (APSEZ) and also oversees cement and logistics businesses. Sagar manages the group's energy business and finance. He is credited with establishing Adani Green Energy's entire solar and wind portfolio. Jeet is responsible for Adani's airport network, India's largest private network, and also oversees digital businesses. You Might Also Like: How Adani bull Rajiv Jain's GQG reshuffled 13-stock portfolio in election season By the time Gautam Adani retires, all the four Adani scions will be quite sharp and hardened managers and entrepreneurs after steering the fast expansion of the group in different segments. Thus, the key-man risk is low. This risk stems from the departure of the key man who had all the knowledge, skills and important relationships in his own control. Gautam is a strict boss even with his sons and nephews, people familiar with the matter have told Bloomberg, which ensures the four young ones learn to take responsibility as well as risk. In 2022, Gautam took Karan to task in front of other executives over an unexpected $11 million quarterly loss at ACC Ltd. and demanded that he spend more time on the cement business, officials who were present said. Earlier this year he pulled up Jeet in a meeting for the slow pace of the group’s digital plans, officials told Bloomberg. However, the problem Indian business houses typically face is not of weak successors but of family disputes disrupting the business. The falling apart of Mukesh and Anil Ambani after their father Dhirubhai Ambani's death and a messy long-drawn division of business that followed is an example of family risk. Wiser with this experience, Mukesh Ambani has deftly planned the succession for his three children. The 130-year-old Kirloskar group finalised a deed of family settlement (DFS) in 2009. Through this, the Kirloskar family had agreed to distribute various assets in the form of shares of group companies and cash held in trust and investment companies among the signatories of DFS to avoid a potential dispute within the family. However, the settlement has been a subject of complex legal challenges by many family members for a long time. But recently, the Godrej group announced a settlement involving close to a dozen family members quite smoothly. “Succession is very, very important for the business sustainability,” Gautam Adani has told Bloomberg. “I left the choice to the second generation as the transition must be organic, gradual and very systematic.” Karan told Bloomberg that company structures have been simplified over the last 15 years to avoid the “criss-cross holdings” found in other Indian conglomerates. The changes have been disclosed in public filings and the family now holds its stakes in each business directly, he said. “Having no clear leader among the four who can take key decisions could turn out to be a drawback,” Raphael Amit, Professor of Management at the Wharton School, has told Bloomberg. “For this to work there need to be clearly laid-out processes for conflict resolution and well-defined roles.” Will the Adani scions be able to manage without a formal hierarchy? Pranav Adani, the eldest among the four scions, doesn't see any difficulties with collective decision-making. “Though each of us are looking at different businesses, we are like one team,” Pranav told Bloomberg. “The family members who are in headquarters have lunch together each day, where day-to-day issues are discussed.” With the benefit of hindsight and the recent example of Mukesh Ambani planning succession for his three children, Gautam is expected to be watchful of any flaws that might grow as challenges after he leaves the group.
