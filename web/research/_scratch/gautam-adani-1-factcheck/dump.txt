GL2 Education: Gave up formal education at 16, in 1978. A Mumbai college, Jai Hind, rejected his application around then. In 2023 he said he regrets not finishing college. [5,6]
GL3 First job: Learning to sort diamonds at Mahendra Brothers in Mumbai, at 16. A cousin got him in. [4,5]

GL7 Family: Married to Dr Priti Adani, who founded and chairs the Adani Foundation. Two sons work in the group: Karan runs Adani Ports as Managing Director, and Jeet is a Director at Adani Airports. [0,27,28,29,30]

GL9 Known for: Mundra Port, eight airports and a big green-energy bet. ## C0 origins | Ages 0–12 | A Trader's Son from the Dry North
0.0 p: Gautam Adani was born on ==24 June 1962== in Ahmedabad, the biggest city in Gujarat. He was born into a Gujarati Jain family with many children. Decades later, his group would describe him as India's first first-generation founder to lead a business group past ==$200 billion== in market value. But when he looks back at the beginning, he does not talk about comfort. He talks about tough days. [2,3,1,40]
0.1 p: His father was Shantilal Adani, born around 1922. We know the year because in June 2022 the family marked his 100th birth anniversary in a very public way. They pledged ==₹60,000 crore== to health, education and skills work in India, timed to the father's centenary and to Gautam's own 60th birthday. In his statement, Gautam called him "my inspiring father." [2,47]
0.2 note: A ₹60,000 crore gift timed to a father's birthday. Family runs deep here.
0.3 p: So what did Shantilal do for a living? Forbes says Gautam later walked away from his father's textile shop to start his own trading firm. Gautam himself describes his father as a trader in something bankers today give a fancy name: forward trades. He has told this story in public more than once, and it is the first business lesson of his life. [0,40,45]
0.4 p: Here is the important part. Outlook Business, retelling one of Adani's speeches, notes that his father traded at a time when no legal contracts or paperwork backed the deals. There were no lawyers and no stamp paper. The only thing holding a deal together was a man's word. [45,40]
0.5 quote: "They were just oral commitments between two parties that trusted each other." -- Gautam Adani, describing his father's trades (quoted by Outlook Business, 2015) [45]
0.6 p: He went on to say he never saw those commitments fail. Those deals, he said, were his first lessons on trust and commitment. In January 2023 he repeated the story to students in Palanpur, saying those spoken promises "were my initial lessons on the power of trust." __The first thing a future billionaire learned about business was not a number. It was that your word is your collateral.__ [45,40]
0.7 example: Picture a forward trade in its simplest form. One trader promises today to deliver goods next month at a price fixed today. The other promises to pay. In Shantilal's world, as his son describes it, nothing was written down, so a man who broke his word once could not easily trade again. [45,40]
0.8 h: A childhood in the dry north
0.9 p: Most short biographies stop at "born in Ahmedabad." Adani's own telling adds a place most people skip. He has said he spent the first days of his childhood in Banaskantha, a district in Gujarat's dry north. Its main town is Palanpur, which is where he gave that 2023 speech. [40,44,52]
0.10 p: Banaskantha is big and harsh. It is Gujarat's second-largest district, about ==12,703 square kilometres==, bordering Rajasthan to the north and Kutch to the west. At its far edge the land runs into desert along the border with Pakistan. The district is named after the Banas river, which flows down from the Aravalli hills toward the Rann of Kutch. [52]
0.11 p: Adani says the dry, tough living conditions there shaped how he deals with people. He grew up in a joint family, a large household of relatives living together, and he says that is where he "learnt various social skills." Some of his relatives studied at Vidyamandir, the Palanpur school whose 75th birthday he came to celebrate in 2023. [40,44]
0.12 quote: "As I look back, I have no agitations in saying that the tough early days I spent here with my parents and being a part of a society that stood by each other, shaped my early beliefs." -- Gautam Adani, Palanpur, January 2023 [40]
0.13 note: He credits a hard place, not a rich one. Remember that.
0.14 p: On the same day, he posted a line on X about the school and the region. Study International quoted it: "It is only those that row on the sands that are best prepared to reach the oceans!" It is a neat image for a boy from the dry north who would one day build Mundra, which he calls India's largest port. [3,42]
0.15 p: His mother appears less often in the record. In an August 2026 address, Adani credited her quiet example of sacrifice and compassion as something he still follows. He paired it with his father's rule that a promise must always be kept. Reports differ on her exact name, so we leave it out rather than guess. [36]
0.16 h: A big family, and two brothers who matter
0.17 p: Exactly how many children the Adanis had depends on which profile you read, and we have logged the conflict in our fact-check notes. What is solid is that two elder brothers shaped Gautam's early path. One was Mahasukhbhai, who would later call him home to run a factory. The other was Vinod. [5,48,3]
0.18 p: Vinod was roughly 13 years older; Scroll reported in 2023 that he was believed to be 74. He studied at Jai Hind College in Mumbai, and in ==1976== he set up a textile mill near the city. That meant that, by the time Gautam was a teenager, the family already had a foothold in Mumbai. [48,6]
0.19 p: Vinod moved to Singapore in 1989 and to Dubai in 1994, trading sugar, oil and copper. In 2023, Scroll called him the mysterious elder brother at the heart of the Adani crisis, after short-seller Hindenburg Research questioned the group. That story, and the group's response, belongs to a later chapter. For now he is simply the big brother who went to Mumbai first. [48]
0.20 note: Family is the first network. Watch who opens each door in this story.
0.21 h: The port that stayed in his head
0.22 p: There is one childhood scene the Adani Group itself puts on its official timeline. Its 1995 entry, about building the port at Mundra, is titled "Turning Childhood Dream into Reality." It says plainly: "Inspired by Kandla port, Mr. Adani transformed Mundra into a leading port." [7]
0.23 p: Kandla is the big government port on Gujarat's Kutch coast, run today by the Deendayal Port Authority. Retellings say young Gautam saw it on a visit while he was still a student. They disagree about how old he was, and the group's timeline gives no age, so we do not pin one down. [57,3,7]

The Adani Group says this port inspired the boy who later built Mundra.","author":"Deendayal Port Authority, Kandla","license":"GODL-India","year":2022}
0.25 p: Do the maths. If the picture formed in his school years, it took about two decades to act on it. Mundra's first jetty came in ==1995==, when he was about 33. __A picture you carry at fourteen can become a plan at thirty-three, but only if you keep carrying it.__ [7,45]
0.26 note: One school visit. One picture in his head. Watch where it leads.
0.27 h: To the city for school
0.28 p: At about 12, the boy moved to the big city. Adani has said he came to Ahmedabad and spent four years finishing his secondary education, ending when he was 16. Counting back from his birthday, that puts the move at around ==1974==. Profiles name his school as Sheth Chimanlal Nagindas Vidyalaya. [40,5,3]
0.29 photo: {"url":"https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8c/Pol_House_of_Ahmedabad_01.jpg","page":"https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pol_House_of_Ahmedabad_01.jpg","caption":"A pol house in old Ahmedabad (2026). Adani finished school in this city in the 1970s.","author":"Riyas16","license":"CC BY 4.0","year":2026}
0.30 p: 1974 is also the year a friendship began. Malay Mahadevia, who later helped build the Mundra project, told Outlook Business he had known Adani "since 1974." Mahadevia came from a family of doctors and went on to study dentistry. Two decades later, Adani would hand this friend a job that had nothing to do with teeth. [45]
0.31 p: Be honest about the gaps. Adani almost never describes himself at five or at ten. A 2022 biography by journalist R.N. Bhaskar, published by Penguin, says it covers his early childhood. But we could not check its details against other sources, so you will not find them here. [54]
0.32 note: No invented childhood scenes. If it isn't sourced, it isn't here.
0.33 timeline: c. 1922 = Father Shantilal Adani is born; his centenary is marked in 2022. [2,47] || c. 1949 = Elder brother Vinod is born (reported to be 74 in 2023). [48] || 1960 = Mahendra Brothers, the diamond firm that will give Gautam his first job, is set up in Mumbai. [50] || 24 Jun 1962 = Gautam Adani is born in Ahmedabad, into a Gujarati Jain family. 0.34 lesson: Your first business school is the table you grew up at :: Adani didn't learn trust from a textbook. He watched his father close deals on a spoken promise and saw that the promises held. Look at the adults around you. Whatever they do every day, good or bad, is already teaching you how business works. Choose on purpose which lessons you keep.

## C1 growing-up | Ages 12–16 | Restless at Sixteen
1.0 p: From about 12 to 16, Gautam Adani was a schoolboy in Ahmedabad. He spent four years there finishing secondary school. The family business was cloth and trading, and the obvious future was to join it. Instead, by 16, he had decided to leave. [40,5,0]
1.1 p: What filled those four years? Here the public record is thin. Adani has not described his friends, his marks, his favourite books or his cricket heroes in any interview we could verify, and we will not make them up. What he does describe, again and again, is a feeling. [40,42]
1.2 quote: "The optimism and desire for independence of a teenage boy are hard to contain." -- Gautam Adani, Palanpur, January 2023 [40]
1.3 p: In the same speech he boiled his teenage plan down to one sentence: "All I knew was that I wanted to do something different, and do it on my own." Notice the last three words. The safe road was his father's textile business, and Forbes notes that he left it to build his own trading firm. [40,0]
1.4 note: The safe job was right there. He said no. That's the story.
1.5 h: Lessons at the dinner table
1.6 p: His teenage education did not happen only at school. At home, he watched his father run trades on spoken promises. Adani says those trades were his first lessons in trust, and that the way people in his community stood by each other in hard times shaped his beliefs. [40,45]
1.7 quote: "Over time, these beliefs became my values and today these values of courage and trust and commitment have driven every action that the Adani Group takes as a company." -- Gautam Adani, Palanpur, January 2023 [40]
1.8 p: This is how Adani explains himself, so read it with care. A founder's story about his own values is not proof that he always lived up to them. Later chapters cover the controversies and legal cases around his group. But it tells you what he believes his foundation is, and he says it was laid before he was 16. [40,48]
1.9 note: A founder's own story is evidence, not proof. Keep both in mind.
1.10 h: A friend from 1974
1.11 p: One person from his teenage years stays in the story. Malay Mahadevia had known Adani since 1974. He came from a family of doctors and had just finished a dentistry degree in Mumbai when, in ==September 1992==, he went to Adani for help in starting a dental college. [45]
1.12 p: Adani, by then running a ₹300-crore trading business, did something odd. He offered his dentist friend a job running a salt works and jetty project in Kutch. Mahadevia joined in ==October 1992== and kept practising dentistry for about 12 years on the side. [45]
1.13 quote: "I pointed out that my background was in complete variance to the role he was offering me" -- Malay Mahadevia, to Outlook Business, 2015 [45]
1.14 note: He hired trust first, skills second. Risky, but it's his pattern.
1.15 h: The college that said no
1.16 p: Around ==1977 or 1978==, the teenager applied to Jai Hind College in Mumbai. He picked it for a simple reason: his elder brother Vinod had studied there. The college turned him down. [42,6,43]
1.17 quote: "Fortunately or unfortunately, the college did not accept him and he went on to work full time" -- Vikram Nankani, president, Jai Hind College Alumni Association, September 2024 [42]
1.18 p: Now jump ahead about 46 years. On Teachers' Day in ==September 2024==, the same college invited Adani, then 62, to give a lecture to its students. Its title was "Breaking Boundaries: The Power of Passion and Unconventional Paths to Success." Introducing him, Nankani told the hall the college had rejected him, then declared him a "deemed alumni" because he had at least applied. [42,6,43]
1.19 facts: 1977–78 = Applied to Jai Hind College, Mumbai, and was rejected [42,6] || 16 = His age when he gave up formal education [41,5] || 2024 = Same college invites him to give its Teachers' Day lecture [42,6] || 62 = His age when he gave that lecture [42]
1.20 note: Rejected as a teenager. Invited back at 62 to teach. Keep going.
1.21 h: School dropout or college dropout?
1.22 p: You will see two versions of what happened next. Some profiles say he enrolled in a commerce degree at Gujarat University and left partway through. Adani's own words are simpler: "I was just 16 years old when I chose to give up my education and move to Mumbai." [3,41]
1.23 p: Press Trust of India, reporting his 2023 speech, says he left formal education at 16 in ==1978==. We go with his own account and list the other version in our fact-check notes. Either way, the point is the same: he chose work over a degree while still a teenager. [5,41]
1.24 p: Here is the twist. Forty-five years later, he told students he wished he had stayed. "I now believe that I would have benefitted If I had finished college," he said in Palanpur. Experience, he said, had made him wise, but he now realised that formal education rapidly expands what you know. [40,5]
1.25 quote: "To acquire wisdom, one must experience but to acquire knowledge, one must study." -- Gautam Adani, January 2023 [41]
1.26 note: A dropout telling students to finish college. Take that seriously.
1.27 h: The ticket
1.28 p: It is ==1978==. Gautam turns 16 in June. He walks into the Ahmedabad railway station, buys a ticket and boards the Gujarat Mail, the train to Mumbai. By his own account, he has very little money on him. [40,5]
1.29 quote: "At sixteen years of age, purchasing a train ticket at the Ahmedabad railway station and boarding the Gujarat Mail to Mumbai with nothing much in my pocket." -- Gautam Adani, Palanpur, January 2023 [40]
1.30 photo: {"url":"https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4a/Ahmedabad_Railway_Station.jpg","page":"https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ahmedabad_Railway_Station.jpg","caption":"Ahmedabad's main railway station (Kalupur), photographed in 2010. In 1978 a 16-year-old Adani bought his ticket to Mumbai here.","author":"Hardik jadeja","license":"CC BY 3.0","year":2010}
1.31 p: Why go? At Jai Hind College in 2024, he said people still ask him: "Why did you move to Mumbai? Why did you not complete your education?" His answer was that he wanted to test himself. "I was driven by a need to see if I had the courage to make a life for myself in the most happening city of our country," he said. [42]
1.32 quote: "The answer lies in the heart of every young dreamer who sees boundaries not as barriers but as challenges that test his courage." -- Gautam Adani, Jai Hind College, Mumbai, September 2024 [42]
1.33 p: It was brave, but it was not a jump into empty space. Vinod had run a textile mill near Mumbai since 1976, and Study International, summarising a Forbes account, says Gautam first stayed with him. A cousin would soon find him his first job. __The boldest-sounding moves often have a quiet safety net underneath. Find yours before you jump.__ [48,3,5]
1.34 note: Brave, yes. But he jumped with a family safety net. Both true.
1.35 p: So what did the 16-year-old get right, and what did it cost him? He went to the city where trade actually happened, instead of waiting for it to come to him. The price, by his own later admission, was the education he never finished. [42,40]
1.36 timeline: c. 1974 = Starts four years of secondary school in Ahmedabad. [40,5] || 1977–78 = Applies to Jai Hind College in Mumbai, where Vinod studied, and is rejected. [42,6] || Jun 1978 = Turns 16. [2] || 1978 = Leaves formal education and takes the Gujarat Mail to Mumbai. [5,40] || Jan 2023 = Tells students in Palanpur he regrets not finishing college. [5,40] || Sep 2024 = Gives the Teachers' Day lecture at the college that rejected him. [42,6]
1.37 lesson: Go where the market is, but count the cost :: Adani's big teenage move was to put himself in the city where deals were done. That paid off. But he also says that skipping college slowed his learning. You don't have to choose between the two the way he did. Get close to real customers early, and keep studying too.

## C2 first-steps | Ages 16–26 | Diamonds, Plastic and a Door Called 1985
2.0 p: Mumbai, ==1978==. The 16-year-old from Ahmedabad needs work. His cousin, Prakashbhai Desai, gets him a place at Mahendra Brothers to learn diamond assorting. The Adani Group's own official timeline begins its story right here: "Mr. Gautam Adani began his career as a diamond sorter in 1978." [5,7]
2.1 photo: {"url":"https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/39/12901_Gujarat_Mail_at_Mumbai_Central.jpg","page":"https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:12901_Gujarat_Mail_at_Mumbai_Central.jpg","caption":"The Gujarat Mail at Mumbai Central, 2012. Adani rode this train to the city at 16.","author":"Superfast1111","license":"CC BY-SA 3.0","year":2012}
2.2 p: Mahendra Brothers was no small shop. The firm was set up in ==1960== and traces its roots to a diamond business founded in 1940 by Bagmalbhai Parikh. Today it is based in Mumbai's Bharat Diamond Bourse. The Parikh family's charity, started in 1985, began in Palanpur, the same Banaskantha town where Adani spent his earliest years. [50,52]
2.3 p: Palanpur has a long link to diamonds. Economic historian Chinmay Tumbe notes that many Indian diamond traders in early twentieth-century Paris were Palanpuri Jains. [51,50]
2.4 note: Banaskantha again. His first employer had roots near his own.
2.5 p: Adani told the diamond industry in 2024 that Mahendra Brothers was where he "learned the art of diamond assorting", in Business Today's summary. It was the bottom of the ladder, but it put him inside a trading business, next to the people who bought and sold. [46,5]
2.6 p: How long did he sort? His two own accounts differ. In 2023 he said he spent about three years in the diamond unit. A 2024 report of his Jai Hind lecture says nearly two years. We note both. [5,40,42]
2.7 h: Zaveri Bazaar and the Japanese buyer
2.8 p: Then he left the job and set up his own diamond brokerage in Zaveri Bazaar, Mumbai's old jewellery market. A broker doesn't own the stones. He finds a buyer, matches him with a seller, and takes a cut. In other words, he was still a teenager and already selling trust. [5,40]
2.9 quote: "I still recall the day I did my first trade with a Japanese buyer. I earned a commission of 10,000" -- Gautam Adani, Palanpur, January 2023 (the amount was in rupees) [40,5]
2.10 p: Press Trust of India dates that first commission to about three years after he left school in 1978, so roughly ==1981==. A 2025 report puts his age at 19 when he earned it. That is all consistent with a June 1962 birthday. [5,55]
2.11 example: How big was ₹10,000 then? World Bank data puts India's GDP per person in 1981 at about ₹2,455. So one commission was roughly four times what the economy produced per Indian in a whole year. [53,40,5]
2.12 note: One deal. Four times India's per-person output. He was about 19.
2.13 p: What did trading teach him? At the 2024 India Gem & Jewellery Awards, he said it gave him the courage to take risks and trust his instincts. Business Today reported his line that "trading, much like entrepreneurship, doesn't come with safety nets." [46]
2.14 quote: "The field of trading makes a good teacher." -- Gautam Adani, Jai Hind College, September 2024 [42,43]
2.15 p: He drew one more rule from those Mumbai years. "I learnt very early that an entrepreneur can never be frozen by over-evaluating the choices in front of him," he told the Jai Hind students. __Speed of decision is a skill you can practise, and trading forced him to practise it daily.__ [42]
2.16 h: The call back home
2.17 p: Just as the brokerage was starting to pay, family called. When Gautam was ==19==, his elder brother Mahasukhbhai asked him to come back to Ahmedabad and help run a small PVC film factory the family had acquired. PVC film is thin plastic sheet, used for things like packaging. [5,6]
2.18 p: Outlook Business dates his return to ==1982== and describes a small PVC film plant bought by his elder brother. The 2024 Jai Hind reports call it a packaging factory managed by one of his brothers. Gautam went back. The diamond broker was now a factory manager. [45,6,42]
2.19 quote: "We used to procure imported raw materials. It was a tough business." -- Gautam Adani, on his brother's PVC factory (PTI, January 2023) [5]
2.20 p: Hold on to that word: imported. The factory's raw material came from abroad, and getting it was hard. Adani was now on the buying side of a problem that small factories across India shared. Within a few years, he would turn that problem into his business. [5,6]
2.21 note: His own supply headache became his first real business. Read twice.
2.22 h: 1985: the door opens
2.23 p: In ==1985==, Rajiv Gandhi's government loosened India's import rules. Adani calls this his first real break. On India TV's Aap Ki Adalat in January 2023, asked about the help he got from Narendra Modi, he answered with a list. [5,49]
2.24 quote: "I got three big breaks in my life." -- Gautam Adani, Aap Ki Adalat, India TV, January 2023 [49]
2.25 p: The first, he said, was in 1985 under Rajiv Gandhi, when the government brought in its new Exim (export-import) policy. He said it "allowed our company to become a global trading house." The other two breaks, the 1991 reforms and his Gujarat years, belong to later chapters. [49]
2.26 p: He moved fast. In 1985 he set up a trading business importing polymers, the raw plastic granules factories melt and shape, for small-scale industries that were struggling to get them. "While I had no trading experience, I took advantage of the opportunity," he said in 2023. [5,45,6]
2.27 p: It worked. "By the time I turned 23, my trading venture was doing well," he told the Jai Hind students. Twenty-three means about 1985 or 1986, only seven or eight years after he stepped off the Gujarat Mail with almost nothing. [42]
2.28 facts: 1978 = Starts work as a diamond sorter in Mumbai [7,5] || ₹10,000 = First commission, from a Japanese buyer [40] || 19 = Age when his brother calls him home to the PVC factory [5] || 23 = Age by which his trading venture "was doing well" [42]
2.29 note: Policy changed on paper. He was ready to act within months. Read twice.
2.30 h: 1988: a name on the door
2.31 p: In ==1988==, at 26, Gautam Adani founded Adani Exports in Ahmedabad, a commodity trading firm. The group's timeline calls it the start of everything that followed. Forbes describes the same moment as the commodities trading firm that grew into the Adani Group. [7,0]
2.32 p: Adani Exports later became Adani Enterprises. On ==1 November 1994== it listed on the BSE and NSE, and its IPO, priced at ₹150 a share, was oversubscribed 25 times. That story, and the port at Mundra that came next, is where the next part of this book begins. [7]
2.33 h: The people who opened doors
2.34 p: Count the hands that helped. His father gave him the idea that a promise is a contract. His brother Vinod had gone to Mumbai first, and his cousin Prakashbhai Desai got him the diamond job. His brother Mahasukhbhai brought him home to the factory that showed him the import problem. And his friend Malay Mahadevia, known since 1974, would later help him build Mundra. [40,48,5,45]
2.35 p: So what did young Adani do right? He went where the trading was, learned the business from the bottom, turned his own supply headache into a product, and acted fast when the 1985 rules changed. And what would he change? By his own account, he would have finished college. [5,42,40]
2.36 p: A fair reading adds one more thing. Few 16-year-olds have a brother with a mill near Mumbai and a cousin who can get them into a respected diamond house. Adani's courage was real. So was his family network. __Most "self-made" stories are really self-made plus well-connected, and it helps to see both clearly.__ [48,5,0]
2.37 note: Courage and connections. Ask honestly which of the two you have.
2.38 p: In an August 2026 address, as summarised by an Adani Group release, he said that leaving home as a teenager taught him lessons no classroom could. IANS reported him tying that early Mumbai life to his belief that trust has to be earned, not inherited. [36,56]
2.39 timeline: 1978 = Arrives in Mumbai; a cousin places him at Mahendra Brothers as a diamond sorter. [5,7] || c. 1981 = Starts his own diamond brokerage at Zaveri Bazaar; earns ₹10,000 on his first trade, with a Japanese buyer. [5,40] || 1981–82 = At 19, returns to Ahmedabad to run his brother's small PVC film factory. [5,45] || 1985 = Import rules loosen under Rajiv Gandhi; he starts importing polymers for small industries. [5,49,45] || c. 1985–86 = Turns 23; says his trading venture is "doing well." 2.40 lesson: Your pain point might be your product :: Adani's real business didn't come from a brainstorm. It came from running a factory that struggled to get imported raw material. When the rules changed in 1985, he already knew exactly who needed that material and why. Write down the problems that annoy you at work today. One of them could be your company.

## C3 every-company | Every venture, win or lose | Every Company He Built, Bought or Buried
3.0 p: Most billionaire stories hide the failures. This chapter does not. Below is every company, major project and abandoned plan of Gautam Adani's that we could trace to a reliable source, in rough order of when it began. Two money words will keep coming up: a lakh is 100,000 and a crore is 10 million, so ₹100 crore is ₹1 billion. [7,58]
3.1 p: Read them as one chain, not a list. A trader needed a jetty, the jetty needed cargo, the cargo was coal, the coal needed a power plant, and the power plant needed wires. The group's own timeline calls this the ==power of adjacencies==, meaning each new business grows out of the one next to it. __Watch how rarely he starts from zero.__ [7,59]
3.2 h: The trading years
3.3 venture: Diamond brokerage, Zaveri Bazaar (c. 1981–1982) shut IDEA:Match diamond buyers with sellers in Mumbai's old jewellery market and keep a commission. A broker sells trust and speed, not stones. DECISION:After about three years as a sorter at Mahendra Brothers, he chose to work for himself rather than climb inside someone else's firm. He later said he had wanted to do something on his own. STARTED:A one-man brokerage in Zaveri Bazaar. His first recalled trade was with a Japanese buyer, and it earned him a ₹10,000 commission. FUNDING:No figure for starting money has been published. A broker holds no stock, so the business needed very little capital. HAPPENED:He left in 1982 when his elder brother asked him to return to Gujarat. The early-life chapters of this book tell that story in full. LESSON:Pick a first business that needs almost no capital. It lets you learn customers at the price of your time alone. [45,5,40]
3.4 venture: Family PVC film factory, Ahmedabad (c. 1982–c. 1985) pivoted IDEA:A small plant making PVC film, a thin plastic sheet, owned by his elder brother. DECISION:He gave up a growing brokerage to help the family when the unit hit trouble. Business Standard says that trouble was an acute shortage of raw material. STARTED:He ran the plant as a young manager. Faced with the shortage, he got a government trading house to import the raw material the factory could not find. FUNDING:Bought by his brother. The price has not been made public. HAPPENED:The factory itself never became big. What mattered was the lesson that small Indian factories could not get imported plastic, and that lesson became his next business. LESSON:Your worst supply headache may be other people's headache too. That is a market. [45,60,5]
3.5 venture: Polymer import trading (1985–1988) merged IDEA:Import polymers, the plastic granules factories melt and shape, and sell them to small-scale industries that could not get supplies. DECISION:India's 1985 trade policy loosened import rules. He moved at once because he already knew who needed the material and why. STARTED:A trading desk in Gujarat serving the same kind of small factories he had just been running. Business Standard says that by 1985 he was also importing coal, oilseeds and synthetic fibre raw materials, and exporting farm goods and textiles. FUNDING:No figure is public. HAPPENED:The desk grew into the commodity house he named Adani Exports in 1988. LESSON:Be ready before the rules change. When a door opens, the person who already knows the customers walks through first. [45,60,7]
3.6 venture: Adani Exports, now Adani Enterprises (1988–present) public IDEA:A commodity trading house that buys anywhere and sells anywhere, and later an incubator, a parent company that hatches new businesses and spins them off. DECISION:The Times of India says he realised in the late 1980s that trading was his forte. Gujarat's state export corporation offered credit lines to exporters, so he built on borrowed credit. When that corporation later tightened lending, he had to find other ways to finance trades, and he calls that the moment the group was truly born. STARTED:A partnership firm in Ahmedabad in 1988, promoted by Gautam and his brother Rajesh. It became Adani Exports Ltd on 2 March 1993. A company-history compilation says it was then exporting frozen foods, dyes, plastics and farm goods to 28 countries. FUNDING:No starting capital figure could be traced to a reliable source. Early growth ran on credit lines from Gujarat State Export Corporation. The IPO on 1 November 1994 sold shares at ₹150 and was oversubscribed 25 times. An IPO is when a private company first sells shares to the public. The compilation records 12.5 lakh public shares, which works out to about ₹19 crore. HAPPENED:Revenue reached ₹643 crore in FY95, with ₹24 crore profit. By 1998 it was the country's top net foreign exchange earner among companies, by the group's own account. It became Adani Enterprises in 2006. In 2022 the company claimed ₹150 invested at the IPO had grown past ₹9 lakh. Two decades after the credit squeeze, the group bought the same state corporation. LESSON:Credit is a lifeline until the lender changes its mind. Build a second way to fund yourself before you need it. [61,62,7,58,63,45,64]
3.7 quote: "We could grow so much because we didn’t take the easy road. We were born again in the true sense." -- Gautam Adani, on losing easy credit, to The Times of India [61]
3.8 note: He bought the company that once cut his credit. Remember that.
3.9 h: Salt, marsh and the accident that made him
3.10 venture: Cargill salt and jetty joint venture (c. 1990–c. 1993) failed IDEA:American commodities giant Cargill wanted several million tonnes of Indian salt a year. The plan was salt farms in Kutch and a jetty to ship the salt out. DECISION:Adani's biographer says Cargill first looked at Kandla port, then chose Mundra after studying NASA maps and advice on water depth. Mundra had deep water, year-round access and was close to Gulf free trade zones. They agreed a 50:50 joint venture worth about ₹100 crore. STARTED:In October 1992 he hired Malay Mahadevia, a dentist friend, to run it. Mahadevia read up on chemical engineering and asked friends how salt technology worked. By some accounts, Adani hired a plane to show a doubtful official the marshes he wanted to buy. FUNDING:Adani bought the land. TIME reported he spent $11,000 in 1993 and was left with 4,500 acres. He says the state charged 10 paise per square metre at the start. HAPPENED:Cargill backed out. Public reports do not give its reason. He was left holding barren, marshy land, with the last nine miles to the port under water. LESSON:When a partner leaves, look at what you still own. His biggest business began as a failed deal's leftovers. [59,45,65,60,66]
3.11 venture: Mundra Port, now Adani Ports and SEZ (1994–present) public IDEA:First a private jetty for his own cargo, then a full commercial port with its own railway and a special economic zone, a tax-friendly industrial area, on the land around it. DECISION:The Gujarat Maritime Board approved a captive jetty in January 1994, and in 1995 the state launched a port policy built on public-private partnerships. He had also noticed that ports earn well from land and storage, not just ships, so he kept buying land. STARTED:Mahadevia, the dentist, ran the build for years while still practising dentistry. Gujarat Adani Port Ltd was formed on 26 May 1998. The first ship was a small tanker, MT Alpha-2, in 1998. On 17 February 2001 the state signed a 30-year concession, a licence to build and run the port. FUNDING:A joint company with a Gujarat state body that first held 26%, which it later sold to Adani. A compilation of company records puts the first project at ₹340 crore. The 2007 IPO sold shares at ₹440, raised ₹1,771 crore and was oversubscribed 116 times, a record then claimed by the company. HAPPENED:Capacity went from 13 million tonnes in 2005-06 to 147 million in 2014-15. In FY26 the company handled 500.8 million tonnes across 19 ports. It aims for 1 billion tonnes by 2030. On Bloomberg's count it is now his single most valuable holding, at $29.6 billion. LESSON:Own the scarce thing around the product. Anyone can build a berth later, but the land behind it was bought in 1993. [67,61,60,68,69,59,45,63,70,71,72,73]
3.12 venture: Adani Wilmar, maker of Fortune oil (1999–2025) sold IDEA:Refine imported edible oil right at the port, so tankers unload next to the factory, and sell it in branded packs to Indian kitchens. DECISION:Outlook Business says he left a Mundra Port brochure with a Wilmar employee during a visit to a Wilmar site. Wilmar's co-founder Kuok Khoon Hong followed up. The two discussed a 1,000-tonne-a-day refinery and settled on 600. STARTED:Incorporated in Ahmedabad on 22 January 1999. Its first project was a 600-tonne-a-day refinery at Mundra, running from 2000. The Fortune brand came out in fiscal 2001. FUNDING:A 50:50 joint venture. A compilation of company records says the agreed equity was $6 million. The 2022 IPO raised ₹3,600 crore (US$482 million) and was subscribed 17 times. HAPPENED:Fortune became India's top-selling consumer pack oil. But FY24 profit was just ₹171 crore on ₹51,500 crore of revenue, a margin of 0.33%. On 30 December 2024 Adani agreed to exit its roughly 44% for about $2 billion, weeks after the US indictment. The company is now called AWL Agri Business. LESSON:Big revenue is not big profit. He sold India's favourite cooking oil because the same money earned more in infrastructure. [45,74,75,76,63,77,78,79,58,80]
3.13 venture: Early power deals: Eastern Generation and the Anjar plant (1997–c. 1999) failed IDEA:Coal-fired power projects with a partner called Eastern Generation, plus a 54 MW naphtha-fuelled plant at Anjar in Kutch. DECISION:A company-history compilation records a 1997 memorandum with Eastern Generation and a letter of intent for Anjar. The motive is not on record, though Adani Power had been incorporated in 1996. STARTED:Adani Eastern Generation Company was set up in 1998 as a 50:50 venture, according to the same compilation. FUNDING:Not disclosed. HAPPENED:We found no record that either was built. Outlook Business dates the group's real entry into power generation to 2006. Treat this as an abandoned first attempt, and note that this conclusion comes from silence in the record, not from an announcement. LESSON:An early failure in an industry does not close the door. He came back about nine years later with a coal business and his own port. [63,81,45]
3.14 note: Salt failed. Oil got sold. Power failed once. He kept the land each time.
3.15 p: Meanwhile the trading desk had found the cargo that would tie everything together. By 2010 he was India's largest importer of coal for power utilities, and Mundra was adding a coal import terminal. [60,7]
3.16 venture: Coal trading and mine services (1999–present) running IDEA:Buy coal around the world and deliver it on schedule to Indian customers, a line the group calls integrated resource management. Later, run coal mines for state utilities for a fee. DECISION:His port needed volume and India needed coal. The group's timeline dates this business to 1999, and by 2010 Business Standard called him the country's largest coal importer. STARTED:Inside Adani Exports. A 2003 tie-up with a Chinese supplier, Shanxi Corporation, is recorded in a company-history compilation. The group also bought into Indonesian coal, and a regulator's 2013 dissent noted that Adani Enterprises held 74% of the Indonesian company it imported through. FUNDING:Run inside the flagship. Amounts for individual steps were not separately published. HAPPENED:Mining services shipped 49.4 million tonnes in FY26. Indian investigators have also long probed whether the group's coal imports were overvalued. Adani denies wrongdoing, and the case was still before the Supreme Court in the latest reports we could read (2023). LESSON:Control supply and demand together when you can. It also means every regulator will look hard at the price between your own companies. [7,58,60,63,82,83,84,85]
3.17 venture: City gas, now Adani Total Gas (2001–present) public IDEA:Pipe natural gas to homes and sell compressed gas to vehicles, under city-by-city licences. DECISION:Bloomberg's account of his pattern: pick sectors the government wants developed and where competition is still thin. STARTED:The group's timeline dates the start of the business to 2001. By 2010 it held licences for 11 cities. FUNDING:It sat inside Adani Enterprises until it was split off and listed as a separate company in 2018. First-stage amounts were not published. TotalEnergies bought 37.4% in a deal announced in 2019. HAPPENED:In FY26 it ran 705 CNG stations and served 1.10 million homes. Business Today pointed out in 2021 that it was valued at ₹1.48 lakh crore with ₹471 crore in profit, while a bigger rival with higher profit was worth far less. LESSON:Utility licences compound slowly, and a high share price is not the same as a big business. Check profit before you admire a valuation. [7,58,60,86,87,83,88]
3.18 venture: Adani Agri Logistics, grain silos (2005–present) running IDEA:Store and move India's wheat in modern automated silos and special rail wagons, instead of open and old warehouses. DECISION:Food Corporation of India, the state grain buyer, wanted a private pilot. Biographer R.N. Bhaskar traces the idea to the corporation's grain operations at Mundra. STARTED:A 20-year build, own and operate agreement with the state food corporation in 2005, covering silos at Moga in Punjab and Kaithal in Haryana, with field depots in five other cities. FUNDING:The Financial Express reported ₹600 crore invested by 2008. The source of that money was not stated. HAPPENED:Its customer was the government's own grain buyer, on a long contract. By October 2022 total silo capacity had reached 15.25 lakh tonnes across 24 locations. LESSON:A boring contract with one reliable buyer can fund bigger dreams. Not every business has to excite anyone. [89,59,90]
3.19 venture: Adani Agri Fresh, Farm-Pik apples (2006–present) running IDEA:Buy apples directly from Himachal Pradesh growers, keep them fresh for months in controlled-atmosphere stores, and sell them year-round under the brand Farm-Pik. DECISION:Himachal changed its farm market law in 2005, letting private firms buy directly from growers. A teaching case says apples were chosen because they grow far from most buyers, are less price sensitive, and store for up to seven months. STARTED:Stores near Shimla in 2006. Staff contacted farmers directly, posted a price for the week, and gave out reusable crates to reduce bruising. FUNDING:A wholly owned subsidiary of Adani Enterprises, so parent money. No amount has been published. HAPPENED:It built 22,400 tonnes of storage and says it buys from about 17,000 growers in 700 villages. We found no separate profit figures for it. LESSON:A law change creates a gap for about a season. Read new rules like a business plan. [91,92]
3.20 h: The giant bets on power and coal
3.21 venture: Adani Power (2006–present) public IDEA:He was already a big coal importer and owned a port. So he would burn the coal himself at Mundra and sell electricity on long contracts. DECISION:In February 2006 Gujarat's power buyer invited bids. Adani first bid ₹3.70 a unit, then revised down to ₹2.89 and won. In 2007 the Gujarat High Court called allowing that revision “discriminatory and arbitrary”. For a second tender he was the only bidder, at ₹2.35. Neither price moved with the cost of coal. STARTED:A 4,620 MW complex next to the port. Vneet Jaain joined in 2006 after a four-hour talk at a Delhi guest house. In 2010 Adani hired a telecom man, Ravi Sharma, to run the unit as chief executive. FUNDING:The 2009 IPO sold 30.17 crore shares at ₹100, about ₹3,016 crore, against a planned ₹43,139 crore for four projects. HAPPENED:This company nearly died, and that story has its own chapter. By July 2025 it ran 18,150 MW and targeted 30,670 MW by 2030. FY26 profit was ₹12,971 crore. LESSON:Never sign a fixed selling price on top of a floating cost. It is the most expensive sentence in this book. [45,82,60,81,93,94,83,65]
3.22 venture: Carmichael mine and Abbot Point, Australia (now Bravus and NQXT) (2010–present) running IDEA:Own the coal in Queensland, the rail line, and the export port, then ship cheap coal to his Indian power plants. DECISION:Coal prices were the risk in his power business, so he tried to own the source. Business Standard reported he would source coal at $45 a tonne, against a $76 international average in 2009. He signed the first deal the same week his mother died. STARTED:He bought mining rights from Linc Energy in 2010, then a 99-year lease on Abbot Point port in 2011. Reports of the price differ, as our fact-check notes show. FUNDING:The plan was A$16.5 billion. More than 30 banks and lenders declined to fund it, so in 2018 Adani said it would pay for a smaller A$2 billion mine itself. HAPPENED:The plan shrank from 60 million tonnes a year to 10, after what Reuters called a sustained campaign that scared off lenders, insurers and engineers. Coal first shipped around December 2021, seven years late. In 2025 Guardian Australia found the mine had paid no corporate tax after over three years of extraction. The company was renamed Bravus in 2020. LESSON:A project can survive but still miss its purpose. Ask what happens if the world stops wanting what you plan to sell. [60,95,96,97,98,86,99,100]
3.23 example: For example: the same Australian port has changed hands inside his empire twice. Adani Ports bought the Abbot Point lease in 2011 at an enterprise value of A$1.829 billion. It sold the asset to the family side in 2013 at A$2.051 billion to cut its debt. Then in December 2025 it bought it back at A$3.975 billion, paying with new shares to a family-linked seller, after approval by a majority of minority shareholders. [100,72]
3.24 note: Same port, three prices, one family. Learn the words “related party”.
3.25 venture: Adani Transmission, now Adani Energy Solutions (2015–present) public IDEA:Power plants need wires. Build the lines, then buy more lines, then sell electricity directly to a city's homes. DECISION:Adani Power had already built its own lines, including a roughly 1,000 km high-voltage link across three states in under 24 months. Those lines became the start of a separate business. STARTED:Separated from Adani Enterprises and listed in 2015. It then bought transmission assets from GMR and Reliance Infrastructure in 2016 and 2017. FUNDING:Demerger first, which means splitting one listed company into two. It paid ₹18,800 crore for Reliance Infrastructure's Mumbai power business in 2018, and later brought in Abu Dhabi's IHC for ₹3,850 crore. A 2025 share sale to institutions raised ₹8,773 crore. HAPPENED:The Mumbai deal cut Anil Ambani's company's debt from ₹22,000 crore to ₹7,500 crore and handed Adani around 3 million customers. By March 2026 the network stood at 27,949 circuit km, including lines under construction. LESSON:Rivals in debt sell good assets. Keep cash and credit ready for the day they must. [45,101,102,103,104,71]
3.26 venture: Adani Green Energy (2015–present) public IDEA:Build giant solar and wind farms in India, and sell the power on long contracts to state-backed buyers. DECISION:Lenders were turning away from his Australian coal project. Bloomberg noted he kept raising funds partly by pivoting to green energy. STARTED:In 2015 the group had only 40 MW of solar running. It then built a 648 MW single-location plant at Kamuthi in Tamil Nadu. FUNDING:Demerged and listed in June 2018. TotalEnergies bought half of a solar portfolio and 20% of the company for $2.5 billion in total by its own figures, with the 20% announced in January 2021. IEEFA, a think tank critical of coal, described aggressive use of debt across the group. HAPPENED:Shares rose about 2,400% from listing to October 2020. By 1 April 2026 it had 19.3 GW running, including 9.4 GW at Khavda in Kutch, where it plans 30 GW by 2029 on land five times the size of Paris. This is also the company at the centre of the US case covered later. LESSON:Follow where cheap capital wants to go. Just remember the money can leave as fast as it came. [86,102,105,87,106,107]
3.27 venture: Solar manufacturing and green hydrogen (Adani New Industries) (2017–present) running IDEA:Make solar panels and wind turbines in India, and later produce green hydrogen, a clean fuel made with renewable power. DECISION:Our reading: a group building giant solar farms needs panels, so this looks like the same adjacency logic. The company has not published a decision memo. 3.28 venture: Adani Defence and Aerospace (c. 2015–present) running IDEA:Build military equipment in India, starting with drones and small arms made with foreign partners. 3.29 venture: Adani Capital and Adani Housing (2017–2023) sold IDEA:Lend to small businesses and home buyers. 3.30 h: Airports, cement, media: buying the big names
3.31 venture: Adani Airports (2019–present) running IDEA:Run airports as more than runways: a consumer business reaching what the group called a 300-million-strong base of fliers and non-fliers. DECISION:In 2018 the government opened six airports to private bidders and set no limit on how many one bidder could win. Adani had little or no airport experience. STARTED:Bids won in 2019, beating GMR, Fairfax, NIIF and others. Handovers ran from October 2020 to October 2021. It lost one contest, Jewar near Delhi, to Zurich Airport. FUNDING:Adani Airport Holdings is owned by Adani Enterprises. For Mumbai it first tried to buy minority stakes in March 2019, was blocked by GVK, then bought GVK's debt and converted it into shares, for about ₹15,000 crore overall according to reports. HAPPENED:It took control of Mumbai in July 2021 after a two-year fight, which made it India's largest private airport operator. The ₹19,650 crore Navi Mumbai airport opened to flights on 25 December 2025. FY26 traffic across eight airports was 95.3 million passengers. LESSON:If you lack experience in a field, buy the people and the hardest asset. Just know that paying above everyone else is a bet you must later earn back. [114,115,116,117,118,119,120,109]
3.32 venture: AdaniConneX, data centres (2021–present) running IDEA:Combine the group's land, power lines and renewable energy with a specialist's know-how to build data centres. STARTED:A 50:50 joint venture announced on 23 February 2021, starting at Chennai, Navi Mumbai, Noida, Vizag and Hyderabad. FUNDING:Both partners said they would invest significant capital. In February 2026 the group pledged $100 billion for AI-ready data centres by 2035. That is a promise, not yet a result. The FY26 milestones the group listed were 17 MW at Chennai and 4.8 MW at Hyderabad. LESSON:Look for what your existing assets could feed. A power company is one partner away from being an AI company. [121,122,123,124,83]
3.33 venture: Yangon container terminal, Myanmar (2019–2023) sold IDEA:Build a terminal on the Yangon River on a 50-year deal, as one link in a chain of ports from India to Southeast Asia. 3.34 venture: Adani Sportsline, cricket (2019–present) running IDEA:Own cricket teams, the one thing in India that everyone watches. 3.35 venture: Ambuja Cements and ACC (2022–present) public IDEA:Buy into cement at scale overnight. Then use the group's ports, coal, power and rail to cut the cost of each bag. DECISION:In May 2022 he struck his largest ever deal for Swiss firm Holcim's India business, having no cement plants of his own. His aim was to become the largest and most efficient maker by 2030. STARTED:An offshore company owned by the family made the purchase in Mauritius. 3.36 venture: NDTV (2022–present) public IDEA:Build what the group called a next-generation media platform, starting from one of India's best-known news brands. DECISION:A loan made years earlier was the way in. In 2009-10 the NDTV founders borrowed over ₹400 crore from a little-known firm, VCPL, then indirectly controlled by Reliance. STARTED:On 23 August 2022 an Adani company bought VCPL for ₹113.74 crore and used the warrants the same day. NDTV said this happened without the founders' knowledge or consent. It is the clearest case in this book of a company taken without its founders' agreement. LESSON:Read every clause before you borrow. 3.37 quote: "this exercise of rights by VCPL was executed without any input from, conversation with, or consent of the NDTV founders" -- NDTV statement, 23 August 2022 [142]
3.38 note: Founders: read this one twice. A forgotten loan became a hostile takeover.
3.39 h: Abroad, and the bets that went nowhere
3.40 venture: Haifa Port and Colombo West terminal (2022–present) running IDEA:Take the port playbook overseas, to the Mediterranean and the Indian Ocean trade routes. DECISION:Haifa came through a two-year Israeli privatisation tender. The company places Haifa and Colombo on what it calls the East-West trade corridor. STARTED:Adani Ports won Haifa in July 2022 with local partner Gadot, holding 70% to its 30%. Colombo West started commercial work in April 2025. Haifa posted its highest-ever quarterly revenue in the April to June 2025 quarter. Both sit in the group's aim of about 15% of cargo from abroad by 2030. LESSON:Keep one way to pay on hand. Walking away from cheap US money only works if you have your own. [143,144,145,83,100,72,146,147]
3.41 venture: Godda power plant, for Bangladesh (2017–present) running IDEA:Build a 1,600 MW coal plant in Jharkhand, India, and sell every unit to another country for 25 years. DECISION:Signed in 2017 with Sheikh Hasina's government without a tender, Reuters reported. 3.42 quote: "We will not let any power producer hold us hostage," -- Muhammad Fauzul Kabir Khan, Bangladesh power adviser, to Reuters [148]
3.43 venture: Dharavi redevelopment (2022–present) running IDEA:Rebuild Dharavi, one of the world's largest slum clusters, in central Mumbai. Rehouse residents and earn from selling new space. 3.44 venture: Mundra Petrochem, green PVC plant (2021–present) pivoted IDEA:A ₹34,900 crore plant to turn coal into PVC, to cut India's imports. 3.45 venture: The deals he dropped in 2023 (2022–2023) failed IDEA:A shopping list of cheap, troubled or strategic assets: DB Power, Macquarie's road companies, SKS Power, PTC India and Future Retail. 3.46 venture: Adani Data Networks, 5G spectrum (2022–2025) sold IDEA:Buy 5G airwaves to run private networks at the group's own airports and ports, not a consumer phone service. DECISION:The 2022 auction let companies buy spectrum directly, and the group wanted its own secure networks. STARTED:Bought 400 MHz in the 26 GHz band for ₹212 crore in July 2022. 3.47 venture: Adani One super app (Adani Digital Labs) (2022–2025) merged IDEA:One app for flights, trains, shopping, payments and rewards, starting from airport passengers. HAPPENED:It reportedly reached about 30 million users by March 2024 without lasting use or clear revenue. 3.48 venture: Kutch Copper (2024–present) running IDEA:Build the world's largest single-location custom copper smelter at Mundra, to cut India's imports for electric vehicles, solar and wires. HAPPENED:Bloomberg reported the smelter only began processing mined ore in June 2025, after delays from a global shortage of copper ore. A second phase is planned to reach 1 million tonnes a year. LESSON:Check your raw material before you build the plant. It is the same issue that hit Adani Power. [161,162,163]
3.49 venture: Kenya airport and power lines; Sri Lanka wind farms (2024–2025) failed IDEA:Run Nairobi's main airport and build Kenyan power lines. Separately, build two wind farms in Sri Lanka, estimated at $1 billion. DECISION:Kenya's was a government procurement process expected to give Adani control of the airport. Sri Lanka's projects went through government review and price talks. STARTED:Proposals with each government, at different stages. FUNDING:Not disclosed. HAPPENED:A day after the US indictment, on 21 November 2024, Kenya's president ordered more than $2.5 billion of deals cancelled. 3.50 venture: Jaiprakash Associates (2025–present) running IDEA:Buy a debt-laden conglomerate out of bankruptcy. DECISION:Vedanta offered more in total, around ₹16,000 crore against ₹14,535 crore. 3.51 p: There are smaller pieces too. The group's road arm operated the Ganga Expressway in 2026, and it owns real estate through Adani Realty. Both are run through the same incubator model that created everything above. Count the failures in this chapter and you will find something odd: most of the ones that hurt were not bad businesses. They were businesses that depended on one fuel, one government, one landlord, or one partner. [109,152]
3.52 lesson: Failures are the cheap part of the tuition :: The Cargill exit gave him Mundra. The 5G and super-app bets cost little against his group's size and he closed them. The one that did real damage, Adani Power's fixed-price coal bet, was too large to walk away from. Keep your experiments small enough to survive, and only bet big on what you already understand.

## C4 the-big-bets | 1988–2015 | Salt, Marsh and a Cheque for ₹3 Crore
4.0 p: Sometime in 1994, a soya trader named Atul Chaturvedi quoted Adani a price for a deal. He later admitted that he padded it by ₹200 a tonne, and casually asked for ₹3 crore upfront. Nothing was signed, and his own company was short of cash, so he let the matter drop. [45]
4.1 p: Days later an envelope reached his Bhopal office. Inside was a cheque for ==₹3 crore== from Adani Exports. When he called to check, Adani's reply was short: “Tumse toh baat hui thi na”, meaning “We did speak about this, right?” Three years later, Chaturvedi joined the group, and he went on to run Adani Wilmar. [45]
4.2 quote: "What I liked about Adani was that he kept his word," -- Atul Chaturvedi, to Outlook Business [45]
4.3 note: A spoken deal, honoured with ₹3 crore. That is how trust compounds.
4.4 p: That was the trader's edge in the early years. By September 1992 he was running a ₹300 crore trading business. The group's financing leaned on credit lines from a state export corporation, and he is frank that money was the limit. “Dreams were infinite but finances finite,” he told The Times of India. [45,61]
4.5 h: The land nobody wanted
4.6 p: When Cargill walked away, most traders would have written off the salt plan and the land. Adani did not. In 1995 Gujarat launched a new policy to develop six ports through public-private partnerships, and Mundra was one of them. __The failed salt deal had quietly left him standing in the right place when the rule changed.__ [61,65]
4.7 p: His biographer records a second insight. As a trader, Adani had paid demurrage, the fee a port charges when cargo sits too long, and he saw that ports earn serious money from land and storage. So he kept buying. Group president Saurin Shah says demurrage was the single number he watched most closely in his trading and shipping days. [59]
4.8 quote: "He would happily take a Rs 5 crore loss on a trade—which he said provided invaluable learning opportunities—but would bring the house down if even Rs 500 had been paid as demurrage." -- Saurin Shah, Adani Group president, in R.N. Bhaskar’s biography [59]
4.9 p: How cheap was the land? Adani gave the numbers himself in 2014, when Rahul Gandhi and others accused him of getting it for ₹1 from Narendra Modi's government. He said Chimanbhai Patel's Congress government charged ==10 paise per square metre== in 1993, Keshubhai Patel's BJP government charged ₹1 in 1995, Shankersinh Vaghela charged ₹1.5, and Modi's government charged an average of ₹15 for about 5,000 acres. He said only a third of his 15,946 acres came during Modi's years. [66,167]
4.10 quote: "Not a single acre has been taken from any farmer…" -- Gautam Adani, to PTI, 2014 [66]
4.11 p: Both things can be true at once. His biographer argues the price was simply what barren marsh was worth in 1993. It was also a set of government decisions that later proved enormously valuable to one buyer. As a founder, notice how often Indian infrastructure begins with a state deciding who gets what, and keep your records clean, because someone will ask you about the price 20 years later. [66,59,60]
4.12 note: Ten paise a square metre. Worth a fortune once a port arrived.
4.13 example: For example: at 10 paise a square metre, one acre (about 4,047 square metres) cost about ₹405. That is his own stated 1993 price. The ₹5 crore trading loss Saurin Shah mentions would have covered more than 1 lakh acres at that price. His whole land bank in 2014 was 15,946 acres, bought at rising prices over the years. [66,59]
4.14 h: Failing on purpose, sort of
4.15 p: In 1999 a crowd stood on a Mundra berth to watch an employee's ==₹4 crore== experiment. His idea was a floating wall of pontoons to break the waves, so ships could dock on more days. Within minutes the pontoons began crashing together, and in about two hours the whole thing had sunk. The man expected to be fired, and then felt a pat on his shoulder. [45]
4.16 quote: "Well tried" -- Gautam Adani to the engineer, as recalled by Malay Mahadevia [45]
4.17 p: Stories like this come from his own executives, so read them as friendly witnesses. There are several. A young trader who lost a couple of crore in a day on castor seeds offered to quit, and Adani is believed to have told him: “After such a costly learning, I can’t afford to lose you.” More than 17 years later that man was a general manager in the chairman's office. __A company learns what failure costs from how the boss reacts on the worst day.__ [45]
4.18 h: The kidnapping, and the Taj
4.19 p: On the evening of 1 January 1998, Adani and a companion, Shantilal Patel, were driving from Karnavati Club when, according to the police case, gunmen stopped the car and took them away blindfolded. Police alleged the ransom demand was ==₹15 crore==, and that the two men were released after it was paid. Those tried were acquitted: six in 2005 for lack of evidence, and two in 2018. Adani did not appear to testify in that last trial, asking the court to rely on his earlier statement, and witnesses could not identify the two men. [168,169]
4.20 p: Ten years later came another close call. On 26 November 2008 he was at the Chambers restaurant inside Mumbai's Taj Mahal Hotel when terrorists attacked, and he spent about two frightening hours hiding in the dark until troops got the diners out. Neither event slowed the building. [60]
4.21 note: Kidnapped in 1998, trapped at the Taj in 2008. He kept building.
4.22 h: Raising money like Dhirubhai
4.23 p: Between 2007 and 2010, Adani went to investors again and again. The Mundra IPO in November 2007 was oversubscribed 116 times. In 2007 he also raised $250 million through a foreign currency convertible bond, a loan that can later turn into shares. In 2009, Adani Power's IPO was subscribed 21 times, and in 2010 Adani Enterprises raised $850 million through a QIP, a fast share sale to large institutions. [58,70,68]
4.24 p: A merchant banker explained the pattern simply: investors who made money on Mundra came back for power. By August 2010 he had raised over ₹10,000 crore from the market, and his three listed companies were worth ₹1,01,878 crore. Business Standard compared him to Dhirubhai Ambani, then pointed out the difference: unlike Dhirubhai, he had so far been mostly a trader, with few assets of his own. [60]
4.25 quote: "The Mundra port acted as a catalyst for the growth of the Adani empire. Credit must go to Adani's risk-taking abilities," -- A retired Gujarat state official, to Business Standard [60]
4.26 p: Rivals on the coast were real but rarely matched him. Business Standard wrote in 2015 that no Indian company came close across both containers and bulk cargo. Essar's terminals mostly served its own needs. Nikhil Gandhi, who promoted the rival Pipavav port, publicly praised Adani's courage to turn plans into reality. Tata Power built its own huge plant in Mundra, and later shared Adani's coal troubles. [67,60,170]
4.27 p: In 2015 he did one more clever thing: he split Adani Enterprises. Ports, power and transmission became separately listed companies, each owned directly by existing shareholders. A demerger like this lets investors buy the piece they like, and it lets each piece borrow on its own. He has used this pattern since, with green energy and gas in 2018. [7,102,58]
4.28 facts: ₹300 crore = Trading business, September 1992 [45] || 116x = Mundra Port IPO subscription, 2007 [70] || ₹1,771 crore = Raised at the 2007 port IPO [68] || ₹1,01,878 crore = Combined value of his 3 listed firms, Aug 2010 [60]
4.29 timeline: 1992 = Hires a dentist friend to run the Cargill salt project. [45] || 1994 = Adani Exports IPO, 25 times subscribed. Jetty approved at Mundra. [7,69] || 1 Jan 1998 = Kidnapped, according to the police case. All accused later acquitted. [168] || Oct 1998 = First berths open. MT Alpha-2 is the first ship. [69,7] || 2001 = Signs the 30-year port concession. [68] || 2007 = Port IPO at ₹440. [68] || 2009 = Adani Power IPO at ₹100. [81] || 2015 = Splits the flagship into separately listed companies. [7]
4.30 lesson: Turn every deal you lose into an asset :: Cargill took its money home, and Adani kept the land, the local knowledge and a team that had learned salt and jetties. When your partner leaves, list what is still yours. You may be holding more than you think.

## C5 near-death | 2010–2022 | The Coal Bet That Nearly Broke Him
5.0 p: In November 2010, TIME magazine visited Mundra. Adani told its reporter that his power plant had one simple risk: unless you completely goof up on fuel, it was a sure bet. Then he added a line that reads differently today. [65]
5.1 quote: "From the first day that we start generating, we start making money," -- Gautam Adani, to TIME, November 2010 [65]
5.2 p: He had signed to sell power to Gujarat at ==₹2.35 a unit== on one contract and ₹2.89 on another. Those prices were not designed to move when coal prices did. The plan rested on cheap Indonesian coal. Then, in 2010, Indonesia issued new rules that raised the price of its coal. In a petition, Adani said this raised its coal cost by ==153%==, and that it would lose ₹790 crore a year on the Gujarat contract alone. [82,170,171]
5.3 note: Fixed selling price, floating cost. Read this page twice, first-time founders.
5.4 p: So he went to the regulators and asked for a higher price, called compensatory tariff, on the grounds that a foreign law had changed. In 2013 the electricity regulator agreed, and an appeals tribunal later upheld that. Adani Power began booking the extra money as revenue, even though it had not been paid yet. A dissenting regulator pointed out something awkward. [170,171,82]
5.5 quote: "Adani Enterprises held 74% of shares in the Indonesian coal company through which the coal was being imported," -- S Jayaraman, regulator, in his 2013 dissent [82]
5.6 p: In other words, part of the higher coal price may have flowed to another Adani company. Then on ==11 April 2017== the Supreme Court set aside the earlier rulings. It said a rise in coal price does not excuse a company from a contract it signed knowing that risk. It did leave open relief for changes in Indian law, like a domestic coal shortage. Adani Power fell 16% that day. [82,170,171]
5.7 quote: "It is clear that an unexpected rise in the price of coal will not absolve the generating companies from performing their part of the contract" -- Supreme Court of India, 11 April 2017 [82]
5.8 h: ₹1 for 51%
5.9 p: The bill was brutal. Adani Power reversed ==₹4,364 crore== of income it had already booked, made a cash loss of ₹1,109 crore in FY17, and saw its debt climb to 6.18 times its net worth. The rating agency CARE noted heavy repayments ahead, and that the promoter family had put in ₹1,702 crore that year with no firm promise for the next. By September 2017 Bloomberg Quint was reporting that the Mundra unit's debt stood at ₹21,707 crore and its market value at just ₹106 crore. [171,82]
5.10 p: Then came the most extraordinary offer in this book. The company offered ==51% of its Mundra plant to Gujarat's power utility for ₹1==. Lenders led by State Bank of India later backed away from the talks, and by mid-2018 Business Standard reported Adani Power Mundra might have to seek bankruptcy protection at the National Company Law Tribunal. The plant's output had sunk to 37% of its capacity in the March 2018 quarter, and loan dues were reported above ₹22,000 crore. [172,173,82]
5.11 note: India's largest private coal plant, offered for one rupee. Sit with that.
5.12 p: Rescue came from governments, not markets. In October 2018 the Supreme Court allowed the regulator to amend the contracts of Gujarat's three imported-coal plants, those of Tata, Adani and Essar, so that future fuel costs could pass through to buyers, with a cap. On 3 December 2018 the Gujarat government accepted a committee's report and allowed the higher cost to pass on to consumers. Scroll, which investigated this in 2019, reported that the change would raise prices for consumers and saved the project from bankruptcy. Adani declined to answer its questions, saying the matter was before the courts. [174,82]
5.13 p: __Our reading: he survived partly because the plant was too large and too important to fail.__ That is a real advantage, but not one you can plan to have. Tata Power sat in the same trap with the same Indonesian coal. In a separate case, the Supreme Court also ruled that Adani Power's termination of a ₹2.35 contract was valid, because its bid had relied on a state miner's promise of coal that never came. Knowing which risks you can survive, before you sign, is the real lesson. [170,174,82,175]
5.14 note: Too big to fail is not a strategy you can copy.
5.15 h: The mine that shrank
5.16 p: At the same time, his Australian dream was stalling. He bought the Carmichael rights in 2010, picturing a 60 million tonne a year mine, a railway of around 400 km and a ==A$16 billion== bill. Then a campaign called Stop Adani started pressing banks, insurers and contractors, and it became an issue in Australia's 2019 election. More than 30 banks and lenders declined to finance it. [95,96,86]
5.17 p: In 2018 he cut the plan. He announced that the group would self-fund a mine shrunk to 10 million tonnes a year, one-sixth of the plan. Coal finally began to move in late 2021. Guardian Australia later pointed out that the company had pledged $22 billion in taxes and royalties, but had paid no corporate tax more than three years into production. For the group, the prize had shrunk from a giant mine feeding its Indian plants to a modest export business. [95,97,99]
5.18 h: ₹33.82
5.19 p: By 2020 Adani Power's share price had been stuck for years well below its ₹100 IPO price. IEEFA, a think tank critical of coal, called it a rare failure in the group's rise. In May and June 2020 the family proposed buying out every public shareholder and delisting the company from the stock exchange, at a floor price of ==₹33.82 a share==. The whole public stake would have cost about ₹3,264 crore. Shareholders approved it. [176,177,102,88]
5.20 p: The stock exchanges never gave their approval. While the paperwork sat, the share price went up more than tenfold, hitting ₹432.80 in August 2022. In September 2022 the family withdrew the offer, saying it was no longer commercially viable. If you had sold at ₹33.82, you would have missed that rise. [176]
5.21 example: For example: the family tried to buy out all public shareholders for about ₹3,264 crore in 2020. In just the July to September 2026 quarter, Adani Power reported a net profit of ₹6,919 crore. That is more than double the entire buyout price, earned in three months. [177,155]
5.22 p: How did it turn? The company used the Gujarat fix as a base and then went shopping in India's bankruptcy courts. It bought struggling plants at what it calls attractive valuations and repaired them: Raigarh and Raipur in 2019, Mahan in 2022, Coastal Energen (now Mutiara) and Lanco Amarkantak (now Korba) in 2024, then Vidarbha for ₹4,000 crore in 2025. Rising demand for power helped. FY26 profit was ₹12,971 crore. [178,94,83]
5.23 note: Same plants, better demand. Timing and luck count. Admit it.

5.25 timeline: 2006–07 = Wins Gujarat power contracts at fixed prices. [82] || 2010 = Indonesia changes coal pricing rules. [170] || 2013 = Regulator allows compensatory tariff. 5.26 lesson: Write down the one assumption that could kill you :: In 2010 Adani himself named fuel as the one thing that could go wrong, after he had already signed fixed prices. Before you sign a long contract, ask what happens if your biggest cost doubles. If the answer is “we die”, change the contract or change the plan.

## C6 the-bombshell | 2023–2026 | The Report, the Indictment, the Comeback
6.0 p: A short seller is an investor who profits when a share price falls. On ==24 January 2023==, a US short seller called Hindenburg Research published its report on the Adani Group, then valued at about $218 billion. It said its two-year investigation showed stock manipulation and accounting fraud, and that it held short positions itself. The group called the report baseless, and a later 413-page response called it a calculated attack on India. [179,180,181]
6.1 p: The timing could not have been worse. Adani Enterprises was about to launch an FPO, a follow-on public offer in which a listed company sells new shares, for ==₹20,000 crore==, or $2.45 billion, the biggest of its kind in India. Within two days of trading, seven group companies lost $48 billion. On the first day, the offer got just 1% of the bids it needed. Abu Dhabi's International Holding Company stepped in with $400 million, and the sale scraped through on 31 January. [182,180,183]
6.2 p: The next day the shares crashed anyway. Adani Enterprises fell 28% and Credit Suisse stopped accepting group bonds as loan security. Late that night, the company did something unusual. It cancelled a fully subscribed share sale, and gave the money back. [183,180]
6.3 quote: "For me, the interest of my investors is paramount and everything else is secondary." -- Gautam Adani, video message, 2 February 2023 [184]
6.4 p: By early February the group's market value had dropped by more than half at its worst, hitting a record low of about ==$82 billion== combined. Adani fell from the third-richest person in the world to outside the top 25 on Forbes' list. Reuters later put the sell-off at $150 billion, The Times of India at $140 billion. Hindenburg's claims were never tested in a trial, and the group denied them all. [185,186,187,188]
6.5 note: Third richest to outside the top 25. Five weeks. Debt makes things fast.
6.6 h: How he survived it
6.7 p: His first problem was a kind of loan called a margin loan. The family had pledged shares as security for borrowings, and when prices fall, lenders demand more security or sell the shares. So the first move was to pay them back early. By 12 March 2023 the family had prepaid ==$2.15 billion== of share-backed loans, plus $500 million on the Ambuja purchase, and pledged holdings fell sharply. [189,185]
6.8 p: His second move was to stop buying. DB Power, PTC India, SKS Power and Macquarie's roads all went, and a ₹34,900 crore plastics plant was paused. His third was to find a single loud believer. In March 2023, Rajiv Jain of US fund GQG Partners bought shares in four Adani firms for ==₹15,446 crore==, about $1.9 billion. Eleven weeks later that stake was worth ₹23,049 crore. GQG kept buying. [113,153,189,185]
6.9 note: One outside investor can change a story. Know who would call you back.
6.10 p: Then the courts. A six-member Supreme Court committee reported in May 2023 that it could not conclude there was a regulatory failure on price manipulation, though Financial Express noted this was not a clean chit. On 3 January 2024 the Supreme Court refused to order a special investigation and left the matter with SEBI, India's market regulator. Then, a year after the crash, the group's market value had risen 112% from the low. __A crisis met with cash and speed can leave you stronger on paper, though the questions do not go away.__ [189,190,185]
6.11 p: SEBI's answers came slowly. On 18 September 2025 it closed two matters without penalty, covering alleged transactions with related companies. A day later Reuters reported more than a dozen cases were still open. On 28 September 2026 SEBI ruled it could not prove that Gautam's brother Vinod Adani controlled two offshore funds that held group shares, and dropped that case. It fined two other people ₹20 lakh each for not giving correct information, saying one had made a deliberate attempt to mislead. Separately, Gautam Adani, four group companies and 13 others paid ₹1.48 crore to settle the same shareholding rules case, without admitting or denying anything. [191,187,192,193,194,195]
6.12 quote: "Having considered the matter holistically, I find that the allegations made against noticees are not established," -- Kamlesh Chandra Varshney, SEBI whole-time member, 18 September 2025 [191]
6.13 p: By then Hindenburg itself had gone. Its founder Nathan Anderson announced in January 2025 that he was disbanding the firm. He wrote that “We shook some empires that we felt needed shaking”. Closing did not prove the report right or wrong, in either direction. [181,186]
6.14 h: The Brooklyn indictment
6.15 p: Then came a harder blow. On ==20 November 2024== US prosecutors in Brooklyn unsealed an indictment charging Gautam Adani, his nephew Sagar and former Adani Green chief executive Vneet Jaain with securities and wire fraud. They said a wider group had agreed to pay about $265 million in bribes to Indian officials to win solar power contracts, and had then misled US investors about it. The SEC filed a civil case too. These were allegations, which Adani denied, and he was not charged under the US bribery statute itself. [196,107,197,198,199]
6.16 p: The damage was immediate and practical. Kenya's president cancelled over $2.5 billion in deals the very next day. TotalEnergies, a 20% owner of Adani Green, said it had not been told of the investigation and paused new money. Adani Ports dropped its $553 million request to a US agency for Colombo, and Adani Green pulled out of its Sri Lanka wind projects. By December he had agreed to sell out of Fortune oil for $2 billion. [164,200,145,165,78]
6.17 quote: "Until such time when the accusations against the Adani group individuals and their consequences have been clarified, TotalEnergies will not make any new financial contribution" -- TotalEnergies statement, 25 November 2024 [200]
6.18 p: A separate problem surfaced too. In May 2026 Adani Enterprises agreed to pay ==$275 million== to the US Treasury's sanctions office, OFAC. OFAC said the company had bought liquefied petroleum gas from a Dubai trader between November 2023 and June 2025, that the gas came from Iran, and that “Red flags should have put AEL on notice”. The first cargo, worth $5.67 million, came on a 25-year-old tanker, with papers naming Oman as its origin. OFAC called the apparent violations egregious. The company settled without admitting them, and told exchanges the LPG trade was under 1.5% of its revenue. [201,202,203]
6.19 p: In 2026 the American case turned. By May, Adani had settled the SEC case, without admitting wrongdoing, for $6 million, with $12 million from Sagar. The Justice Department, now under the Trump administration, asked to drop the charges with prejudice, which means they cannot be brought again. Adani had pledged in late 2024 to invest $10 billion in the US, and reports asked whether that mattered. The judge asked Adani in court whether anything had been promised in exchange. [204,205,206,33,198]
6.20 p: On ==10 August 2026== Judge Nicholas Garaufis dismissed the fraud charges against Gautam Adani, Sagar Adani and Jaain. He accepted just one of the government's reasons: that the anti-bribery language in Adani Green's documents was generic enough to be mere puffery, the kind of vague claim no investor could rely on. He also made clear this was no endorsement of the department's decision and no opinion on whether the allegations were true. He called the way the decision was reached “highly unusual”. [198,207,33,208]
6.21 p: So how should you read this? No jury ever heard the evidence, and no court decided whether bribes were paid. Adani is free of the charges and did not admit guilt. On 3 September 2026 the same judge refused to drop the case against the five other accused, because the government had not given enough evidence for its reasons. The Justice Department went to an appeals court on 28 September, and that fight was still running at the time of our last reading in October. [208,199,198]
6.22 note: Dismissed is not the same as disproved. Nor is it proof of guilt.
6.23 timeline: 24 Jan 2023 = Hindenburg publishes its report. [179] || 1 Feb 2023 = Fully subscribed ₹20,000 crore FPO cancelled. [183] || Mar 2023 = GQG buys ₹15,446 crore of shares. Pledged loans prepaid. [189] || 3 Jan 2024 = Supreme Court declines to order a special probe. [190] || 20 Nov 2024 = US indictment unsealed. [196] || 18 Sep 2025 = SEBI closes two Hindenburg-linked matters. [191] || May 2026 = SEC settlement, OFAC settlement, DOJ asks to drop case. [204,201] || 10 Aug 2026 = Fraud charges against Adani dismissed with prejudice. [207]
6.24 lesson: In a crisis, act on cash first and story second :: Within seven weeks of the report, Adani repaid the loans that could have forced his shares onto the market, returned ₹20,000 crore, and cancelled deal after deal. Whatever you think of the allegations, that order is worth copying. Fix what can kill you this month before you argue about reputation.
6.25 lesson: Do not treat a compliance rule as paperwork :: A 25-year-old tanker, a Dubai trader and a $5.67 million first shipment became a $275 million settlement. OFAC's point was that the paperwork looked clean, but the red flags were visible. Assign someone to look for what the documents do not say.

## C7 scale | As of 2 November 2026 | Where the Money Actually Sits
7.0 p: Ask how rich Gautam Adani is and you can get four very different answers in the same week. Forbes's real-time page for him showed ==$77.7 billion== on 1 November 2026. Its India's Richest list, measured on 30 September, gave “Gautam Adani & family” ==$112.7 billion==, and put him back at number one in India after three years. Bloomberg's index had him at ==$109 billion== on 31 October, ranked 18th in the world. Hurun's Indian list in September said about $96 billion. [209,0,210,73,211]
7.1 p: Why the gap? Most of this wealth is not money in a bank. It is a share of companies, multiplied by a share price that changes every second, minus debt that outsiders cannot fully see. Lists also differ on who counts: Forbes's India list explicitly covers a fortune he shares with his family. __Remember this whenever you read a billionaire's net worth: it is an opinion about share prices, not a bank balance.__ [73,210]

7.3 p: Below those sit $12.8 billion in Adani Energy Solutions, $2.32 billion in Adani Total Gas, and $1.75 billion in Ambuja Cements, plus private holdings such as Adani Properties at $4.69 billion, all on Bloomberg's estimate. In the flagship, the family held 71.97% in September 2026, with 47.43% held by Gautam and his brother Rajesh on behalf of the S.B. Adani Family Trust. [73,212]
7.4 note: The power company that nearly died is now his second-biggest stake.
7.5 h: How the fortune actually grew
7.6 p: For most of his career he was rich, not record-breaking. The leap came in a few strange years. His fortune was $32.4 billion in December 2020, up $21.1 billion that year. From January 2020 to June 2021, group shares rose between 122% and 819%, while the Sensex rose 27%. By June 2021, Business Today put his wealth at $77 billion, and it asked directly why the shares had risen so much. [86,88]
7.7 example: For example: in June 2021 Adani Total Gas was valued at ₹1.48 lakh crore on ₹471 crore of profit. Gujarat Gas, a much larger rival, was worth ₹36,921 crore on ₹1,275 crore of profit. One analyst in 2020 called it “FOMO syndrome”, the fear of missing out. Business Today said the family then owned 56% to 75% of the listed firms, so ratings like that made it far richer on paper. [88,86]
7.8 p: He peaked at about ==$150 billion== in 2022, briefly one of the richest people on the planet. After the Hindenburg report it fell far faster than it rose. That swing shows the risk in the structure. Forbes describes his growth as debt-financed acquisitions and close ties to Prime Minister Modi. He has said he got no special favours and keeps good relations across parties, and The Times of India notes the group invests in states run by different parties. Read those claims side by side and judge for yourself. [211,186,0,66,61]
7.9 quote: "The market is having FOMO syndrome when it comes to Adani stocks," -- Sanjiv Bhasin, IIFL Securities, to Bloomberg, December 2020 [86]
7.10 h: The machine underneath
7.11 p: Strip away the share prices and there is still a very large machine. In FY26, the group reported EBITDA of ==₹94,834 crore==. EBITDA means operating earnings before interest, tax and wear on assets, a rough measure of cash generated. It spent ₹1,52,967 crore on new projects in one year, which it says is the highest ever by any Indian company and about equal to what it built in its first 25 years. [109]
7.12 p: It reports net debt of 3.3 times EBITDA, meaning roughly 3.3 years of those earnings would repay its debts if nothing else were paid. It also says its cost of borrowing fell from 10.3% in FY19 to 7.8%. When it needed fresh equity it raised ₹24,930 crore through a rights issue, and the company says it was oversubscribed by 30%. A rights issue offers new shares to existing owners. These are the company's own figures, so read them as its best case. [109,83,213]

7.14 h: Rivals, in plain sight
7.15 p: The rival in the headlines is Mukesh Ambani. Adani went from second place back to first on Forbes's India list in 2026, with a $20.7 billion rise that year. In cement he is chasing UltraTech, and he said in 2022 he aimed to be the largest by 2030. In airports he beat GMR in 2019, and lost Jewar to Zurich. In power, Business Today's 2021 comparison with Tata Power showed the gap in valuation and debt. [211,210,133,115,88]
7.16 p: Our reading: he wins mostly by being willing to pay more and wait longer than others, in sectors where the government hands out licences. When that works, as in airports, he ends up as the largest private operator. When it does not, as in Yangon or Carmichael, he takes the loss or cuts the plan. Now he is making his biggest bet yet: $100 billion toward AI data centres by 2035, with Google's Vizag hub at its centre. Whether it pays is unknown, but the pattern is familiar. __He is again betting that the thing everyone will need is the thing he already controls: land, power and wires.__ [114,117,125,95,123,122]
7.17 note: Same trick since Mundra: own the boring thing everyone will need.
7.18 h: What he got right and wrong
7.19 p: What he got right: he found one geographic advantage and kept stacking businesses on it. He moved faster than rivals, and he kept his word on deals, and he backed people through expensive mistakes. He turned a lost partner into a port, and in 2023 he chose survival over pride in a matter of days. [45,59,113,183]
7.20 p: What he got wrong: he signed a fixed price on a floating cost and nearly lost his power company. He built an Australian mine that shipped about seven years late, at one-sixth of its planned size. He leased land in Myanmar from a company that later fell under US sanctions. His flagship paid $275 million to settle over Iranian-origin gas, without admitting the allegations. Each time the group was big enough to survive. You will not be, so learn from his costs, not his recoveries. [171,95,125,201]

7.22 lesson: Own the thing beside the thing :: Every Adani business began by looking at what the last one needed. A port needs coal. Coal needs a plant. A plant needs wires. Wires need customers. If you are starting up, ask what your first customers must buy next, and whether you can be the one who sells it.
7.23 lesson: Make your debt boring before it makes you famous :: Part of the fear in 2023 came from share-backed loans. Borrowed money makes your rise faster, and your fall faster too. If a lender can force you to sell at the worst moment, you do not fully control your own company.

## C8 how-they-work | How he works | A Day Inside the Empire
8.0 h: The day, in his own words
8.1 p: The most detailed account of Gautam Adani's day did not come from a business magazine. It came from a TV audience. In January 2023, someone on India TV's show Aap Ki Adalat asked how a man with his schedule finds time for family. His answer started with travel: three days a week he is out of Ahmedabad. On the other four he reaches the office at about ==10:30 or 11 am==, because he got home at 11 or midnight the night before. [215,216]
8.2 p: The mornings are the surprise. He says he wakes at 6:30 and exercises for about an hour. Then he spends ==two to two and a half hours== at the dining table, reading newspapers and talking with his wife, children and granddaughter. He also has a rule for lunch: whichever family members are in the office eat together in the dining room. [215,216]
8.3 facts: 6:30 am = When he says he wakes up, followed by an hour of exercise [215] || 2–2.5 hrs = Newspapers and family talk at the table, before he leaves for work [215] || 3 days = Time he says he spends out of Ahmedabad each week [215] || 11–12 hrs = His office day, according to his biographer R.N. Bhaskar and India Today [217,218]
8.4 note: Notice the order. Body, family, newspapers. Only then the empire.
8.5 p: When he does get to work, he stays there. Bhaskar's 2022 book puts his day at eleven to twelve hours in the office, and India Today's profile says twelve hours on most days. Adani told India Today he does not really separate rest from work. [217,218]
8.6 quote: "I always tell people that when I am at work, I relax. Whenever I go on a holiday, I work too." -- Gautam Adani to India Today, 2022 [218]
8.7 p: In December 2024 India was arguing about Narayana Murthy's 70-hour work week. Adani told the news agency IANS that nobody can set this rule for anyone else. "Your work-life balance should not be imposed on me, and my work-life balance shouldn't be imposed on you," he said. Then came the line that went viral: if you spend eight hours with family, "biwi bhaag jayegi", your wife will run away. The joke got the headlines, but the sentence after it says more: "For us either it is family or work, we don't have a world out of this." [219,220]
8.8 note: Two worlds, no third. Decide honestly if you want that life.
8.9 h: What he says he does, and what others saw
8.10 quote: "I do not interfere in the day-to-day functioning of the companies. My role is limited to formulating strategy, capital allocation and performance review." -- Gautam Adani to India Today, 2022 [221,222]
8.11 p: He has told this story for a long time. In 2012 he told the Economic Times, "Delegation has never been a problem for me," and said he began handing off operating decisions back when Adani was only a trading firm. His long-time lieutenant Malay Mahadevia added one detail: Adani "reviews the functioning of the businesses every month" to check that the processes are not turning bureaucratic. Bhaskar's book adds another. __He delegates, yet he expects each person to report directly on progress__, and he steps in fast on a go or no-go call when a job gets stuck. [223,217]
8.12 p: Outsiders describe a tighter grip. Bloomberg editor Anto Antony, who spent nearly two weeks around the family, said he has essentially been the "super CEO", making all the decisions for the conglomerate. Bloomberg also reported, citing officials who were present, that in 2022 he took his son Karan to task in front of other executives over an unexpected $11 million quarterly loss at ACC. In 2024 he pulled up his son Jeet in a meeting over slow digital plans. [224,225]
8.13 p: Hindenburg Research, a short seller that stood to profit if the shares fell, quoted unnamed former executives saying that all key decisions flow back to him. Treat that as an interested party's claim. Still, the accounts fit together better than they first appear. __He hands over the running of things. He does not hand over where the money goes.__ [226,221]
8.14 note: Delegate the work. Keep the capital decisions. That is the real split.
8.15 example: For example: the Economic Times retold a story in 2012 that it openly called legend. In the early 1990s a commodities trader lost Adani Enterprises ==₹6 crore== in one day and offered to resign. Adani is believed to have told him, "You have learnt a lot today. Your learning cost me six crore rupees. Now you must stay and earn back that much." The tale goes that the trader did it in six months. We cannot confirm it happened, but it is the story his company chooses to tell about mistakes. [223]
8.16 h: How he hires
8.17 p: In 1997 Adani walked into the Mumbai Port Trust office about some consignments and offered its vice chairman, an IAS officer named Rajeeva Sinha, a job on his planned port at Mundra. Sinha said no, and thought it was a spur-of-the-moment offer. "In 1997, he couldn't get anyone to join him," Sinha recalled later. Adani remembers it differently. [223]
8.18 quote: "I had done my homework. I had heard Mr Sinha was passionate about ports." -- Gautam Adani to the Economic Times, 2012 [223]
8.19 p: Sinha joined ten years later as a director of Adani Ports. Meanwhile, nobody, it seemed, wanted to risk joining an unestablished Ahmedabad firm. So Adani asked his childhood friend Malay Mahadevia, who had just finished a dentistry degree, to spearhead the project. The Economic Times called Mahadevia, in many ways, Adani's alter ego. He is still listed on the group's leadership page, as a director of airports and healthcare. [223,227]
8.20 note: A dentist helped build Mundra. When no expert will come, pick someone you trust.
8.21 p: His hiring talks can run long. Vineet Jain, a CEO at Adani Power in 2012, remembered being invited to the Adani guest house in Delhi and discussing the power industry for four hours, until one in the morning. A week later Mahadevia called with the offer. In 2008 Adani told Mint that people were his biggest limit: "Give me people and I would do projects, just like that." That same year he mentioned the Adani Knowledge Centre, which he called a nursery for growing managers. [223,228]
8.22 p: Not every hire worked. Ravi Sharma ran telecom companies before he became CEO of Adani Power, with no power experience, and he left after about two years. We found no reliable on-record account of how he personally fires people, so we will not guess. [223]
8.23 h: What it is like to work for him
8.24 p: A warning first: most on-record voices are people who worked for him and speak well of him, so read them as friendly witnesses. Vinay Prakash, who joined in 2001, said in 2012, "Gautambhai gives us his dreams and however big they may be, he wants us to achieve them." He also described a culture that lets people take risks, make mistakes and get a second chance. [223,229]
8.25 p: Jain told the Economic Times he is a fan but not a flatterer: "I disagree and argue with him." He said Adani answers email and SMS very quickly even when away. Then he gave the most useful advice anyone has given about this man. [223]
8.26 quote: "You have to be very direct and to-the-point when you communicate with Gautambhai. He's got a very short attention span." -- Vineet Jain, then CEO at Adani Power, 2012 [223]
8.27 note: Lead with your one line. Every investor you pitch will thank you.
8.28 p: There is a softer route too. Prakash said he called Adani's brother Rajesh more often than Gautam: "Rajeshbhai is softer, less aggressive." [223]

## C9 mindset | What he believes | Eight Beliefs, Tested
9.0 p: Anyone can print values on a wall. What matters is what a person does when the value costs something. Here are eight beliefs Adani keeps repeating, each with a real moment beside it. Some moments flatter him. Some are more complicated, and we say so. [215,233]
9.1 h: 1. There is no shortcut, so stop looking for one
9.2 p: On Aap Ki Adalat the host asked him for his formula for making money. He said there is no maths, chemistry or physics to it, only "hard work, hard work and hard work". The host then mentioned a story from his friends: that while the Mundra land was being developed, he got up at 4 am and went there daily. He did not deny it, and he shared the credit. [215]
9.3 quote: "The success of any man depends on honesty and hard work, and the rest is left to God; that's the only formula." -- Gautam Adani, Aap Ki Adalat, January 2023 [215]
9.4 h: 2. Do not carry what you cannot control
9.5 p: He has been held for ransom and trapped in a terror attack, and we tell those stories in the next chapter. What is striking is what he says about the nights after them. "I was kidnapped and released the next day," he told the same show. "I tell you, that I slept well that very night." He told India Today that even on traumatic days he sleeps peacefully. [215,221]
9.6 quote: "When things are not in your control, why worry? Destiny decides such things." -- Gautam Adani to India Today, 2022 [221]
9.7 note: Most founders lose more sleep than money. Practise this one early.
9.8 h: 3. Your word is worth more than your legal right
9.9 p: He says his father's rule was that a promise must always be kept. The group's biggest test of this came on 1 February 2023. Adani Enterprises had just closed a ==₹20,000 crore== share sale, fully subscribed, and it could legally have kept the money. But the stock had crashed well below the offer price, so the board returned the cash. In August 2026 he called this the clearest expression of his philosophy. [233,234,235]
9.10 quote: "Trust is greater than any legal right." -- Gautam Adani to Adani Group staff, August 2026 [233]
9.11 p: Be fair about the other side of it. Keeping the money would have meant investors holding shares at more than double the market price, as PTI noted, and that would have done its own damage. __So this was both a principled move and a smart one.__ The two often go together, and that does not make it less real. [234]
9.12 h: 4. Build what your country is short of
9.13 p: He says the idea came from growing up in the 1970s and 80s, when people had to struggle for basic services. He watched China, which he notes had a lower per capita income in 1990, leap ahead on infrastructure while India waited. When the 1991 reforms opened the door to private players, he went through it. [222]
9.14 quote: "I was born in a middle-class family and I lived through the 1970s and 80s when we had to struggle for bijli, sadak and paani." -- Gautam Adani to India Today, December 2022 [222]
9.15 p: He also treats this as strategy. Aligning with national aims, he says, "gives our activities the required tailwind that makes for smooth sailing". Critics hear something else: a business empire that rises along with one political leader. His companies and the Modi government have consistently denied favouritism. Both things can be true of the same strategy, and we look at this again in the chapter on his public image. [221,236]
9.16 h: 5. Connect the dots
9.17 p: His favourite word is "adjacencies", and the chain is easy to follow once you see it. The Mundra port brought in coal, and coal plus Mundra's land led to power. Power led to transmission and renewables. Bringing in edible oil for Wilmar led to a 50:50 venture. And cement came next, because power plants produce fly ash that cement can use as feedstock. [221]
9.18 note: Do not ask what is hot. Ask what your last business makes possible.
9.19 h: 6. Know the limit of your risk, then jump
9.20 p: He says diamonds taught him this as a teenager, with no safety net, and Bhaskar's book tells a story from those years. Young Gautam sold ==₹1.5 lakh== of stones on a spoken promise, told nobody in the office, and went home on holiday. The trader then stopped answering calls. So Gautam and his cousin Girish went to the man's house at 6 am, arriving when the milk boy rang the bell, and left with other stones in settlement. They made very little money on the deal. [237,217]
9.21 quote: "In this field, hesitation is the difference between winning and losing." -- Gautam Adani, Jaipur, November 2024 [237]
9.22 p: A young trader's nerve is not the same as a plan, and he knows it. "When you take risks, you need to know its limit," he told the same TV audience, adding that he has failed in many places too. He added a harder idea in Jaipur: "Too much of an attachment to outcomes limits your ability to challenge the status quo." __Bold on the decision, calm about the result.__ [215,237]
9.23 h: 7. Scrutiny is the price of big dreams
9.24 p: Ten days after US prosecutors' charges became public, he stood up at a jewellery awards night in Jaipur. He listed his crises like a scorecard: the Australian mine, the short seller, the US case. "Every attack makes us stronger," he said. In June 2026 he told shareholders, "We did not bend. We did not pause." [237,238,231]
9.25 quote: "The more bold your dreams, the more the world will scrutinize you." -- Gautam Adani, Jaipur, November 2024 [237]
9.26 p: Take this belief carefully. It works when the critics are wrong and it blinds you when they are right. To his credit, in August 2026 he also thanked those who questioned the group, saying criticism of any intent offers a chance for introspection. In the "what went wrong" chapter you will see a case where ignoring early warnings cost $275 million. [233,201]
9.27 note: Some critics are noise. Some are your free audit. Learn to tell them apart.
9.28 h: 8. Wisdom needs knowledge too
9.29 p: This one is for every student reading. Asked by the TV host if dropping out was the secret, he said no, firmly. He called education very important and said missing it was his misfortune. His own formula is plain: "While education equips you with knowledge, it's life's experiences that give you wisdom. Knowledge combined with wisdom ensures success." [215,221]
9.30 quote: "I sometimes wonder that had I had the education along with this hard work and experience, then I would've become much bigger than the current Gautam Adani." -- Gautam Adani, Aap Ki Adalat, January 2023 [215]
9.31 p: An audience member said many people want to become like him. His reply is worth copying down. Find what actually interests you and follow it, he said: "You should have the liking for it, not like I saw him, I want to become like him." [215]
9.32 note: The man himself says do not copy him. Read that again.

## C11 the-brand | The public image | The Quiet Man Everyone Argues About
11.0 h: From invisible to inescapable
11.1 p: For most of his career Adani's brand was that he had no brand. The BBC noted in 2022 that he often describes himself as low-profile and rarely talks about the crises of his life, apart from once telling a journalist that "two or three very unfortunate incidents have happened in my life". Bloomberg's editor said that until 2023 many saw the group as opaque, with little known about how it worked inside. [236,224]
11.2 p: The group wrapped itself in a single idea instead of one man's personality: "Nation Building" and "Growth with Goodness". Those phrases are the guiding philosophy on its website, and they appear in almost everything it publishes. His own speeches take the same tone. He rarely sells a product. He talks about India's century and his part in it. [236,233,240]

11.4 h: The courtroom on TV

11.18 h: How India sees him
11.19 p: In India he is not one thing. To admirers he is a first-generation builder. He leans on that image when he says he knows the mindset of the person on a scooter, a rickshaw or a bus. ## C12 what-went-wrong | The hard chapter | What Went Wrong, and What It Cost
12.0 p: Read this chapter slowly and precisely. Adani has been accused of many things and has denied them, and several high-profile cases have ended without any finding of guilt. Others ended in settlements, where people pay without admitting or denying. __A young reader should learn to tell apart an allegation, a settlement, a regulator's finding and a conviction.__ Each is different, and we keep them separate below. [264,265,266]

12.2 h: The first brush: the market ban
12.3 p: In May 2007, SEBI ruled on trading in Adani Exports shares from 1999 to 2001. It found that promoter entities had aided and abetted firms linked to Ketan Parekh, the market operator at the centre of that scam, in manipulating the stock. It barred seven promoter entities from the market for two years. The promoters' defence was that they sold shares to raise money for Mundra and did not know who was buying. SEBI said the same buyers on nearly every share could not be a coincidence. [267,268]
12.4 p: The tribunal stayed the ban after about 50 days, and in April 2008 the case ended through consent terms: one entity paid ₹10.5 lakh and others ₹7.5 lakh each. Within a week the group filed papers for the Adani Power share sale. Hindenburg later described the outcome as bans reduced to token fines, and the group says the matter was closed and disclosed. You can read that in two ways: a clean exit or a cheap one. Either way, the shadow followed him for 15 years. [269,270,277,272]
12.5 note: A cheap settlement in 2008 can still be a headline in 2023.
12.6 h: The Belekeri allegations
12.7 p: In July 2011 Karnataka's anti-corruption ombudsman, the Lokayukta headed by Justice Hegde, published its report on illegal mining. It said Adani Enterprises had paid bribes for undue favour in exporting iron ore through Belekeri port, and it recommended blacklisting the firm. The group's own later account is that a special investigation team found the allegations false, filed a closure report, and that a Lokayukta court accepted it in July 2017. That closure detail rests on the company's document, so we report it as its account. [271,272]
12.8 h: The coal mine that swallowed a decade
12.9 p: He bought the Carmichael project in Queensland in 2010, planning up to 60 million tonnes a year. Activists fought it for seven years, and lenders, insurers and engineering firms walked away. The first cargo shipped only in December 2021, from a mine with capacity for 10 million tonnes. He calls it a sign of resilience. Then in Jaipur in 2024, he gave a rare honest number about what it cost. [256,237]
12.10 quote: "The fact is that 100% equity funding took away over 30 billion dollars of debt financing from our green energy projects." -- Gautam Adani, Jaipur, November 2024 [237]
12.11 p: He says the mine took about ==$10 billion== of the group's own equity. That is his framing and his sum, and the activists would tell the story very differently. There is also a complaint on file: Reuters reported in 2024 that an Aboriginal group filed a complaint with Australia's Human Rights Commission accusing the coal unit of racism. __When a project gets too hot for lenders, the real bill arrives in the projects you can no longer fund.__ [237,256]
12.12 h: The short seller and the share sale
12.13 p: On 24 January 2023 Hindenburg Research accused the group of "the largest con in corporate history", alleging stock manipulation and accounting fraud through offshore entities. It also said it had taken a position that would profit if the shares fell, and the group called the report baseless. The timing was brutal, as Adani Enterprises was in the middle of a ₹20,000 crore share sale. By 2 February the group had lost about ==$108 billion== in market value, and his own fortune was down by tens of billions. [235,224,234,266]
12.14 quote: "For me, the interest of my investors is paramount and everything is secondary." -- Gautam Adani, video message, 2 February 2023 [278,234]
12.15 p: What happened next is worth studying: the sale's proceeds went back. Group promoters prepaid ==$2.15 billion== of share-backed loans in about six weeks, plus $500 million of Ambuja acquisition debt. They sold ==$1.87 billion== of stock to the US investor GQG Partners. They slowed acquisitions and made hard choices on capex, and Business Today reported net debt to EBITDA falling to 3.27 times from 3.81. Reuters reported that the shares have since recovered. [279,249,280,266]
12.16 note: Loans against your own shares make a bad week far more dangerous.
12.17 p: Then the verdicts, which need care. In January 2024 the Supreme Court disposed of the petitions and told SEBI to finish its last two probes. On 18 September 2025, two SEBI orders found that the charges they examined, about money routed through three companies, were not established. All the loans were repaid with interest, and at that time the law did not count such indirect deals as related-party transactions. [281,282,276,275]
12.18 p: Reuters headlined this as SEBI dismissing the allegations. The Indian Express also pointed out what the orders did not address: low public float, foreign portfolio investors and the offshore links of his brother Vinod Adani. An earlier Supreme Court panel had found no conclusive evidence of price manipulation but flagged opacity around such investors. [266,282]
12.19 p: So be careful with the phrase "clean chit". It is accurate for the charges decided, and less clearly so for everything Hindenburg alleged. Hindenburg itself shut down in January 2025, and the group's shares went on to recover. [282,283,266]
12.20 h: The US case
12.21 p: In November 2024 US prosecutors charged him, his nephew Sagar and six others over an alleged scheme to pay about ==$265 million== in bribes to Indian state officials for solar contracts, and to hide it from American investors. Arrest warrants were issued for him and Sagar. The group called the claims baseless. It also pointed out that he faced three fraud-related counts and was not charged in the count of conspiring to violate the US foreign bribery law. The Hindu noted that the indictment still names him in describing the alleged scheme. [273,274,284]
12.22 p: The damage was immediate. Adani Green dropped a $600 million bond, and Kenya scrapped deals worth over $2.5 billion. TotalEnergies paused new investments and said it had not been told of the investigation, which is a painful thing for a partner to say in public. Sri Lanka began reviewing its projects, and Norway's fund later excluded Adani Green. Adani himself never appeared in a US court. [283,164,285,165,260,264]
12.23 note: Notice who moved first: partners and lenders, long before any court.
12.24 p: Then the US government changed its mind. In May 2026, after Adani hired a legal team led by Sullivan & Cromwell's Robert Giuffra, who is also a personal lawyer to President Trump, the Justice Department asked to drop the case. Judge Nicholas Garaufis first refused to accept a "terse, bland, and conclusory" request. The department replied that the alleged conduct was centred in India, that no investor had lost money, and that the case "should have been dropped a year ago -- or never brought in the first place". [264,286,287]
12.25 p: On ==10 August 2026== the judge dismissed the charges with prejudice, meaning they cannot be brought again. His 47-page order examined whether Adani's $10 billion US investment pledge had played a role, and concluded it had not. But he said a senior official appeared to have acted without meaningful input from the prosecutors and investigators who built the case. Adani said he accepted the result with humility, and that his faith in the rule of law remained unwavering. [264,33]
12.26 quote: "The irregularities in the decision to dismiss the indictment are concerning." -- US District Judge Nicholas Garaufis, order of 10 August 2026, as quoted by the BBC [264]
12.27 p: Be exact about what this means. __No court ever ruled on whether the bribery happened, in either direction.__ A separate civil case ended the same day with a consent judgment: he pays ==$6 million== and Sagar ==$12 million==, without admitting or denying the allegations, and both are barred from future violations of certain US securities laws. [265,264,287]
12.28 note: Dismissed is not disproved, and settled is not guilty. Learn these words.
12.29 h: The cargo no one questioned
12.30 p: This one is smaller and more instructive, because it reads like a textbook. In 2023 Adani Enterprises entered the Indian LPG market. Facing strong rivals, it needed discounted gas, and a Dubai trader offered it, said to be from Oman and Iraq. According to the US Treasury, the gas was actually Iranian, and from November 2023 to June 2025 the company bought 35 cargoes, with 32 payments totalling about ==$192 million== going through US banks. [201]
12.31 example: For example: look at the warning signs the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control lists. The first cargo was refrigerated propane said to be loaded at Sohar, Oman, where no facility for exporting it existed at the time. Certificates of origin had illogical numbering. Ships switched off their tracking or faked their location. Third parties raised concerns about Iranian origin at least four times. And the price looked too good, in a region where Iran, almost alone, sold at such a discount. [201]
12.32 p: On 18 May 2026 the company agreed to pay ==$275 million==, against a legal maximum of about $384 million. Treasury called the apparent violations egregious and not voluntarily disclosed, while crediting remedial steps and cooperation. The company's checks did find no sanctioned names, and none of its documents explicitly showed Iranian origin. The settlement itself contains no finding of fault. But the lesson is simple, and it is expensive: __a checklist is not the same as curiosity.__ [201,202]
12.33 facts: ~$150 bn = Market value lost at the worst point after Hindenburg. The shares later recovered [266] || $275 mn = Paid by Adani Enterprises to settle Iran-related sanctions liability [201] || $18 mn = Civil penalties paid by Gautam and Sagar Adani in the SEC case [265] || $2.5 bn+ = Kenyan deals cancelled after the US charges [164]
12.34 h: Fighting the critics
12.35 p: There is one more fight a young founder should think about. In September 2025 Adani Enterprises won a one-sided court order restraining five journalists and unknown others from publishing what the judge called "unverified, unsubstantiated" and defamatory reports. The company had argued they were aligned with "anti-India interests". The government then sent takedown notices. On appeal, one judge set the order aside for four journalists, and another said the fifth need not follow it until he was heard. [288,289,290]
12.36 p: He may feel wronged, and courts exist for that. But a giant company asking a court to silence reporters rarely looks like strength. This is our view, not a legal finding. __You win more trust by publishing the answer than by removing the question.__ [289]
12.37 h: What not to copy
12.38 p: Here is our own list, drawn from the record above. Do not borrow against your own shares, because a falling price then turns into a margin call on your life. Do not let one political relationship become your whole public story. Do not treat a compliance team as a box to tick. [279,224,201]
12.39 p: Do not leave partners to learn about your legal trouble from the papers, as TotalEnergies said it did. And do not assume an expensive legal team will rescue you. Most founders will never have that option. [285,264]

## C13 today | Where he stands now | Sixty-Four, and Not Done
13.0 p: As of November 2026 Gautam Adani is 64 and chairman of the Adani Group, with four younger relatives taking on more of the operations. Forbes put him back at ==No. 1== on its India's 100 Richest list that came out on 1 October, up $20.7 billion to ==$112.7 billion== which he shares with his family. Mukesh Ambani fell to second place at $86.3 billion. Forbes also noted that his fortune is still below the $150 billion peak he hit in 2022. [291,292,225]
13.1 facts: $112.7 bn = Family fortune on Forbes' India list, valued at 11 Sept 2026 prices [292] || ₹1.5 lakh cr = Group investment in FY26, which he says is over 30% of India's new private capex [231,262] || ₹2 lakh cr = CFO's investment target for the current year [293] || 70 = The age he told Bloomberg he plans to step down, around 2032 [224]
13.2 h: What he is betting on
13.3 p: The theme he repeats now is that infrastructure and intelligence are becoming one business. In his May 2026 letter to shareholders he summed it up in one line: "Before AI can think, energy must flow." So the plan is to supply the things AI needs. Forbes says the group is investing $100 billion in AI infrastructure. At the June 2026 AGM he named a 3 GW data centre platform by 2030 and a gigawatt-scale Google campus in Visakhapatnam. [294,291,262]
13.4 p: Then the rest of the list, at his usual scale. He is targeting ==10 GW== of nuclear power by 2035 through a new company, Adani Atomic Energy, which is more than the 8 GW India has today. He wants 45 GW of Adani Power capacity within five years, 5,000 MW of hydropower with Bhutan, and one billion tonnes a year through Adani Ports by 2030. He also has an aerospace and defence push. In October 2026 Adani Enterprises reported its best half-year ever, with EBITDA up 56% to ₹11,958 crore. [262,295,283,231,296]
13.5 p: The numbers go further still. In October 2026 CFO Robbie Singh said he wants the group able to invest up to ==$100 billion a year== by 2033, and that 72% of forward capex now comes from its own cash flow. "India is a tremendous opportunity and it is for us to lose," Singh said. "And I do not want to lose." __That dependence on its own cash, not on pledged shares, is the clearest sign of what 2023 taught them.__ [293]
13.6 note: ₹2 lakh crore this year. The 2033 goal: nearly five times that.
13.7 h: The project he says is closest to him
13.8 p: The one he talks about differently is Dharavi, Mumbai's huge informal settlement, home to about a million people. In his May 2026 speech he called it perhaps among the most challenging tasks of his life and the one closest to his heart. "We did not make this decision for the sake of profit," he told staff, calling Dharavi a symbol of "our collective failure". Hold him to that line as the project progresses. It runs through a company with the Maharashtra government as co-shareholder, and the first test is whether residents end up better off. [240,297]
13.9 p: Work has begun. In mid-October 2026 a bhoomi poojan marked the start of rehabilitation construction at a 22-acre railway site in Matunga, with 10 towers and 10,432 homes due in phases by August 2030. Pranav Adani told Fortune India the plan is about 200,000 houses in total and $4–5 billion in the first phase. Meeting Michael Bloomberg that month, Adani wrote that Dharavi should be judged on housing, sanitation, electricity and residents' economic security. [297,250]
13.10 h: After the storm
13.11 p: His tone in 2026 has been part victory lap and part sermon. He called the ₹25,000 crore rights issue in Adani Enterprises "a referendum on our credibility", and told shareholders, "We built when it was hardest to build." In August, days after the US dismissal, he told more than three lakh staff and partners that the experience left him with humility rather than triumph. He asked them what their generation will be remembered for. [283,233]
13.12 p: The legal cloud has lifted but the questions have not all gone. The judge's concerns are on the record, Norway's exclusion list still carried his companies in August, and Bangladesh's dispute was unresolved at last report. At the same time, money keeps coming. He tops Forbes' India list and is building faster than ever. Whether that is vindication or just survival is an argument people in India will keep having for years. [264,260,150,293,292]
13.13 note: A closed case is not the same as a settled argument.
13.14 h: What he says he wants next
13.15 p: The clearest answer is about leaving. In early 2018 he invited his sons Karan and Jeet and his nephews Pranav and Sagar to lunch and asked if they wanted to split the group and part ways. He gave them three months. They came back and said they would stay together. Today, Bloomberg reports, four heirs split the businesses and even shared services such as HR and finance, with no first among equals, once he steps down at 70. [224,225]
13.16 quote: "Succession is not a one-off event but it is a long term journey. And my plan of succession started almost a decade ago." -- Gautam Adani to Bloomberg, 2024 [224]
13.17 p: Not everyone is convinced. Wharton professor Raphael Amit told Bloomberg that having no clear leader could become a drawback, unless there are well-defined roles and processes for resolving conflict. That is the real open question of his final chapter. He ended his August 2026 address by calling for institutions that outlast individuals. __The hardest thing he will ever build is a company that does not need him.__ [225,233]
13.18 note: He asked at 55, not 75. Start your succession thinking absurdly early.
L0 Do your homework before the handshake. :: In 1997 he offered a port job to Rajeeva Sinha, who assumed it was an impulse. It wasn't. Adani had already learned that Sinha loved ports and feared being transferred away from them, so he offered what the man really wanted.
L1 Hire for trust when you cannot hire for experience. :: When no experienced person would risk joining Mundra, he asked a childhood friend who was a newly qualified dentist. Malay Mahadevia learned the job and stayed for decades. Skills can be learned, and character mostly cannot.
L2 Delegate the work, keep the capital. :: By his own account his job is strategy, capital allocation and performance review. His monthly business reviews keep him close while CEOs run operations. Early on, decide which decisions only you can make, and hand off the rest.
L3 Get to the point. :: Vineet Jain's advice to future Adani CEOs was to be very direct, because the boss has "a very short attention span". Most powerful people are the same. Put your one best line first, whether in an email, a pitch or a meeting.
L4 Protect your mornings. :: With one of the world's largest fortunes, he says he still gives two hours or more each morning to exercise, newspapers and family before reaching office. Then he works late. Decide what comes first, or your calendar will decide for you.
L5 Do not lose sleep over what you cannot control. :: He says he slept well the night he came home from a 1998 kidnapping, and that he does not get worked up even on traumatic days. You will rarely face anything close to that. Save your energy for what you can actually change.
L6 Thank the people who save you. :: Trapped at the Taj on 26/11, he was led to safety by waiters and staff, and he still praises their dedication years later. Your culture shows on the worst night, not on a poster. Build the kind of team that would do the same for your customers.
L7 Connect the dots from what you already own. :: A port brought coal, coal and land led to power, and power plants' fly ash fed into cement. He calls these adjacencies. Before chasing the hot new sector, ask what your last business has made easy for you.
L8 Stay in school if you possibly can. :: The most famous dropout in Indian business told a national TV audience that missing his education was his misfortune. He said he might have been much bigger with it. Do not use his story as your excuse.
L9 Give the money back when it feels wrong. :: In February 2023 he returned a fully subscribed ₹20,000 crore share sale after the stock crashed. It hurt then, and he now calls it the clearest proof of his values. Keeping your word in a bad week buys more trust than any advertisement.
L10 Never let your shares become your collateral. :: After Hindenburg, promoters rushed to prepay $2.15 billion of share-backed loans in about six weeks to remove the risk of margin calls. A loan against your own stock can turn a falling price into a fight for survival. Borrow against cash flow instead.
L11 If the deal looks too cheap, ask why. :: Adani Enterprises bought discounted gas with clean-looking papers, and paid $275 million to settle when the US Treasury concluded it was Iranian. The red flags were the price, the ships and the documents. A checklist is not a substitute for curiosity.
L12 Tell your partners first. :: When the US charges landed in 2024, TotalEnergies paused new investments and said publicly that it had not been told of the investigation. Bad news ages badly. Your partners should hear it from you, and quickly.
L13 Don't confuse a dismissed case with a clean record. :: His US criminal case ended with prejudice in August 2026, yet the judge wrote that the process was concerning and no court ever ruled on the facts. A win on paper does not end the public question. Build a record that does not need a lawyer to explain it.
L14 Plan your exit while you are still strong. :: He sat his sons and nephews down in 2018, in his fifties, and gave them three months to decide whether to stay together. Most founders avoid that talk until it is too late. Ask early what your company looks like without you.
