# Story 3: Airbnb, research dossier

**Track 1: "Everyone Saw It. Few Decoded It."** Framework: the Problem Iceberg
**Segment length:** about 2 minutes
**Researched:** 26 Oct 2026. Live figures (market cap, listings) are dated where they appear. Re-check them before final lock.

**Confidence labels**
- **VERIFIED**: 2+ independent reliable sources, or a primary source plus independent corroboration
- **SINGLE-SOURCE**: one reliable source only
- **DISPUTED**: credible sources disagree (explained)
- **MYTH**: widely repeated but wrong or unsupported

> Bottom line for the script: the most dramatic beats are also among the best documented. These include the $40 cereal, the "cockroaches" line, Paul Graham's "what's wrong with them?", the door-to-door photo trips in New York, the $150K-for-10% rejections, the Andreessen "add a zero" moment, the Stanford trust study, and Chesky going speechless on IPO day. The softest spots are the exact cereal totals, the exact rent figure, the exact $200 to $400 weekly revenue numbers, and any claim of a Secret Service angle (no source found).

## G. Problem Iceberg decode

| Depth | What it was | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **1. What people say** (visible complaint) | "Hotels are sold out or too expensive." For the founders it was simpler still: "We can't pay rent." | Conference hotels sold out (S-1; Gebbia, TED). Denver had about 28K rooms for about 80K DNC visitors (HuffPost). Amol couldn't afford a hotel after a ~$1,000 conference ticket (NextShark). Chesky had $1,000 against $1,150 rent (Stanford GSB). |
| **2. What people do** (the workaround) | Crash with friends, use couches, CouchSurfing, Craigslist sublets. The founders themselves inflated airbeds. The workaround market already existed but felt sketchy. Investors judged the workaround ("strangers sleeping on floors"), not the need. | Gallagher's site copy: "like Craigslist & Couch surfing.com, but classier". Politico 2008: Denver residents listing rooms on Craigslist. Fred Wilson couldn't see airbeds as "the next hotel room". The S-1 says people told them "Strangers will never stay in each other's homes." |
| **3. Why it really happens** (root cause) | (a) **Strangers equal danger:** a deep, childhood-taught bias with no trust infrastructure between strangers. (b) **Information gap:** bad photos meant guests "couldn't see what [they] were paying for", so risk felt even higher. (c) Founders sitting at desks building for "scale" instead of meeting users. | Gebbia, TED: "strangers equal danger". Gebbia, First Round: "the photos sucked". Graham: "Go to your users." Stanford/PNAS: homophily drives trust judgments. |
| **4. What people actually want** (hidden desire) | **Trust and safety** (the dignity of opening your home without fear). **Belonging and connection** ("make friends while also making rent"). **Control** for hosts (choose guests, a guarantee, see reviews). **Status/identity** for guests: a local, authentic trip instead of a "fast food" hotel. | Gebbia's TED: reputation beats similarity; "connection beyond the transaction". Amol: "the real story is about much more than saving money". The 2011 EJ crisis made the hidden need explicit, and the response was the $50K, then $1M, guarantee, ID verification and 24/7 support (Masters of Scale; TechCrunch 2012). |

**One-line decode for the video:** *People said "hotels are expensive." Investors heard "no one sleeps in a stranger's home." The founders decoded "people will, if they can see it and trust it", so they fixed the photos by hand and designed trust into every screen.*

## I. Myths and corrections

| Claim in circulation | Status | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| "Amol Surve was **the** first Airbnb guest." | **Partly true** | He was one of the first three (with Michael and Kat). Airbnb lists the three without order. "First" is his own framing, widely repeated. Say "one of the first three" or "who calls himself guest No. 1". |
| "Guests paid **$20 a night**." (Vox, 2016) | **MYTH / misreading** | Gebbia said they slept on "a **20-dollar airbed**". The price was **$80 a night** per guest. |
| "The cereal raised **$40,000**." | **MYTH** | That's $40 × 1,000, but Cap'n McCain's never sold out. Best supported: **about $30K** (Chesky), or $20–30K (Gallagher). |
| "They sold **800** / **1,000+** boxes." | **Unclear / overstated** | 1,000 boxes were *made*. All 500 Obama O's sold. The McCain count is unknown. |
| "**Secret Service** / Obama campaign intervened over the cereal." | **UNVERIFIED** (no source found) | Do not use. |
| "There was breakfast." | **Contradictory** | Gebbia remembers cooking ham-and-Swiss omelettes (TED). Wikipedia mentions Pop-Tarts. Amol told Tech in Asia there was "no real bed… and no breakfast either". *A fun "even the breakfast is disputed" aside, not a fact to assert.* |
| "The conference was IDSA" vs "it was ICSID" | **Both** | It was the joint **ICSID/IDSA World Design Congress "Connecting '07"**, Oct 17–20, 2007. |
| "Rent was $1,150" vs "$1,050" | **Minor dispute** | Chesky himself says $1,150. Guy Raz's book says $1,050 was Chesky's share. Attribute it to Chesky. |
| "Seven investors rejected them." | **True but nuanced** | Five rejected and **two never replied**. Other accounts give 15 or 20 investors, likely different batches. |
| "Revenue was **exactly** $200/week, then $400." | **Single-source dollar figures** | The doubling is corroborated (Gebbia to BI 2012, HBS case). The $ amounts come from First Round's account of a Gebbia talk. Attribute them. |
| "The photo fix happened in **summer 2009**." (HBS case) | **Timing dispute** | Graham's Jan 26, 2009 email already describes the founders "on the ground in NYC", and Chesky says they commuted to NY *during* YC (Jan–Mar 2009). Most likely the NYC visits began in YC and the photo push carried into 2009–2010. Safe wording: "In 2009, during Y Combinator…" |
| "YC funded them because of the idea." | **Reversed** | Graham doubted the idea. The cereal (proof of grit) convinced him. Graham later wrote he was "genuinely worried" they wouldn't raise money after Demo Day. |
| "Credit-card debt was $20K" vs "$25K" | **Minor dispute** | Chesky: $25K (himself). Gallagher: about $20K each. Say "over $20,000 each". |
| "10% would be worth $10B today." | **Misleading without caveat** | Simple undiluted maths only. Later rounds would have diluted the stake. Say "on paper". |
| "Andreessen added a zero ($5K to $50K)." | **Likely true, book-sourced** | Reported in Gallagher and Stone (via excerpts and summaries). Confirm the page reference before airing. |
| "Gallagher says the first guests were a 30-year-old Indian, a 35-year-old from Utah, a 45-year-old from New York." | **MYTH** (an AI-summary garble) | The correct profiles (WSJ 2009 + Gallagher) are a woman designer from Boston (30s), a father of five from Utah (40s), and Amol, an Indian-born ASU graduate. |
