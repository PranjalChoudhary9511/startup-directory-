# 01 — Tata Nano: Research Dossier

**Track 1: "Everyone Saw It. Few Decoded It."** (Problem Iceberg). This is the primary story.
**Tone:** a tribute, not a takedown. Ratan Tata died on 9 Oct 2024, aged 86.
**Research date:** 26 Oct 2026. Every source URL below was opened or returned in search during this research pass.

**Confidence labels used:**
- **VERIFIED**: 2 or more independent, reliable sources (or the person's own words in 2+ venues).
- **SINGLE-SOURCE**: one reliable source only. Usable, but attribute it on screen ("according to...").
- **DISPUTED**: sources conflict. The note explains how.
- **MYTH**: commonly told, but wrong or exaggerated.

> **Biggest correction to our brief:** the origin story is not one fixed moment. Ratan Tata told it at least **four different ways** (Bangalore near-miss on a rainy day; a family of four on a bike in heavy *Bombay* rain; a general pattern of families on scooters; and the 2022 Instagram version). See section I. The safest script line: *"He kept seeing families of four on one scooter, often on slippery roads. One day, one of them fell in front of his car."*
>
> **Second big correction:** the ₹1 lakh price was **not a number he planned**. In his own telling, it came from a Financial Times headline after the Geneva Motor Show. He nearly issued a rebuttal, then adopted it as a target. In Jan 2008 he called it *"media speculation that we accepted as a challenge."*

## E. Top 15 "wow" details (ranked for a 17-year-old)

1. **The ₹1 lakh price was an accident.** Tata said "about $2,500" in a newspaper interview. The FT made it the headline, and he nearly published a correction. Instead he made it the goal, and his engineers were "aghast". (Tata Q&A; Tribune 2008.) *Reversal: the bold vision was a misquote he chose to honour.*
2. **His first idea wasn't a car at all.** He doodled safer scooters during boring board meetings, then a doorless buggy with rain curtains. "I finally decided it should be a car." (Cornell 2011; Instagram 2022.)
3. **The world's most talked-about car was built by people barely older than our viewers' older siblings.** The team averaged 25–30, and the leader was reportedly 32. That leader, Girish Wagh, became MD & CEO of Tata Motors' commercial-vehicle company in Oct 2025. (BusinessWeek; Storyboard18.)
4. **They moved an entire, nearly finished factory across India.** It took 3,340 truckloads (per Wagh), and the new plant was built in 14 months (Singur had taken 28). (eNidhi notes of Wagh; Tata press kit.)
5. **A one-word SMS changed India's industrial map.** "Suswagatam" (or "welcome") from Gujarat's CM. Two years later Modi said: "what a Re 1 SMS can do". (IANS; Tribune.)
6. **"Gun to my head."** In 2006 Tata said he wouldn't leave Singur even at gunpoint. In 2008: "I think Ms Banerjee has pulled the trigger." In 2022 Mamata said, "I did not drive out the Tatas." (UNI; Outlook; EastMojo.)
7. **The first prototype was "an embarrassment"** (Wagh). It had bars instead of doors and plastic flaps for the monsoon. Failure forced them to build a real car. (BusinessWeek.)
8. **2 lakh people applied in 17 days, and a computer lottery decided who could buy India's cheapest car.** (Wharton.)
9. **But the people who bought it weren't the scooter family.** For half the buyers it was a *second car*, and 75% of sales were in five big cities. (Wharton 2011.) *This is the iceberg in one statistic.*
10. **Real scooter families felt too intimidated to walk into car showrooms,** so Tata had to open 289 special "Nano Access Points". (Wharton.) *That is dignity, depth 4, in action.*
11. **The "people's car" didn't get a TV ad until 17 Dec 2010,** 21 months after launch. When it came, it showed a grandmother putting a kohl dot (nazar) on the car. (Wharton.)
12. **Its launch slogan, "Now You Can", was borrowed from Barack Obama's "Yes We Can".** (Outlook 2009.)
13. **The man himself owned the mistake.** "It became termed as a cheapest car by the public and, I am sorry to say, by ourselves." (CNBC 2013.) Rare honesty from a founder.
14. **From 10,000 a month to exactly one.** In June 2018 the factory built one Nano all month, and in all of 2019 India bought one Nano. (Bloomberg/Ada Derana; PTI.)
15. **The "failed" Nano factory now builds Tata's electric cars.** Sanand crossed 10 lakh cars by 2024, Tata bought Ford's plant next door, and a whole auto hub grew around it. And at 84, India's most respected industrialist still rode to the Taj in a Nano, without bodyguards. (Wikipedia/Autocar; Tribune 2022.)

**Bonus (strong but secondary):**
- 2008 was the same year as the Nano unveiling, the JLR buyout, the Singur exit and the 26/11 attack on Tata's own Taj hotel. The Nano launch press conference was many journalists' first event at the Taj after 26/11. (Autocar Pro 2008; Outlook 2009.)
- 8% shorter than a Maruti 800 but 21% roomier (company claim). The engine sits under the rear seat. 3 nuts per wheel, 1 wiper, and a boot you couldn't open from outside.
- The car was lengthened 100 mm at the last minute so a six-foot-plus chairman could get out gracefully on stage.
- At the unveiling, Tata said: "I am somewhat sad that Indians are becoming the greatest critics."
- The Supreme Court later said the land should never have been taken, and Tata later won ₹766 crore plus interest. Both sides were in some sense proven right, and the bill is still being fought in 2026.

## F. Story beats and reversals (emotional arc)

**Act 1: The Seeing (HIGH: empathy)**
- Open cold on rain, a scooter, a child sandwiched between parents. *Line:* "Millions of Indians saw this every day. One man couldn't stop thinking about it."
- **Expect X:** a genius instantly imagines a car. **Get Y:** he spends years doodling safer *scooters* in boring board meetings; the car came last.
- Stakes: about 1.2 lakh road deaths a year then. Today two-wheeler riders are about 45% of India's road deaths. The problem was real, and still is.

**Act 2: The Promise (HIGH: audacity)**
- **Expect X:** ₹1 lakh was a carefully planned price. **Get Y:** it was a newspaper headline he could have denied. He chose to keep a promise he never quite made.
- "Most of my colleagues thought I was mad." Suzuki's boss publicly doubted it.
- Young team (25–30). First prototype "an embarrassment". Decision: "never going to be a half-car." He *did* understand dignity in the product itself.

**Act 3: The Reveal (PEAK)**
- 10 Jan 2008: he drives it on stage. "A promise is a promise." Global awe (Time: "one of the most important cars ever designed").
- But even here, a crack: "Indians are becoming the greatest critics."

**Act 4: The Storm (LOW #1: Singur)**
- Farmers, hunger strike, a near-finished plant, "gun to my head" … "pulled the trigger".
- *Fairness note (tribute tone):* the Supreme Court later said the land was acquired illegally. Present farmers' pain and Tata's loss side by side; avoid villains.
- **Reversal:** an SMS ("welcome"), 3,340 truckloads, 14 months. Resilience as a high within the low.
- Same year: JLR purchase and 26/11. A man carrying a lot.

**Act 5: The Rush (FALSE HIGH)**
- Mar 2009 launch, "Now You Can". 2 lakh applications, a lottery, a customs officer drives to Siddhivinayak.
- **Expect X:** this is victory. **Get Y:** hidden in the data, half the buyers already owned a car, and the scooter family wasn't in the queue.

**Act 6: The Slide (LOW #2)**
- Fires and videos of burning Nanos. 509 cars in a month. No TV ad for 21 months. Dealers far from small towns. Prices creep to ₹2 lakh. Second-hand cars with AC cost the same.
- The word that killed it: *cheapest.* **Expect X:** "cheapest" is the best selling point. **Get Y:** it was the poison. Nobody wants the neighbours to see them in "the cheapest car".

**Act 7: The Honesty (EMOTIONAL TURN)**
- 2013: "…I am sorry to say, by ourselves." A founder admitting the mistake publicly. That is the lesson moment.
- 2016: Mistry's letter, "emotional reasons". 2018: one car a month. 2019: one car a year.

**Act 8: The Afterlife (BITTERSWEET HIGH)**
- **Expect X:** the Nano was a waste. **Get Y:** its factory now builds Tata's EVs, crossed 10 lakh cars, and seeded a Gujarat auto hub. Its young project lead now runs Tata Motors' CV business.
- 2022: an 84-year-old rides to the Taj in a Nano, without bodyguards. Instagram: "The Nano, was always meant for all our people."
- 2020: "Looking back, I'm proud of the car."
- Close: *He saw the problem better than anyone. He solved it brilliantly. What he couldn't engineer was how it would feel to be seen in it.*

## G. Problem Iceberg decode (Nano customer)

| Depth | What it was | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **1. What people say** | "A car is too expensive for us." "We need something safe for the family in the rain." | The cheapest car (Maruti 800) cost about ₹1.9–2.2 lakh, double the Nano target (Tribune/AFP). Only about 7 cars per 1,000 Indians (AFP 2008). |
| **2. What people do (workaround)** | Pile 4–5 people onto one scooter, child sandwiched; ride in rain and dark; buy a *used* car once they can stretch. | Tata's own repeated description (Cornell 2011; Q&A; Instagram 2022). 72.5 lakh two-wheelers vs 12 lakh cars in FY08 (SIAM). Experts noted second-hand cars "with air-conditioning" cost the same as a Nano (Murad Ali Baig, via Wheels24/Naharnet, 2011). |
| **3. Why it really happens (root cause)** | Income gap vs car prices; poor mass transit; limited access to credit and formal documents; car-buying culture built around big-city showrooms. | Tata: "mass transport is often not available or is of poor quality" (Q&A). Tata Motors later offered 48-hour loans for people with "unclear documentation" (Wharton). Target buyers were "intimidated" by showrooms (Wharton). |
| **4. What people actually want (hidden desire)** | **Status and dignity:** to be seen as having *arrived*. A car in India is a public signal to neighbours, relatives and marriage prospects. Also **trust/safety** (fires shook that) and **pride** (not charity). | "A car is an aspirational product" (ISB's Sudipt Roy, Wharton 2011). Tata 2015: people did not want to be associated with a cheap car (IANS). Tata 2013: "cheapest car… by ourselves". Seen as a "poor man's car" (AFP 2012; What Car? India via Time/WaPo). Buyers who did come were existing car owners using it as a second car (Wharton): those *without* status anxiety. Even Tata's first ad used a nazar (kohl) dot: pride and envy, not price. |

**The nuance that keeps it a tribute:** Tata *did* see depth 4 in the product. "Nobody wants a car that is less than everybody else's car" (his words, before launch), and Ravi Kant said it "ought not to have looked like a $2,500 car". The engineering protected dignity. The **label** ("₹1 lakh car", "world's cheapest car"), born from a headline he chose to honour, broadcast the opposite signal. The lesson: you can solve depth 4 in the product and still lose it in the story you tell.

## I. Myths and corrections

| Claim | Verdict | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| "Ratan Tata decided on ₹1 lakh as his vision price." | **MYTH / half-true** | He said "about" $2,500 to the FT at Geneva. The headline fixed it; he chose to accept it. He called it "media speculation that we accepted as a challenge" (Jan 2008). |
| "One rainy day in Mumbai he saw a family of four on a scooter and decided to build the Nano." | **DISPUTED** | His own versions differ: Bangalore near-miss on a rainy day (Globe and Mail 2008; Nat Geo 2020); "heavy Bombay rain" (Humans of Bombay 2020); a repeated pattern of families on scooters (Cornell 2011; Instagram 2022). Script it as a pattern plus one near-miss. |
| "The Nano cost ₹1 lakh." | **MYTH (mostly)** | ₹1 lakh was the base model *at the dealer*, excluding VAT and transport. Launch base ex-showroom was ₹1,12,735 (Pantnagar), on-road about ₹1.2 lakh+. Only the first 1 lakh lottery buyers got price protection. By 2017 the base was about ₹2.15 lakh. |
| "He wanted to build a car from day one." | **MYTH** | He started with safer scooters, then a doorless buggy or "rural car" with roll-down plastic curtains. |
| "The Nano was a total failure; nobody bought it." | **EXAGGERATED** | About 2.03 lakh bookings, a peak of 74,527 in a year, about 3 lakh lifetime. It was a commercial failure vs plan (2.5 lakh/yr) and loss-making, but not a car nobody bought. Its plant and supplier base live on. |
| "Mamata Banerjee alone drove Tata out." | **DISPUTED** | Tata blamed her directly (2008). She later blamed the CPI(M) (2022). The SC ruled the Left government's acquisition illegal (2016). Present the multi-party story. |
| "Modi's SMS said 'Suswagatam'." | **DISPUTED (wording)** | Widely reported. Modi's own 2010 account says he sent "welcome". |
| "Ratan Tata drove his Nano to the Taj." | **MYTH (detail)** | In the viral May 2022 video Shantanu Naidu is driving; Tata is the passenger. Tata *did* drive the Nano on stage at the 2008 unveiling. |
| "Ratan Tata was gifted an electric Nano." | **Imprecise** | Electra EV *custom-built and delivered* a one-off 72V Nano EV (Feb 2022). The post called him "our founder"; "gift" is not stated. |
| "Nano was uniquely unsafe: zero stars." | **Misleading out of context** | Zero stars in the Global NCAP 2014 64 km/h test, *alongside* the Alto 800, i10, Figo and Polo (all base, no airbags). It met Indian norms of the day (full frontal crash test claimed in 2008). |
| "Nano fires proved a design defect." | **DISPUTED** | Tata cited a switch fault and foreign material on the exhaust, did inspections (not a recall) and extended the warranty to 4 years. No official defect finding was located. |
| "The Nano used the Tata Ace engine." | **MYTH** (repeated by Autocar Pro 2022) | BusinessWeek 2008 documents a new 540 → 623/624cc 2-cylinder petrol developed in-house from 2005. The Ace used a separate diesel. |
| "Nano production ended in 2018." | **Partly true** | Production effectively stopped mid-2018 (1 unit in June). A few units were built in late 2018. 2019 had zero production and 1 sale. It never got BS-VI (Apr 2020). No formal "last day" announcement was found. |
| "The arbitration settled Singur: Tata got ₹766 crore." | **Incomplete** | The award (Oct 2023) is under challenge. In May 2026 the Calcutta HC allowed only a conditional 8-week stay. Payment status is unconfirmed as of Oct 2026. |
| "Tata Nano was on the cover of Time/Newsweek." | **UNVERIFIED** | No cover found. Time did write it "could well be one of the most important cars ever designed". |
| "Bookings raised ₹2,500 crore." | **UNVERIFIED** | Not found. Autocar reported about $101m in booking value. Deposits ranged from ₹2,999 to about ₹95,000. |
| "Geneva 2003 was in April." | **Minor error in some case studies** | The Geneva Motor Show is held in March. |
