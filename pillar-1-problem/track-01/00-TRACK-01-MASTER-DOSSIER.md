## 1. The core concept: what we are teaching

### 1.1 The teaching spine, in order
1. **Start with the misconception** and say it out loud: *"A problem is what people complain about."* This is Derek Muller's research finding: stating the wrong belief first, then breaking it, roughly doubles learning compared with a clean explanation.
2. **Define a problem:** a **gap between how things are and how someone wants them to be, big enough that they spend time, money or effort on it.** The definition is adapted from Gause & Weinberg, *Are Your Lights On?* ✅
3. **Separate four things people mix up:**
   - **Complaint:** words only. It is a signal, and it is Depth 1.
   - **Inconvenience:** a real gap, but too small to change behaviour.
   - **Problem:** a gap that costs someone something.
   - **Opportunity:** a problem that is painful, frequent, shared by many people, something they will pay to fix, and solvable now.
4. **Why the problem is the base of everything:**
   - CB Insights read 101 founder post-mortems (2014). The No. 1 reason startups died was **"no market need" (42%)** ✅. Its newest analysis of 431 shutdowns finds that **70% ran out of money**, but it calls that the final symptom. The leading root cause is **poor product-market fit (43%)** ✅.
   - Paul Graham: *"By far the most common mistake startups make is to solve problems no one has."* ✅
5. **Teach the Problem Iceberg from a concrete example first** (the school canteen). Only after that, name the abstract model. This is "concreteness fading": concrete first, then the diagram.
6. **The four depths:**

| Depth | English / Hindi label | What it is | Visual |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **SAY**: *Kehte hain* | The visible complaint | Above water, white ice, speech-bubble icon |
| 2 | **DO**: *Karte hain* | The workaround people already use. What they spend money and time on is the strongest evidence. | Just below water, light aqua, a hand holding a tool (jugaad) |
| 3 | **WHY**: *Kyun hota hai* | The root cause: the system, the structure, the clock | Teal to indigo, roots or gear icon |
| 4 | **WANT**: *Kya chahte hain* | The hidden desire. There are 5: **Status 👑 · Trust 🤝 · Dignity (izzat) · Control 🎛 · Time ⏱** | Darkest indigo with a **gold glow**. In our iceberg, the bottom is not danger, it is **treasure**. |

7. **The anchor marker.** This is our signature move and what makes the framework scoreable. In every story, an anchor drops to the depth where the company actually **built its answer**. If it reaches Depth 4, the gold glow lights up.

| Story | Where the anchor stops | Verdict stamp |
|---|---|---|
| Nano | Depth 3 (an affordable, safer family car, engineered brilliantly). The Depth 4 glow (status/izzat) stays **unlit** because the world heard "cheapest". | **SOLVED AT DEPTH 3. LOST AT DEPTH 4.** |
| Airbnb | Depth 4: **trust** between strangers (photos, profiles, reviews, guarantee) | **DECODED: TRUST** |
| Amul | Depth 3 + 4: the **clock** (perishability) and the **chain** (ownership) solved, and **control + izzat** delivered through farmer ownership | **DECODED TO DEPTH 4** |

### 1.2 The Nano nuance (it keeps the story fair and makes it smarter)
Ratan Tata **did** understand Depth 4 in the product itself. Before launch he said: *"This was never going to be a half-car. Nobody wants a car that is less than everybody else's car."* ✅ (his own words, pre-launch Q&A).

What broke Depth 4 was **the story the world heard**. Two words did it:
- **"₹1 lakh"**, a number that began as a newspaper headline he decided to honour.
- **"cheapest"**. In 2013 he said the car *"became termed as a cheapest car by the public and, I am sorry to say, by ourselves."* ✅

Add the buyer data: half the buyers already owned a car, and the real scooter families felt intimidated walking into showrooms. **The lesson is bigger than "he missed status": you can solve Depth 4 in the product and still lose it in the name, the label and the showroom.** That is a lesson a 30-year-old will remember too.

### 1.3 Positioning the iceberg honestly
Show this credit once, in small type: *"The iceberg is an old idea, used in systems thinking and user research. Ours asks a narrower question: how deep does your solution go?"*

The systems-thinking iceberg (events → patterns → structures → mental models) diagnoses *systems*. Ours decodes *a person's problem*, and its bottom layer is an **opportunity**. It builds on:
- the user-research rule that what people say differs from what they do
- Toyota's 5 Whys
- Jobs-to-be-Done

### 1.4 Learning outcomes (for the notes page)
By the end of Track 1, the learner can:
1. **Define** a problem as a costly gap, and correctly sort at least 3 of 4 statements into complaint, inconvenience, problem or opportunity.
2. **Explain** in two sentences, with the CB Insights statistic and one example, why a startup without a real problem fails even when it has money and talent.
3. **Name the 4 depths in order** (SAY → DO → WHY → WANT) and give an everyday example of each.
4. **Apply** the Iceberg to a new situation, one line per depth, and name the hidden desire.
5. **Analyse** Nano, Airbnb or Amul: at which depth did the founders' answer stop, and why?

### 5.6 Legal rules (India): short version
- **Fair dealing** under Section 52(1)(a) of the Copyright Act has **no safe clip length**. Avoid news-channel clips (strike risk; see the ANI vs YouTubers dispute).

- **Never use photoreal AI images of real people** (Ratan Tata, Kurien, the founders). India's 2026 IT Rules require labelling synthetic media, and YouTube requires disclosure. Use reconstructions only as obvious illustration, labelled "Illustration".
- **Logos:** show them to identify, never distort them or imply endorsement. Add the description line "All trademarks belong to their owners; no affiliation implied." Don't animate the Amul girl.
- **Send permission emails on day 1** (replies take weeks):
  - Tata Motors corporate comms (press images; the 2022 Instagram photo via Tata Sons)
  - GCMMF/Amul and Film Heritage Foundation (*Manthan* clip and stills, Amul archive)

## 6. Voice and delivery: how to speak this video

### 6.1 Five principles
1. **Talk to one person.** Tape a photo of one real 17-year-old (a cousin or student) beside the lens. Say "aap", never "dosto, sab log". Use "aap" consistently for Track 1: warm and respectful, and right for parents watching too.
2. **Pace is a dial, not a setting.**
   - Explaining: **150–170 wpm**
   - Tension montage: **170–190 wpm**, in bursts
   - Reveals and sad beats: **110–130 wpm**
   - Target gap between your explaining pace and your sad pace: **at least 40 wpm**
3. **Silence is punctuation.**
   - 0.5 s is a comma.
   - 1–1.5 s lets a fact land.
   - **2–3 s of total silence (music out) marks a turning point. Use it only 3–4 times in the whole video:**
     - after the 0:08 "one car" card
     - before "RIGHT PROBLEM. WRONG DEPTH."
     - before "Paanch lakh kisaan"
     - optionally before the final lesson line
4. **Land the last word.** End statements with a **falling pitch**; kill uptalk. One stressed word per sentence. Don't let volume fade at the end, where the key word usually sits.
5. **Hindi carries emotion, English carries the framework.** *Izzat, bharosa, sapna, apna, jugaad* in Hindi; *problem, root cause, depth, customer* in English. Set up in one language and **punch in the other**.

### 6.2 Delivery by moment (use it while marking your script)

| Moment | Pace | Pitch | Volume | Pauses | Face | Music |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Reveal** ("Right problem. Wrong depth.") | Decelerate to ~110 | Lower-middle, falling | Drop slightly, land the key word at normal level | 1.5–2 s before, 1–2 s after | Still, eyes on the lens, slight lean in, no smile | Silence or one low drone |
| **Sad / loss** (Nano's end, farmers' plight) | 110–130 | Low and steady, no sing-song | Soft, close to the mic | Long, after facts | Soft eyes; never perform tears | Solo sarangi or cello, or nothing |

| **Explaining** (the depths) | 150–170 | Natural variation | Normal | Micro, between chunks | Your hand "lowers" level by level | Light bed, ducked 10–15 dB |

### 6.3 Marking your teleprompter script (Kerry Barrett's system)
- `...` = slow down
- **bold** = the one stress word
- `x` = 1-second pause; `xx` = 2-second pause
- `/` = breath point (every 1–2 lines; a deep breath *before* each emotional line)
- `( )` = keep the phrase together
- One sentence per line. Write names and Hindi words phonetically.
- **Read one line ahead of your voice.** This "eye-lead" takes 3–4 sessions to become automatic.
- **Talk it, then read it.** Explain each section to your phone without the script first, then copy your natural rhythm into the script.
- **Hybrid mode for the two emotional beats** (the Nano tribute, the Amul climax): put bullet points on the prompter instead of full text, and keep whichever take sounds more alive.

### 6.6 Shooting setup
- Medium close-up (chest up) for explaining. **Punch in to a tight close-up for sad and reveal lines.** Keep your hands in frame for the depth gesture.
- Lav or shotgun mic 20–30 cm from the mouth. Record **30 s of room tone** at every setup.
- Shoot **paragraph by paragraph**, 2–3 takes each, a break every 20 minutes. A B-camera at 45° (or a 110–120% punch-in) hides cuts.
- **Common mistakes to avoid:**
  - going flatter on the prompter
  - shouting the triumph
  - nervous smiling through serious lines
  - fillers ("toh basically", "matlab")
  - stressing every word
  - opening with "hey guys, welcome back"

### 10.3 Quiz ("Check yourself")
**Q1. The hostel mess.** Students say "mess food is terrible", yet half of them pay extra for noodles or delivery at dinner. Which statement is **Depth 2**?
→ **Half of them pay extra for delivery.** This is behaviour that costs money, the strongest evidence a problem is real.

**Q2. Riya's short cable.** She has complained for a year but never bought the ₹150 longer one.
→ **An inconvenience.** There is a gap, but no workaround and no willingness to pay.

**Q3. The cheap tuition app.** A founder launches the cheapest online tuition app. Parents don't sign up, because they proudly tell relatives which famous coaching centre their child attends.
→ **Depth 4: status (and trust)** was missed. This rhymes with the Nano lesson.
