# Startup Blueprint — The Founder Curriculum (working name)

> **One line:** A structured, India-first platform that takes anyone from "I want to start something" to **their first rupee of revenue**, using 7 pillars of deep video tracks, notes and real deliverables, plus free tools (grants, books, founder stories) and weekend challenges.

This document is the brainstorm, written down. I've gone through every part of your idea, kept what's strong, pushed back where I think it'll hurt you, and turned it into decisions you can act on.

---

## 0. TL;DR: the 12 decisions that matter most

> ⚠️ **The market signal that shapes everything below:** in May 2026 Ankur Warikoo reportedly shut down WebVeda, a profitable ₹100 Cr+ course business, and said AI's impact was "huge". Generic "information" videos are being commoditised: anyone can ask Claude how to find a startup idea. **Our moat has to be outcomes, India-specific operating depth, story quality, community and your face. Not information.** Details in [05-grants-and-competitors.md](05-grants-and-competitors.md).

1. **The product isn't the videos. It's the outcome:** every learner leaves with a **Founder Dossier** (problem brief → validated solution → market report → live website with payments → brand kit → first ad campaign → first sale). Sell *"From problem to first ₹"*, not "350 videos".
2. **Fix the pillar order and ownership:** Problem → Solution → Market Research → **Build with AI** → **Branding** → **Marketing** → **Sales**. Branding comes before marketing, Meta ads belong to Marketing only, and Sales owns channels (D2C/B2B/B2G/offline). That removes duplicate tracks.
3. **Add an 8th pillar later: Money & Operations** (unit economics, pricing, company registration, GST, compliance, cash flow, fundraising, hiring). Your 7 pillars produce someone who can sell but can still die of a GST notice or negative margins.
4. **Don't build 365 videos before launch.** Launch with **Pillar 1 (Problem) complete + the first module of every other pillar free**. Fanout does this: every course has a free first part. Publish new tracks weekly after that.
5. **Pre-sell before you produce.** A "Founding Member" lifetime plan (₹3,999–₹4,999) sold from a waitlist funds production and proves demand. That's your own Pillar 1 and 2, applied to yourself.
6. **Build this startup in public, using your own framework.** Every week you post what you did on *this* company: problem interviews, pricing tests, ad results. That's the cheapest, most believable marketing you'll ever have.
7. **Beachhead audience: 18–28 (college students + early-job "I want out" professionals).** They pay for themselves, they live on Instagram/YouTube, and they convert from content. Schools (13–17) are **phase 2, sold B2B**. Different buyer, slower cycle.
8. **Video length: 12–20 min chaptered, not 20–30 min.** Long videos hurt completion. Each track = video + notes + one "do this now" task. Keep the immersive style (you on camera, motion graphics, B-roll), just make it tighter.
9. **Split AI content into evergreen and disposable.** Principles get the full production; tool walkthroughs (Claude, ChatGPT, Razorpay setup…) are cheap screen recordings you re-shoot every 3–6 months. Otherwise 80–90 polished AI videos go stale within a year.
10. **Weekend challenges: make them free for members, with sponsored prizes. Not ₹100 paid entry.** Paid-entry contests with cash prizes are legally risky in India, especially after the 2025 online money-gaming law. Get a lawyer's opinion before you take any entry fee. As a membership perk, challenges also become your best retention feature.
11. **Grants directory, books and founder stories are free and public.** They're your SEO and social engine, not paid features.
12. **Distribution order:** founder-led short-form content → free tools for SEO → college E-Cell partnerships → paid Meta ads (only once organic shows conversion) → schools B2B.

**Positioning statement:**

Unlike scattered YouTube content or single-skill courses, you finish with a working business, not a certificate.

## 2. Who it's for: two personas, two motions

You said a 17-year-old and a 30-year-old should both get a "wow". That's right for the **content**. But they're different **customers**, so they need different selling motions.

### Persona A: "Arjun", 20, BTech/BBA 3rd year, Tier-1/Tier-2 city *(beachhead)*
- Watches Think School, Warikoo, Shark Tank India clips. Has 3 half-ideas in Notes.
- Pays for himself (₹300–₹500/month is OK; ₹3,000/year if convinced). Pays via UPI.
- Wants a startup, a side income, a strong CV/college application, bragging rights.
- Reached by: Instagram reels, YouTube, college E-Cells, fests, WhatsApp groups.

### Persona B: "Neha", 29, 6 years into a corporate job, frustrated *(beachhead, higher value)*
- Has savings, has a domain (pharma sales, ops, IT), fears quitting blind.
- Will pay ₹5,000–₹15,000 for something that *de-risks* the jump.
- Wants a step-by-step plan, a community of peers, real examples in her industry.
- Reached by: LinkedIn, YouTube long-form, Meta ads, podcasts, newsletters.

### Persona C: "Riya", 16, Class 11 *(phase 2, B2B via schools)*
- Doesn't pay. Parents and schools do.
- Needs: shorter tracks, more games and challenges, parent-friendly outcomes (school projects, competitions, college portfolio).
- Reached by: school partnerships, the CBSE Entrepreneurship elective, Atal Tinkering Labs, Olympiad-style competitions.

**Decision:** Build the core product for A and B. Create a "Young Founders" edition for C in month 6+ (the same tracks re-cut shorter, plus a teacher dashboard).

The full module-and-track map is in **[02-curriculum-map.md](02-curriculum-map.md)**.

### 3.3 Anatomy of one track

Every track is the same unit, so the experience is predictable and production is repeatable:

1. **Video (12–20 min, chaptered).** You on camera, motion graphics, B-roll, one deep real-company story.
2. **Notes (5–10 min read).** The framework, diagrams, key terms, sources. Searchable, skimmable. This is fanout's strength: text people come back to.
3. **"Do this now" task (15–60 min).** One concrete action that feeds the module deliverable.
4. **Check (2 min).** 3–5 scenario questions ("You're Zomato in 2008. Which of these is the real problem?"), not trivia.
5. **Story card.** The company used in the track, linked to its full page in Real Stories.
6. **Tools & templates.** Interview script, TAM calculator, negotiation checklist, Notion/Sheets templates.

### 3.4 What makes this a "wow" and not just another course

- **Depth of stories.** Not "Steve Jobs was a visionary". Instead: what went wrong, what it cost, what they'd do differently. The Tata Nano: right problem, wrong positioning. Juicero: $120M raised for a problem that didn't exist. Captain Krishnan Nair founded The Leela in his sixties. Rolex: Hans Wilsdorf turned a Channel swim into the most famous waterproofing demo ever.
- **Indian operating reality.** IndiaMART and trade-fair sourcing, MOQ negotiation, GST on D2C, Shiprocket, COD vs prepaid, GeM tenders for B2G, Meta ads for Tier-2 audiences, regional-language marketing.
- **Boring industries are welcome.** Chemicals, agriculture, packaging, manufacturing, logistics. That's where many Indian fortunes are made and nobody teaches it.
- **Build while you learn.** By the end of the program, the learner *has a business*, not notes.
- **An AI co-founder coach, built in.** Learners submit each deliverable (problem brief, interview notes, ad copy) and an AI coach trained on *your* rubrics critiques it within seconds: "Your problem statement describes a solution, not a pain. Rewrite it." Human mentors review the best ones. That turns AI from the thing that kills course businesses into the reason yours is better.
- **You.** One consistent, trusted face and voice across everything. That's the brand (Think School and Warikoo prove Indians follow people, not platforms).

## 6. Weekend Challenges: the format, and the legal catch

**Your idea:** ₹100 entry, branding/commerce challenges, cash prizes for the top 3, help winners start up.

**The catch:** in India, paid entry plus a cash prize can be treated as gaming or a lottery. The **Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025** bans "online money games" (pay to enter, expecting to win money), and on paper it doesn't distinguish skill from chance. A judged business-plan contest probably isn't a "game", but you don't want to be the test case. **Get a written opinion from a lawyer before charging any entry fee.**

**Recommended format (safer and better for the business):**
- **Free for paid members.** That makes it a reason to subscribe and stay, so it isn't a ₹100 micro-transaction.
- **Prizes funded by sponsors** (fintechs, D2C brands, incubators, banks). The sponsor sets the brief: "Rebrand our chai brand for Gen Z." They get ideas and hiring leads; you get prize money and revenue.
- **Judged on published rubrics** by named judges (skill only, transparent).
- **Prizes:** cash plus non-cash (mentor hours, incubator fast-track, AWS/Google credits, feature on your channel).
- **Weekly rhythm:** brief drops Friday 6pm → submissions close Sunday 9pm → winners announced in a Monday live → the best entries become content.
- **Formats:** 48-hour brand sprint, "find 10 suppliers and the best quote", "write the ad that wins", "pitch in 60 seconds", "sell ₹1,000 by Sunday", "decode this company's failure".

## 7. Content production: the engine

A full playbook (pipeline, script template, a sample script, team, costs) is in **[03-track-production-playbook.md](03-track-production-playbook.md)**. Key points:

- **Volume reality check:** 365 tracks × ~15 min ≈ **90 hours of finished, motion-graphics video**. With a lean team producing 4–5 tracks a week, that's **18 months**. Plan for it. Don't wait for it.
- **Batching:** research and script 8 tracks → film all 8 in one studio day → edit over 2 weeks.
- **Repurpose everything:** 1 track → 1 YouTube cut + 5–8 reels + 1 LinkedIn carousel + 1 newsletter section.
- **AI speeds things up, but humans verify:** Claude/ChatGPT for research drafts, outlines and B-roll lists. *Every fact checked against a primary source*: one wrong claim about a famous founder costs you credibility, and possibly a legal notice.
- **Fact standard:** every story claim carries a source in the notes.

## 10. Risks, and how to handle them

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| AI makes course content a commodity (see WebVeda) | Learners ask Claude instead of paying for videos | Sell outcomes (dossier, first sale), live challenges, community, verified India-specific depth; build an AI "co-founder coach" into the product that reviews deliverables, so AI works for you, not against you |
| Building too much before launch | 18 months of production with no revenue kills you | Launch with Pillar 1 + free samplers; presell |
| "Just another course" perception | Indian edtech has a trust problem after the 2022–24 crash | Outcome-led positioning (Founder Dossier, first ₹); show learner results publicly |
| AI content goes stale | Tools change quarterly | Separate evergreen vs. disposable tracks; refresh calendar |
| Piracy | Indian course content leaks to Telegram within days | DRM video (VdoCipher), watermarking with user ID, value in community and challenges that can't be pirated |
| Factual errors in founder stories | Credibility and defamation risk | Source every claim; avoid claims about living people you can't cite; legal review of sensitive stories |
| Founder-dependency (only your face) | Can't scale production | Bring in guest experts per pillar; build the brand around the *method* too |
| Paid challenge legality | Online money gaming law | Free for members + sponsored prizes; lawyer opinion |
| Low completion | Self-paced courses often finish in single digits | Deliverables, streaks, weekly challenges, cohort option, WhatsApp nudges |
| Image/footage rights | Using founders' photos, brand logos, news footage | Licensed stock (Storyblocks, Envato), fair-use commentary practices, illustrated motion graphics instead of copyrighted photos |

## 11. Metrics to watch

- **North Star:** number of learners who complete a module deliverable each week.
- **Funnel:** visitor → free signup (target 5–10%) → first track completed (60%) → paid (2–5%).
- **Retention:** % of paid users active in week 4 (target 40%+).
- **Outcomes:** dossiers published, learners with a live website, learners with a first sale. **Market these numbers.**
- **Unit economics:** CAC vs. first-year revenue per paid user (target CAC < ₹1,000 on a ₹2,999 plan).
