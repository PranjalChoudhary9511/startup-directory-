# Track Production Playbook

How to turn one track title from the curriculum map into a finished, immersive track (video + notes + task), the same way every time.

---

## 1. The pipeline (one track, roughly 10–14 working days end to end)

| # | Stage | Owner | Output | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Research** | Researcher | Research doc: core concept, 1 primary story, 2–3 supporting examples, every fact with a source link | 1–2 days |
| 2 | **Outline** | You + researcher | 1-page beat sheet using the script template below | 2 hrs |
| 3 | **Script** | Scriptwriter, then you rewrite in your own voice | Two-column script (what you say / what's on screen) | 1 day |
| 4 | **Fact-check** | Second person | Every claim ticked against its source; risky claims cut | 2 hrs |
| 5 | **Film (batched)** | You + camera operator | A-roll for 6–8 tracks in one studio day | 1 day per 6–8 tracks |
| 6 | **Rough cut** | Editor | Talking head cut, pacing tight, chapters marked | 1–2 days |
| 7 | **Motion graphics + B-roll** | Motion designer + editor | Frameworks animated, story visualised, stock B-roll | 2–4 days |
| 8 | **Sound + captions** | Editor | Music, SFX, mix, English captions (later Hindi) | 0.5 day |
| 9 | **Notes + task + quiz** | Researcher | Notes page (MDX), "do this now" task, 3–5 scenario questions, templates | 1 day |
| 10 | **QA + publish** | You | Watch end to end, approve, upload, schedule | 2 hrs |
| 11 | **Repurpose** | Social manager | 5–8 reels, 1 carousel, 1 newsletter block | 1 day |

**Batching rhythm (lean team):** script 8 tracks in week 1 → film them all on one day in week 2 → edit and publish over weeks 2–4, while scripting the next 8. Steady state: **4–6 tracks a week.**

---

## 2. The script template: every 🎬 track follows this

Target **12–20 minutes**, ~140 words per minute → **1,700–2,800 words.**

| Beat | Time | Purpose | Rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Cold open hook** | 0:00–0:45 | Make them need the answer | Start inside a story at its most tense moment. No "Hi guys, welcome back". |
| **2. The promise** | 0:45–1:30 | What they'll be able to do by the end | One sentence. Concrete. |
| **3. Why this matters to you** | 1:30–3:00 | Connect to *their* life | Speak to both Arjun (student) and Neha (job-leaver). |
| **4. The core idea** | 3:00–7:00 | Teach the concept / framework | One framework, animated. Name it. Make it memorable. |
| **5. The deep story** | 7:00–12:00 | Show the idea in a real company | Go beyond the obvious: the mistake, the cost, the turning point. |
| **6. Indian worked example** | 12:00–15:00 | Apply it to an everyday Indian business | A chai stall, a D2C brand, a chemical trader. Do the maths on screen. |
| **7. Common mistakes** | 15:00–16:30 | Save them pain | 3 mistakes, each with a one-line fix. |
| **8. Your task** | 16:30–17:30 | Turn watching into doing | Exactly what to do in the next 24 hours, linked to the module deliverable. |
| **9. Recap + bridge** | 17:30–18:00 | Lock it in, and pull them to the next track | 3 bullets + an open loop: "Next, we'll find out why…" |

**On-screen language:** pattern interrupts every 30–60 seconds (cut, zoom, graphic, B-roll, question on screen). Framework graphics stay consistent across the whole platform: same colours, same icons per pillar.

## 3. Sample script: P1.01.01, "Problems vs ideas: why founders fall in love with the wrong thing"

*Format: SAY = your voice; SCREEN = visuals. The facts below are as commonly reported. **Re-verify each one against a primary source before filming.***

---

**[1. COLD OPEN, 0:00]**

SCREEN: Slow push-in on a sleek white machine on a kitchen counter. Text: *San Francisco, 2017.*

SAY:
> This machine raised about 120 million dollars from some of the smartest investors in Silicon Valley. It was Wi-Fi connected, it scanned QR codes, and it squeezed juice from a pack with several tonnes of force.
>
> Then two reporters did something nobody at the company seemed to have tried. They took the juice pack… and squeezed it with their bare hands.

SCREEN: Hands squeezing a pouch; juice pours out. Freeze frame. Text: *Same juice. No machine.*

SAY:
> Within months, the company shut down. Its name was Juicero. And it didn't die because of bad engineering. It died because it solved a problem nobody had.

---

**[2. THE PROMISE, 0:45]**

SAY:
> By the end of this track, you'll be able to tell the difference between an *idea* and a *problem*, and you'll have a simple test to run on every startup idea you ever have. Including the one sitting in your Notes app right now.

---

**[3. WHY THIS MATTERS, 1:30]**

SCREEN: Split screen, a college hostel room and an office cubicle.

SAY:
> If you're 19, you probably have three startup ideas. If you're 29 and stuck in a job, you might have one big idea you think about every night. Here's the uncomfortable truth: most of those ideas start from the wrong end. They start with "wouldn't it be cool if…" instead of "people are suffering because…".
>
> Founders don't usually fail because they can't build. They fail because they build something nobody desperately needed.

---

**[4. THE CORE IDEA, 3:00]**

SCREEN: Animated framework, two columns: **IDEA → looks for a problem** vs **PROBLEM → pulls a solution.**

SAY:
> An idea is a solution looking for someone to care. A problem is a pain that already exists, whether or not you ever show up.
>
> Here's the test. I call it the **Three-P Check**:

SCREEN: Three cards flip in.

> **People:** can you name *specific* people who have this problem? Not "everyone". Names.
> **Pain:** what are they doing *today* to cope? If the answer is "nothing", it may not hurt enough.
> **Pay:** are they already spending time or money on a bad workaround?
>
> If an idea fails the Three-P Check, it isn't a startup yet. It's a hobby.

---

**[5. THE DEEP STORY, 7:00]**

SCREEN: Archival-style illustration (licensed or drawn, not copyrighted photos). Text: *Delhi NCR, 2008.*

SAY:
> Now let me show you the opposite. In 2008, a young consultant at Bain & Company's India office kept noticing the same thing at lunchtime: colleagues crowding around, trying to find menus for the restaurants nearby.
>
> His name was Deepinder Goyal. He didn't start with "let's build a food-tech giant". He started with a small, annoying, *daily* problem: people want to know what's on the menu before they order.

SCREEN: Animated menu cards being scanned and uploaded.

SAY:
> So he and his co-founder did something unglamorous. They collected menu cards and put them online. The site was called Foodiebay. People used it, because the pain was real and it happened every single day.
>
> Run the Three-P Check. People? Office workers around him, by name. Pain? Hunting for menus daily. Pay? They were already spending time, every lunch, on a bad workaround.
>
> Foodiebay became Zomato, and Zomato went public in 2021. The billion-dollar company came *after* the problem, not before it.

SCREEN: Side-by-side cards. *Juicero: started with a machine. Zomato: started with a menu card.*

SAY:
> Juicero started with a machine and went looking for a problem. Zomato started with a problem and let the solution grow out of it. Same Silicon Valley money in one case, almost no money in the other. Guess which one survived.

---

**[6. INDIAN WORKED EXAMPLE, 12:00]**

SCREEN: A tiffin service in Pune, illustrated.

SAY:
> Let's try it on an everyday business. Say your idea is "an app for ordering home-cooked tiffins". That's an idea. Let's turn it into a problem.
>
> Who exactly? Maybe: *first-year IT employees in Hinjewadi who moved from another state, living in PGs.*
> Pain? They eat outside, spend a lot, and miss home food. Some get sick.
> Pay? They're *already* paying aunties via WhatsApp, with irregular quality and no backup when the aunty is away.
>
> See what happened? The problem is now specific, painful and already being paid for. *That's* something you can build on. And notice: you might not even need an app. A WhatsApp group with three reliable cooks might be your first version.

SCREEN: Quick on-screen maths: 40 customers × ₹3,000/month = ₹1.2 lakh/month.

---

**[7. COMMON MISTAKES, 15:00]**

SCREEN: Three red cards.

SAY:
> Mistake one: **"Everyone has this problem."** If it's everyone, it's no one. Fix: name ten real people.
> Mistake two: **asking friends if they like your idea.** They'll lie to be nice. Fix: ask what they did the *last time* they faced the problem.
> Mistake three: **falling in love with the technology.** Juicero's press was a beautiful machine. Fix: describe the problem without mentioning your product.

---

**[8. YOUR TASK, 16:30]**

SCREEN: Task card with a checkbox UI.

SAY:
> Here's your task for the next 24 hours. Open your notes and write down every startup idea you've ever had. Then rewrite each one as a problem: *who* is suffering, *what* they do today, and *what* it costs them. Run the Three-P Check on each one. Keep the ones that pass.
>
> This list is the first page of your Founder Dossier.

---

**[9. RECAP + BRIDGE, 17:30]**

SAY:
> Ideas look for problems. Problems pull solutions. Run the Three-P Check: People, Pain, Pay.
>
> But here's the thing. Even a real problem can be the *wrong* problem. In the next track, I'll show you how Ratan Tata saw one of the most real problems in India, a family of four on one scooter in the rain, and still built a car that couldn't win. See you there.

## 5. Quality bar: the checklist before anything publishes

- [ ] The hook starts inside a story within the first 10 seconds.
- [ ] One framework, named and animated.
- [ ] One deep story with at least one *non-obvious* detail (a mistake, a cost, a turning point).
- [ ] One Indian worked example with real numbers.
- [ ] A task that produces something for the Dossier.
- [ ] Every fact has a source in the notes.
- [ ] No copyrighted photos or footage without a licence. Use illustrations or licensed stock.
- [ ] Captions on. Audio levels checked. Chapters marked.
- [ ] Would a 17-year-old understand it? Would a 30-year-old learn something new?

## 7. Repurposing map: one track becomes many pieces

| From one track | Becomes |
|---|---|
| Cold open story | 1 reel (45–60s) |
| The framework | 1 carousel (Instagram/LinkedIn) + 1 reel |
| The deep story | 1 reel + 1 YouTube Short + a Real Stories web page section |
| Mistakes | 1 reel ("3 mistakes every first-time founder makes") |
| The task | 1 WhatsApp-community prompt + a challenge seed |
| Whole track | Newsletter section + a free YouTube cut (for free modules) |
